Swami Vivekananda

July 14th, after crossing the Red Sea, the ship

[reached] the Suez Gate.

Ahead was the Suez Canal. There was great

fear of disembarking at Suez from the ship.

This is the place for contamination. On top of that, there's a plague in Egypt, and

we are bringing our native

people [who] probably [have] the plague __ so where does this double-edged contamination touch?

Goods can't be

landed. The

poor sailors on the ship are

[dumping] the bundles into Suez boats below _ they are taking [them] to the shore.

Mercy! They are working! The company's

agent has come to the ship in a small launch,

[but] there is no order to board. The Captain is conversing with a

"white man" __ someone above the plague law __ by boat here in Europe. This is not India.

Plague as the mode of transportation.

It gets to heaven straight

[up], that's why there is so much

detention. But our fate [is sealed]!

Plague-poison appears within ten days of entry; so the tenth day has passed _ the danger has passed. But touching an Egyptian man means

[another] ten days of

detention _ then

no one will be allowed to disembark at Naples

or Marseille; so whatever work is being done, all

[of it] will take all day. The ship can easily cross the

canal at night,

stay __ at Suez Port.

There is lightning

before [us]. Well _ ten days of quarantine

[is enough]. But that light

[is useless to us]. Therefore, it is a very beautiful natural port at night, almost surrounded on three sides

[by hills] _ the water is also very deep. In the water there are countless numbers of

and sharks floating around. There is no other place like this in the world.

On top of that, the morning

brought news that animals too have caste prejudices;

[some] even eat people if they get them alone. Who will go into the water? Snakes and

[people] don't let them go if they get them alone. There are as many sharks in Australia's Sydney port,

[and] even more sharks

are swimming around behind Suez. Seeing the water,

the ship was only there for a short time,

and that too, close to the city.

Sharks had never been seen before __ the last time, on hearing the news

about the sharks, we

arrived quickly in Suez.

The second class were leaning over the ship's porch __ watching the sharks from that porch.

When we arrived, then

men, women and children were standing in rows along the veranda,

watching. The shark-gentlemen had moved away a little; [I] was very disappointed. But I saw that a kind of gangadhar-like fish was teeming in the water.

From time to time a big fish was floating in flocks. And another kind of very small

fish

are darting here and there. [I] thought, perhaps these are baby sharks. But on inquiring,

[I] learnt __ no, they are

called Bonito. [I] had read about them before

indeed; and

[I] had read about them also being imported in dried form from the Malacca Islands, piled on carts. Its flesh

became [clear]. Seeing every gesture of that big fish inside the water, [I] was very pleased by its power and speed.

[It] is said that

the water is very tasty too.

For twenty minutes, half an hour or so, this kind of bonito darting and the small fish darting about could be seen. After half an hour,

someone said __ there, there! Ten or twelve people said

__ "It's coming, it's coming!" [I] looked and saw a huge black object floating in the distance,

five to seven inches below the water.

Gradually the object began to move closer

and a huge flat

head appeared; [it] was moving slowly,

without a care; there was no agitation in it; however, once in a while the head would turn

and a few small fish [would] go ahead; and

a few small fish were playing on its back, sides and belly. Some

were even sitting firmly on its neck. This

was the shark with attendants. The fish which show the shark its prey and probably get the spoils of

war, are

called "pilot fish."

However, it is doubtful

whether the shark's gaping jaws

[are] more successful. The fish that are circling around it, sitting on its back, are shark-"suckers."

Like many English rubber shoes, there is a four-inch-long and two-inch-wide flat area near the chests of the shark-suckers,

cut

with slits.

The fish grips that area onto the shark's skin; that's why it seems to be moving around on the shark's body. It is said that they

eat the lice and ticks on the shark's body. The shark does not move without being surrounded by these two types of fish. And

knowing them to be their helpers, the sharks do not say anything to them. One of these fish was caught in a small

thread. Its chest

had a little pressure from the bottom of the shoe, and as soon as

[I] picked it up, it stuck to

[my] foot; it started to rise with [my foot].

The people in the second class

were very enthusiastic. Among them there was a military man __

he had no limit to his enthusiasm. Finding a huge hook from somewhere on the ship, he strongly wrapped and tied a thick rope,

then put about a seer of meat and

a head of something called "Balaram's grandfather"

on it as bait. Then the hook, bait and

a thick rope were

dropped into the water with a splash. A police boat under the ship was

causing a lot of trouble.

Since then they have become great

friends of the passengers. From the boat, two policemen were sleeping soundly, and the boat was

stationed as a guard to prevent any kind of contact with the shore. On waking with a start, the Arab gentlemen rubbed his eyes and

stood

[up]. Realizing that there was some commotion, he tightened his waist

[and] pushed the float further away with the end of a stick, since the clamorous shouting was only for a request to move the float away.

He breathed a sigh of relief

[and] gave a wide smile on the end of the stick,

while we were eagerly

preparing for the shark.

"With anxious eyes,

[one] watches for thy path" -

that's what a human does when they are excited; it was the same as it always is __ meaning, "O friend, Shyam has arrived,

we remained."

But

every sorrow has an end. Then suddenly about ten feet from the ship, something huge, like a water carrier's leather bag, floated up; at the same time, there was a shout, "There's the shark, there's the shark!" "Quiet, quiet __ children! The shark will run away," "Hey! Why don't you take off your white hats, the shark will get startled" __ while such sounds were entering the ear, the shark, born of the salty sea, came soaring forward like a boat under full sail to incinerate the attached piece of pork in its stomach-fire. Five more feet _ now the shark's mouth has touched the bait. That fearsome tail tilted a little _ the straight motion turned into a circle. Oh, the shark has gone away! Again the tail twisted a little, and that huge body turned, staring at the hook. Again it's coming soaring _ it's opening its mouth and holding the hook! Again that sinful tail moved, and the shark circled far away. Again it's coming in that circle, again it's opening its mouth; there - it's taken the bait, this time _ it's lying on its back; it's done, it's eaten the bait _ pull, pull, pull, forty or fifty people pulling, pulling with all their might. What strength the fish has! What flapping _ what a gasp! Pull, pull. It's coming out of the water, there it is whirling in the water, again it's lying on its back, pull, pull. Oh, the bait has come loose! What's the hurry, folks! You didn't give [it] time to eat the bait! As soon as [it] lay on its back, did you have to start pulling? Now - there's no lamenting the past, the shark has broken free of the hook and run away at full speed. Whether it gave the pilot fish a good lesson, [I] didn't get any news; the shark [fled]. And that was a "tiger" __ black stripes like a tiger. Anyway, the "tiger" disappeared with its "pilot" and "bloodsucker" to abandon the hook.

But there's no need to be too disappointed _ that's another huge "flat-faced" one coming up alongside the fleeing "tiger"! Ah, sharks don't have a language, otherwise "tiger" would surely have warned him about his stomach. Surely [it] would have said, "Look, be careful, there's a new animal over there. Its meat is very delicious and fragrant, but what strong bones! [I] have been a shark all this time, [I] have eaten many kinds of animals, alive, dead, half-dead _ [I] have stuffed many kinds of bones, bricks, stones, pieces of wood into [my] stomach, but all those are like butter compared to these bones __ butter! Look at the condition of my teeth, the condition of [my] jaw" _ [it] would surely have shown the approaching shark its wide-open mouth.

And [the approaching shark] would have given some advice on using some marine medicine from the sea, like a pitta from a chang fish, a spleen from a humpback, a cold broth of oysters, with the experience appropriate to [its] old age. But when all that didn't happen, then either the sharks don't have a language, or [they] have a language, but talking in water is not possible! Therefore, until some kind of shark letters are invented, how can that language be used? __ Or "tiger" has become used to humans by living near them, so without saying anything to "flatface" about the real news, [it] just smiled and said, "Are you well?" _- "Am I the only one to be deceived?"

"Brahmin goes ahead playing the conch, and Ganga follows behind...". But the sound of the conch is not heard; but "pilot fish" are going ahead, and "flatface" is coming behind, shaking [its] huge body, and "shark-sucker" fish are dancing around [it]. Ah, how can that greed be resisted? Oil is floating ten feet above the sea, shimmering, and "flatface" alone can say how far the fragrance has spread. And what a sight _ white,

red, yellow _ all in one place! Real English pork, tied around a black huge hook,

is swinging in the water like Krishna among a colorful circle of gopis!

Now everyone is silent - don't move, don't stir, and stay close. [It]'s circling closer and closer to the hook; [it]'s testing the bait

by touching it with [its] mouth! Let it. Quiet, quiet _

now it's lying on its back - it's swallowing it diagonally; quiet _

let it swallow. Then

at its leisure, "flatface" will swallow the bait diagonally, and just as it will go away, the pull will fall

on top of it! The opposite of the origin! The hook has pierced it, and

that shark's head has risen from the water __ pull, pull, folks,

pull! What a mouth! It's all mouth __

the hook has pierced [its] lip! Tie the rope _ otherwise [it]'ll get loose!
Stop, stop __ O Arab police-boatmen,

don't pull up a big animal like that. Be careful brother, that [lash] can break a horse's leg. Again [the rope] is getting loose. Oh my, what's that? That's right, that's the shark's

son! The amazed "flatface" shook its head and looked at it,

as children and adults, young and old, pulled on the rope.

The half-shark-

is hanging below its stomach. It's like a potbelly! It can't bear its own weight.

Cut that off, let it fall into the water, lighten the load; pull, folks, pull.

Oh, that's a fountain of semen! And no more sentimentality about clothes. Pull __ it's coming. Now drop it onto the ship; be careful, brothers,

be very careful, strike it on the head without letting it come at you.

Hey military man, you are a sepoy, this is your task. Really? In a moment the military passenger's

clothes became smeared with blood, and [he] began to strike the shark on the head with a wooden beam _ shouting, "Strike, strike," yet not stopping to watch

how a river of blood flowed when the shark's belly was cut open, how

[its] intestines spread out. Women and girls said, "Ah, how

cruel! Don't kill [it]," and so on.

Let that ugly story end here.

How long that dismembered shark continued to tremble, to move; how

bones, skin,

meat, pieces of wood came out of its belly _ all that is

now old news.

After that, all the things seemed to smell of the shark.

The Suez Canal is a strange example of engineering. Ferdinand de Lesseps, a famous French architect, dug this canal. The connection of the Mediterranean Sea and the Red Sea has greatly facilitated trade between Europe

and India. Of all the countries benefiting from trade since the beginning of time, perhaps India's trade is the most important.

What other country is there like India? All the cotton fabrics, cotton, the

current state of cotton development for all the countries, shellac, rice, diamonds, pearls,

etc. which were used until 100 years ago,

all came from India. Besides, the best silk and

brocade etc. was not made anywhere else like in this country. Again, India is the place for spices _

cloves, cardamom, pepper, nutmeg, mace, etc. Therefore, since ancient times, whichever country became civilized, then that country depended on India for

things. That trade flowed in two main streams: one overland via Afghanistan and Iran, and the other through the sea via the Red Sea. After Sikandar's victory over Iran,

he sent a general named Nearchus

by sea through the mouth of the Indus River across the sea,

to scout out the road via the Red Sea.

Babylon, Iran, Greece, Rome,

etc. Many people don't know how much the wealth of these ancient countries depended on India's trade.

After the destruction of Rome, Muslim Baghdad and Italian Venice and Genoa became the main Western centers of Indian trade. When the Turks occupied the Roman Empire and closed the road for Italian trade with India, then

Columbus (01150101010 00109), a resident of Genoa, tried to find a new route to India across the Atlantic, and as a result __ discovered the continent of America. Even after reaching America, Columbus did not get rid of the delusion that it was not India. That is why the original inhabitants of America are still called "Indians." In the Vedas, the Indus River has two names, "Sindhu" and "Indu"; the Iranians made it "Hindu," the Greeks made it "Indus"; hence via Indian. With the rise of the Muslim religion, "Hindu" became __ "Kala Khora (black devil)," as it is now __ "Native."

Meanwhile, the Portuguese discovered a new route to India by way of Africa. The fortune of India became kind to Portugal; later to the French, Dutch, Danes, and English. In the English's possession came the trade and revenue of India __

that is why the English are now the greatest nation of all. However, now India's goods in America, etc., are being produced better than in India in many places, so India is not as valuable anymore. Europeans don't want to admit this; India __ full of natives, India which is their wealth, the main support and basis of their civilization, they don't want to admit that, and they don't understand it either. Shall we stop trying to explain? Think about what it is? Those insignificant human beings of India __ the farmers,

weavers, despised small castes, reviled by their own people, those are the ones who have been silently working for ages, but they are not getting the fruits of their labor! But slowly, due to natural laws, so many changes are taking place all over the world. The country, civilization, wealth are being overturned.

O laborers of India! The result of your silent, constantly-reviled labor is the gradual rise of the power and wealth of Babylon, Iran, Alexandria, Greece, Rome, Venice, Genoa, Baghdad, Samarkand, Spain, Portugal, French, Danes, Dutch, and English. And you? __ Who thinks of this? Swamiji! Your ancestors wrote two books of philosophy, created ten poems, made ten temples __ the sky is cracking from your cries; and who sings the praises of those whose brainpower is the source of all the progress of mankind?

The victorious religious hero, the war hero, the poetic hero are all in front of everyone's eyes, worshiped by all; but where no one sees, where no one gives a cheer, where everyone hates, lives boundless endurance, infinite love and fearless efficiency; don't our poor people who are silently doing their duty day and night in their homes and doorways have courage? Many become brave when a big job comes their way, even cowards give their lives without hesitation in front of the cheers of ten thousand people, even the extremely selfish become selfless; but blessed are those who show that selflessness and devotion to duty in the smallest of tasks without anyone knowing _ those of you, the eternally downtrodden laborers of India! _

I bow to you.

This Suez Canal is also a very ancient thing. In the time of the ancient Egyptian Pharaoh, a canal was made touching both seas by connecting some brackish water canals. During the Roman Empire in Egypt, attempts were made to keep that canal open. Later, the Muslim general Amru conquered Egypt and renovated that canal by dredging the sand and changing some parts.

Then no one did much; the Turkish Sultan's representative, the Egyptian Khedive Ismail, got this

canal dug again with mostly French money. The problem with this canal is that because it passes through the desert, it gets repeatedly filled with sand.

I heard that a big warship or merchant ship cannot go through at all. If one ship is going and another is coming, there might be a collision __ for this reason, the whole canal has been divided into several parts, and at both ends of each part there is a space made wide enough for two or three ships to stay together. The main office is at the Mediterranean end, and there is a station like a railway station in each division. As soon as a ship enters the canal, news is constantly sent to that main office by wire. How many

are coming, how many are going, and where they are every moment __ news is going, and it is being marked on a big map. If another ship comes in front of one, that ship cannot go to the next station unless it gets an order from one station.

This Suez Canal is in the hands of the French. Although

most of the shares of the canal company are now in the hands of the English, still the French do all the work _ this is a political solution.

Swami Vivekananda (1863-1902): Born in Simulia, Kolkata. In his early life, his name was Narendranath Dutta. The talented student Narendranath met Ramakrishna Paramahamsadeva while studying for his F. A.

After Ramakrishnadev's death, he established a monastery in Baranagar

and travelled all over India as a monk. He took the name Vivekananda in monastic

life. In 1893,

he went to Chicago to attend the Parliament of the World's Religions as a monk and traveler. On returning home, he founded the Belur Math. His

writings "Bartaman Bharat" (Present India), "Bhabbar Katha" (Thoughts on Things), etc. are notable for their specialty.

He also published the "Prabuddha Bharat" (Awakened India) magazine in English. His eloquent, dynamic and energetic language is the special quality of his prose.
