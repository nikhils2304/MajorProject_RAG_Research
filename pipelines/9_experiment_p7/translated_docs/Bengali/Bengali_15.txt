Everyone called him Alubabu, but his real name was Alo (light). His appearance, however, didn't match his name. He had pitch-black skin, a face like a burnt eggplant, thick black mustache and beard, a double chin, and shoulder-length curly hair. He wore a tulsi bead necklace, which had also turned black. The loincloth he wore was, however, spotlessly white. His shawl was also white. He didn't wear rumpled clothes, nor did he wear shoes.

One morning, he entered my drawing room, greeted me with a 'Namaskar,' and stood there, fidgeting. That was the first time I saw him.

"What do you want?"

"Would you please help me a little?"

Many people come seeking help, most of them want money. I thought he might be one of them. I felt a little annoyed in my mind, but couldn't express my annoyance verbally. Instead, I said, "If it's not impossible, I will certainly do it. Tell me, what needs to be done?"

He had a small bag in his left hand. He put his hand inside it and took out a small bird chick. "A boy was dragging this bird chick along, tying a rope to its leg. I gave him two annas and took the chick from him."

"It seems its leg is injured. It was being dragged along, you see. Would you be kind enough to have a look? I've heard you're a great doctor."

I looked at the bird chick. It really was hurt. I applied tincture of iodine and bandaged it.

"What will you do with it, keep it as a pet?"

"If it gets well, I'll set it free. I don't have the capacity to keep any living thing as a pet. I want to very much, but I don't have money. That's why I haven't even married."

He raised his hesitant gaze and smiled at me. "Haven't seen you before, where do you live?"

"Why? Do you have some relatives here?"

"No, nothing like that. I live at Abinash Babu's house. I've been here for about seven days." He raised his hesitant gaze once more. Abinash Babu is a renowned lawyer here. He is a distant relative of Abinash Babu's wife's niece's father-in-law. Actually, he's a very good man. That's why he has kindly given me shelter. Alo Babu left with the bird chick.

A few days later, I had to go to Abinash Babu's house. There I saw Alo Babu again. He was engaged in taking care of a foreign breed puppy. Seeing me, he smiled broadly. "Binubabu's eyes are full of rheum, you see. He likes to keep dogs but doesn't know how to care for them. I cleaned his eyes with cotton soaked in water. And can a dog be kept tied up all the time? They have to play with others." He started tapping the dog in front of its face. The dog started wagging its tail and playing. Binu is Abinash Babu's younger son, ten years old.

I spoke with Abinash Babu for a while.

I said, "This Alo Babu of yours seems to be quite an unusual person!"

"Yes, he is unusual. A soul longing for affection, poor fellow. Very poor too. Have you met him?"

"Yes, he brought a bird-patient to me."

"See if you can find him a job somewhere. He loves to serve, especially if the object of his service is helpless."

A few days later, I met the Civil Surgeon. We had been in college together. While talking, Alo Babu's name came up. The Civil Surgeon said, "I can take him in as a probationary dresser at the hospital here. But he won't get more than ten rupees now. If he passes the exam, then his salary will increase."

Alo Babu started washing the wounds of patients in the hospital's outpatient department. But his job was gone within a month. One day, he came to my laboratory and sat down with a gloomy face.

"What's the news?"

"A man's wound on his leg just wouldn't heal. He showed me a medicine and said, 'Give me that medicine - then it will heal.' Apparently, many people have been cured by applying it. I applied the medicine, and almost immediately, the man started screaming, it was a big commotion! The doctor came and got very angry, saying, 'On whose orders did you pour carbolic acid on the wound!' What could I say? I kept quiet. The doctor dismissed me."

I kept quiet, what else could I say! He had really done the wrong thing.

After sitting for a while, Alo Babu left. I felt sorry for the gentleman, but couldn't think of what to do.

A few days later, Alo Babu had to leave Abinash Babu's house as well. I heard that Abinash Babu's wife had driven him away. What Alo Babu had done, no mother could tolerate. He was kissing the puppy's face with one arm and Abinash Babu's infant son, Tinu, with the other.

Finally, I gave Alo Babu shelter.

One evening, I saw him playing a solar hat and humming a song.

"Do you know how to sing and play music?"

"I used to play the dugi-tabla once. I had to sell everything due to poverty. Now I play the hat."

Needless to say, I felt a great deal of amusement. "Where did you get the hat?"

"I used to wear a suit a long time ago. Everything is gone, only this hat remains."

I learned more about Alo Babu a few days later. One day I see him coming running.

"It's ten o'clock, my watch hasn't been wound yet. I had gone to see Ram Babu's cow that gave birth to a calf, when I suddenly heard Ram Babu's clock chime ten times. Then I immediately ran, I wind my watch at exactly ten o'clock. Just like we need food, so does the watch need winding, the poor thing is late for its meal today."

He quickly entered his room. I was a little surprised, I didn't know that Alo Babu had a watch. I followed him from behind and started watching from a little distance. I saw that as soon as he entered the room, he opened his broken trunk. From inside it, he took out a small tin box. From inside the box, he took out something like a small bundle of cloth. As he opened the cloth, a red-colored silk bundle emerged. He opened it. A bundle of cotton came out, then the small watch. He kept the watch, covered in three layers of cloth, as carefully as he would keep a living thing. Taking out the watch, he sat down with a pat, then closed his eyes and slowly started winding it. It seemed as if he was worshipping.

Abinash Babu's words came to mind. A soul longing for affection! He's not getting an opportunity to love anywhere in life. Perhaps he has poured all his affection onto the watch.

One day, returning from the laboratory, I saw Alo Babu playing the hat and singing loudly. He was singing the same two lines over and over:

"No one accepted me / No one stayed with me..."

I stood there for a moment, astonished. I had never heard him sing so loudly before. Seeing me standing there, he stopped.

"You're singing so loudly today!"

"Just like that."

Then, looking at me with a hesitant smile, he said, "My watch has been stolen. Maybe it won't be wound properly at the right time."

A few drops of water rolled down from his eyes.

Alo Babu is now in a mental asylum.

He couldn't adapt himself to society at all.

Bonophul (1899-1979): Literary pseudonym of Balaichand Mukhopadhyay, 'Bonophul.' He was born in Manihari, Purnia district of Bihar. While studying at Sahebganj High School in 1915, his first poem "Mal" was published in the magazine. After that, he wrote poems under the pseudonym "Bonophul" in "Probashi" and "Bharati" magazines. He passed medicine from Patna Medical College. He is also the father of the smallest short story in Bengali literature. He is equally skilled in all branches of literature - short stories, plays, novels. His notable works include "Stabar," "Jangam," "Mantramugdh," "Hatebajare," Srimadhusudan," "Vidyasagar," etc. The autobiographical work "Paschatpat" was honored with the Rabindra Puraskar. He received the 'Jagattarini Padak' from the University of Calcutta and D.Litt degrees from Bhagalpur and Jadavpur Universities.
