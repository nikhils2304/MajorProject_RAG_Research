Shabaltala Field
Bibhutibhushan Bandyopadhyay

I saw Shabaltala Field again that day after a long time. In my aunt's home country. It was twenty-five to thirty years ago when I used to study at Durgapur Higher Primary School from my aunt's house. I never went to that village again after my aunt passed away.

That day, while going on a bullock cart for work, Shabaltala Field caught my eye, but a huge factory was being built there. Railway lines have been laid over the field - big railway lines. So many iron and steel machineries have arrived! The crowd of people and coolies, the thumping sound, what a huge affair. Temporary sheds are everywhere. The team of engineers and overseers are worn out from working. A Punjabi contractor's car is parked there. Wire poles are being installed, electric and telephone wires will be strung. Lorries loaded with bricks and wood are crowding the newly built wide roads. Lime dust and cement dust are flying in the air.

What has happened? Where is my childhood field?

Today I have come to a village to find a groom for my daughter. I got off the Martin Line train at the small station in the morning, a heap of limestone, a heap of wood, a brick kiln, a heap of stone coal, the noise of people, the line of lorries.

Where has the field gone? There is no Shabaltala Field anymore. Instead, I am going to my aunt's house on a bullock cart. Suddenly I see this situation in Shabaltala Field.

I ask the bullock cart driver -

"What's happening here?"

"I don't know, sir, I think a factory is being built."

"Oh, this is Shabaltala Field, isn't it?"

"Yes, sir."

"How far are you taking me?"

"Up to Charghata."

Uma Charan Master had been the headmaster of Durgapur UP Pathshala for so long.

"Where have all the villages gone?"

"They have all been moved away."

"Moved away? How far?"

"Very far - Ujitpur, Mansatala, Chhaoyal-Mari, Bede-Poti, Hanskhali - those areas."

It was a midday in my sixteen year old. And I remembered Durgapur Higher Primary School. I won't say village. A broken room of the landlords of the village. In a corner of the sitting room with pigeon nests, on an old cot lay a worn-out mat and a jute bag filled with odds and ends. On the wall, his hookah hung on a nail, and on the bamboo shelf, his two half-dirty dhotis and his unique old shirt were carefully hung - and under the cot lay a pair of wooden sandals. A faded tin trunk stood in a corner.

My eyes were drawn to the mat, the old shirt, the trunk, and - most of all - Uma Charan Master. Sometimes I wondered if Uma Charan Master would ever write a book again. That memory was vague - but still, I kept looking at the book-filled jute bag. In the middle of the lesson, Master would start telling the fame of someone or other. He would seriously take out a water-stained, oily pen from his pocket and start writing with it.

I was young then, I didn't understand what a writer or literature was. I just kept looking at the stern face of the master.

The book's name was "Akel Gudum" - he used to say "Farce." At my age, I didn't understand the meaning of either "Akel Gudum" or "Farce." I remember there was a English-reading boy in the book and reading in the style made it seem that the English-reading boy was not a very good person.

Uma Charan Master would look at us proudly and say - Do you know what the brother-in-law of Gobardanga's second son said after reading this book? He said, Uma Charan Babu, you will one day be an author equal to Girish Ghosh. Understand?

I asked - Who is Girish Ghosh, Pandit Moshai?

Uma Charan Babu smiled compassionately at me and said - Girish Ghosh? Don't you know? Huh! What do you know?

I was silent with shame. What could I say? I really didn't know who Girish Ghosh was, I had never even heard the name! Uma Charan tried to sell this valuable farce to us and he sold many. Every student's house had one or two copies of "Akel Gudum." He didn't have a habit of returning change when paid salary. He would say - How much is left? Seven annas? Take a good book instead. Let everyone read it at home.

One day my aunt said - Hey, I gave the salary money, didn't he return nine annas?

- No, aunt. Master Moshai gave a book instead.

- What book?

- Akel Gudum.

- Oh my, what kind of book is that? What did you say and bring that book? Like the unlucky master, like the unlucky boy! Don't you hear the name of the book - "Akel Gudum". Bring a Kentor Shatnam instead - return that book.

- That won't happen, aunt, he doesn't write those kind of useless books. This is a farce.

- What is that again?

- You won't understand? Have you heard of Girish Ghosh?

- Who is that again? There is no one of that name in our village. Is he from Durgapur?

- You won't understand. He writes farce books like our Master Moshai.

My aunt would scold - You shut up, boy - you have become too scholarly! I don't know - if you pinch him on the cheek, milk will come out, he knows - you rascal! I don't understand all those Girish Ghosh Satish Ghosh - return that book tomorrow and bring Kentor Shatnam if you can, otherwise bring back the nine annas - go.

One day Uma Charan Master explained to us, all the great writers had been school teachers like him in their early lives. In the middle of the conversation, Sarada from our class suddenly said - Why don't you go anywhere?

- Why, son?

- That's what I'm saying.

Uma Charan Master scolded him and made him sit down, but we understood where the boy's doubt was. If Uma Charan Master was still teaching in our school at this age, then at what age would he go and do some great work somewhere? But Satu from our class used to say - Master Moshai is a very great scholar. It doesn't happen like that.

I said - Why, son?

- He sits near the Chaltebagan field every day and what does he do! He probably writes. He is a poet, after all.

One day I went with Satu to see what was happening.

Chaltebagan was a very old mango and tamarind tree garden where the darkness was like evening even in the daytime. Many thick vines were intertwined with the trees. After crossing the garden, there was a small field, Uma Charan Master was sitting near that field, under the shade of the garden, on a torn mat. Papers and books were scattered on the mat. Small clods of soil were placed on them so that they would not fly away. We stood behind the sheora bush and watched, he was sometimes lying down and writing something, sometimes thinking something, sometimes laughing to himself, mumbling something.

Satu said respectfully in a whisper - Did you see? He is a poet!

I said - What is he doing?

- He is writing.

- What is he muttering?

- Poets do that.

We watched quietly for a long time. This was the first time in my life that I had the rare fortune to see the activities of a living poet. I remember, next to the sheora bush was a big wood-apple tree, the ground was covered with ripe wood-apples. That age was prone to greed, especially greed for wood-apples, I controlled my greed with great difficulty. The reason was that my mother's elder brother, my elder uncle - who was then thirty-five years old, I heard - he used to call Master Moshai "Dada" (elder brother).

Just as we had gone there silently, we came away silently with wonder and joy in our minds. After this, whenever Uma Charan Master used to teach, I used to look at him with my mouth open. He is a poet indeed! He has also become a poet. Sarada doesn't understand anything.

A year passed. A few of us boys were preparing to take the Higher Primary examination. That year Uma Charan Master would take us to Ranaghat to give the examination. Four boys - I remember Kanai of the Chakraborty family, me, Satu and Sarada. When we reached the Shabaltala field after walking from Durgapur, it was past noon.

I remember that afternoon very well. The Shabaltala field was shimmering in the sun. A vast field with jujube trees, sheora-data and wild basil jungles here and there. There was no village near this field in any direction. I had never seen such a big field. A narrow dirt road went far through the middle of the field. A fragrance of some flower was coming out in the afternoon sun. We were all tired of walking, Uma Charan Master said - Let's sit down under those trees for a while.

Each of us had a bundle on our shoulders. In that bundle, there were our books, clothes, towels and mats. We unloaded the bundles and sat down under a jujube tree.

Master Moshai said - See if there are any jujubes.

Satu looked and said - There are jujubes, small ones - can they be eaten?

Everyone ate a little. Kanai's mother had given him coconut molasses to eat on the way for lunch. I wanted to eat, Kanai refused. We started playing around in that open field with great joy. It seemed that the small village world had suddenly taken on a new state of fairytale.

The suggestion of the distant land that the wandering boys always crave in their minds, today here after coming to Shabaltala field, in the sun-filled afternoon air, among the jungle of wild basil, I was seeing that distant land directly before my eyes. Uma Charan Master was reading his same old, monotonous book, I don't know how they felt, but my mind was filled with the liberation of a vagabond. I used to play in the neighborhoods, I had never seen such a big field. Playing in the field among the thickets of wild basil, the faint smell of the field was telling me that this is the world of life, of freedom. The murmur of the shalik and chatare birds, the holes of foxes in the sandy soil here and there, the red kelekauda flowers were spreading the dust in this country as if no human lives, only wild bushes. The wild koluchatka vine has wrapped around a mango tree. There is no obligation here. There is no place for learning and writing in the middle of this truant game and leisure at all. No one will say don't play here - this is not a world for people, this is a new country. A world where studies have fallen asleep.

Suddenly Satu said - Hey, where is Master Moshai?

I said - Why, wasn't he under the jujube tree?

- I haven't seen him for a long time. Where did he go? We have to catch the train, don't we?

It seemed to me that catching the train would not make us kings! We are fine here, but where is the Higher Primary examination, where is the station? More than two hours have passed.

The inspector had said at school that there was no problem in Ranaghat. The exam is written in the register, there are guards there, leaning over your head, there is no way to cheat. If someone is caught, they will be taken to the sub-divisional magistrate. He will threaten you and say that you will be punished like a goat to a butcher. If he wants, he can also imprison you, he can also fine you. There is no room to whisper. You have to enter the hall like a banyan tree leaf trembling in the wind. What a terrible consequence of student life!

After seeing the Shabaltala field, after running around here for these two hours, I seemed to have found the meaning of life. It is to play in the free peace and freedom of such a vast, wild, dusty field. What is the point of taking the exam! Kanai also came and said - When will we go? Where is Master Moshai?

Indeed, he could not be seen in any direction. Everyone went out to look for him. Satu started calling - O Master Moshai, Master M-sha-i -

There was no response.

Satu said with a frightened face - Did a tiger take him?

Kanai said - Nonsense, will there be a man-eating tiger here?

- No, there isn't! Who told you!

- Then where did he go?

I said - You guys look for him. I'll play here.

Just then Sarada came running breathlessly and said - Quickly - quickly come - see what -

We all said together - What happened? Is he alive?

While saying this, we ran behind Sarada. After running quite a distance, Sarada stopped and, pointing in front, told us to be quiet and walked on tiptoe.

In a dry canal-like low place, behind the kuch bush, Uma Charan Master was sitting bent over, writing and muttering something to himself, even laughing to himself in such a manner. Anyone would say he was an insane madman. We had never seen him write so absorbedly, we had never seen him laugh to himself in that way.

Satu looked at him with admiration and said - Master Moshai is a real poet.

Sarada agreed with him and said - Exactly.

Kanai and I, without saying a word, kept looking at this real living poet. How lucky we are to have such a Master.

Kanai said a little later - But brother, it's evening. What will we do if we don't call him? Let's call him! What do you say?

No one dared.

Sarada's faith in the poet was a little faint, he made a coughing sound once or twice to announce our presence.

Satu said quietly - Hey! Slowly!

Sarada said - Yes, slowly, what else! We will die in this field this evening! Let the tiger catch us all - saying this, as soon as he coughed loudly once, Uma Charan Master turned back in surprise.

Sarada said - Come, Master Moshai, it's getting late - the station is still a long way -

Uma Charan Master hurriedly gathered his papers and came up to us from the dry canal with a smile of embarrassment. He said - That's right, I see it's getting late. Let's go!

Then, looking back, he said - The place is very beautiful - isn't it?

Satu said respectfully - What were you doing there, Master Moshai? What is there?

Uma Charan Master scolded and said - What will you understand? Do you know what a scenery is? A wonderful scenery at that place. I was writing a poem. What a wonderful field, do you understand anything?

The fact that I too had strangely liked this afternoon this field, finding its resonance in Master Moshai's words, my mind was filled with happiness. I got a new vision that day, on the way to take the Higher Primary examination. What a great teacher's job Uma Charan Master did that day - did he understand that himself?

My story ends here. The history of Uma Charan Master also ends here. Those are stories of almost thirty-two years ago. Uma Charan Master is probably not alive today. After growing up, I have not read the writings of any poet named Uma Charan Chakraborty anywhere, nor have I heard the name from anyone. It doesn't matter. Does everyone have good fortune! Today, after so long, coming to Shabaltala field, I remember that wonderful afternoon of my childhood again, I remember Uma Charan Master. I was saddened to see that the Shabaltala field had been completely destroyed. That beauty, that solitude has been erased. After so long, my mind felt for Uma Charan Master.

Bibhutibhushan Bandyopadhyay (1894 - 1950): Born in Banogram, Twenty-four Parganas. His childhood and adolescence were spent in poverty, deprivation and hardship. His experience in the field of livelihood was wide and multifaceted, he also taught for a long time. The exquisite beauty of the rural nature fascinated him. His literary career began with the story "Upekkhito" published in the Probashi magazine. One of his most famous works, "Pather Panchali", was written in Bhagalpur. In just twenty-one years of literary life, he has written many stories, novels, travelogues, diaries and children's literature. His notable books include "Bone Pahare", "Maraner Danka Baje", "Hire Manik Jwale", "Chader Pahar", "Aranyak", "Aparajito", "Ichamati", "Debayan", "Adarsha Hindu Hotel", "Hrishtipradip", "Kinnardal", etc.

"Utkarna", "Smritir Rekha", "Abhijatrik" etc. are his diary type compositions. In his writings, rural nature and the wilderness have gained amazing vitality, as well as the sorrows, poverty, dreams, hopes and aspirations of rural Bengal have come alive in his writings. He was posthumously awarded the "Rabindra Puraskar" in 1951.
