Rabindranath Tagore

In that chaotic, primeval era,
When Ashtaa, dissatisfied with his own creation,
Repeatedly devastated the new world,
In those days of his impatient, frequent headshakes,
The violent sea's arm
Snatched you away, Africa—

Binding you within the dense guard of the forest's trees,
In the light-starved interior.

There, in secluded leisure, you
Collected the mysteries of the insurmountable,
Recognized the inscrutable signs of water, land, and sky,
Nature's unseen magic
Awakened mantras in your subconscious mind.
You mocked the terrifying,
Sought to defeat apprehension,
Making yourself fierce in the overwhelming glory of dread,

In the thunderous cadence of destruction.

Beneath the black veil,
Your human form was unknown,
In the tainted gaze of neglect.
They came with iron handcuffs,
Their nails sharper than your wolves',
Came the human-catching horde,
Blinded by pride, more than your sunless forests.
The barbarous greed of civilization
Bared its own shameless inhumanity.
In your voiceless weeping, on the vaporous forest path,
The dust became muddy, mixed with your blood and tears,
Under the vile mud-ridden soles of the robber's boots,
A hideous mark
Was left forever on your humiliated history.

Across the sea, at that very moment, in their neighborhoods,
The temple bells were ringing for the worship.

Rabindranath Tagore (1861-1941): Birth

In the poet's music resonated

The adoration of the beautiful.

When the western horizon descends into the twilight,
When the beasts emerge from their hidden lairs—
In the suffocating gale of the storm,

Poet,

Stand at the door of that humanity stripped of dignity;
Let that be your civilization's last virtuous word,

Announcing with ominous sounds

Amidst the raving of savagery,

The final hour of the dying day.

[From Bharati and Balak, published from the Tagore family home in Jorasanko, Kolkata. From a young age, Tagore regularly wrote in various magazines and newspapers. Throughout his long life, he created countless poems, songs, short stories, novels, essays, plays, and wrote extensively on various topics, and painted pictures. He was the first Asian to win the Nobel Prize in 1913. His first books of poetry were "Sandhya Sangeet" and "Prabhat Sangeet". The unprecedented development of his talent is evident in these works. This phase of work also include "Chhabi O Gaan", "Bhanusimha Thakurer Padabali" and "Kadi O Komal". From 1888 to 1896, within just eight years, the poet wrote three books of poetry - "Manasi", "Sonar Tori" and "Chitra". Not only that, his construction techniques are also evident in these poems. The poet's self-realization is evident in these poems, which are marked by emotion, imagination, and mastery of rhyme. After this, the poet wrote "Kanakani", "Katha O Kahini", "Kalpana, Kshanika and Naivedya". The poet Rabindranath dedicated the book "Gitanjali" to Gitanjali, a collection of songs, which won him the Nobel Prize in 1916. "Shesh Saptak" marks the end of the lyric poetry phase.
