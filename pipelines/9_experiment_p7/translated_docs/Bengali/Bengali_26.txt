In fairy tales, there was a king with two queens __ the favored queen and the unfavored queen, similarly, Bengali also has two queens of language; one is affectionately named Sadhu Bhasha (formal language), and the other is Kathya Bhasha (colloquial language), some call it Cholti Bhasha (current language).' Rabindranath Tagore wrote this while discussing the two forms of the Bengali language. Almost every developed language in the world has these two forms. In the nineteenth century, the Sanskrit-educated Bengali scholars created a formal, erudite language rich in Sanskrit words and based on Sanskrit grammar and vocabulary for the purpose of writing Bengali prose; we call it Sadhuriti (formal style). The matter can be clarified with the help of a few examples _

1. This mountain peak is constantly adorned with a deep blue hue due to the constant connection of the cloud groups traveling through the sky; the plateau region is constantly lush, cool, and beautiful due to the dense forest trees covering it; at the foot, the cheerful Godavari is flowing with great force, spreading its waves. (Sitar Bonobas/Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar)

2. Saying this, the Arab general bid him farewell with affectionate greetings and a handshake. He left immediately. The Arab general also, upon seeing the sunrise, mounted his horse and set out in pursuit of him. (Adbhut Atitheeyata/Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar)

(Durgeshnandini / Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay)

On the other hand, the form of the Bengali language that has been recognized as the ideal language suitable for literary composition based on the spoken polite language of the educated people of Kolkata is what we call Standard Colloquial Bengali (Promito Cholito Bangla). Although there are differences between this standard colloquial style and the prevalent spoken language. The colloquial form of Bengali in Purulia-Bankura is not the same as the colloquial form of Bengali language in North Bengal. There are many variations of spoken colloquial Bengali depending on the region.

Therefore, since then, colloquial Bengali has gradually come to mean the polite colloquial form of the Bengali language of Kolkata, Nabadwip, Shantipur, i.e., the region along the Bhagirathi River, which has been recognized as the Standard Colloquial Bengali. A few examples are given below __

1. There is a Parsi fellow traveler on our ship. His pointed trimmed beard and large eyes are the first things that catch the eye. He is young. He has been traveling in Europe for nine months and has adopted British clothing and manners. He says he doesn't like India. (Europ-jatrir Diary/Rabindranath Tagore)

2. Reaching Natore. Everything is so grand. How beautifully they have decorated the house, the drawing room. Chandeliers, cushions, nice expensive flower vases, carpets, there is no comparison to them__it's like Indrapuri (the city of the gods). (Ghoroa/Abanindranath Tagore)

3. The incident probably happened in the winter of 1928 __ I don't quite remember the year and date. I was returning from my in-laws' place in Gaya. I will catch the Dehra-Dun Express. I have the return part of a middle-class return ticket with me. (Pathcholti/Suniti Kumar Chattopadhyay)

4. This morning, I woke up to see that the entire sky was filled with rain as far as the eye could see. An uninterrupted, continuous stream of rain is falling from above. The stream is not so fine that it escapes the eye, yet not so coarse that it fills the eye. (Boitha/Promath Chowdhury)

5. The boatman did not like the way he spoke. Thinking about various possibilities and impossibilities, he became firm in his mind. __ Should I go or stay lying in this dark alley? (Adab/ Samaresh Basu)

6 The use of semi-Tatsama, Tadbhava, Deshi (native), and foreign words is much more in the colloquial style.

* The use of compound words is much less in this style. Instead, the use of idioms or common sentence structures is abundantly seen.
* Abbreviated forms of pronouns and verbs are used in colloquial Bengali.

Different forms of postpositions are prevalent in colloquial Bengali. For example: in the Sadhu form: dwara, hoite, sohit etc., in the colloquial form: diye, hote, songe etc.
The specific rules of sentence structure that exist in the Sadhu style are much more relaxed in the colloquial style and are often changed. Rabindranath showed _
Kothay gelen tomar dada? (Where did your brother go?)
Tomar dada kothay gelen? (Where did your brother go, your brother?)
Dada tomar gelen kothay? (Brother, where did you go?)
