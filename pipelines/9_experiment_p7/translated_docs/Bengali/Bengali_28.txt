A funny characteristic of the Bengali language is the application of the same word in multiple senses. The application of a single word in multiple senses is a common practice in our language. The same word, used in different sentences, expresses completely different meanings and sentiments. This chapter briefly discusses this topic with examples.

The same words used in different senses fall into the noun, adjective, and verb categories.

In this discussion, four nouns – *chokh* (eye), *matha* (head), *hat* (hand), *buk* (chest) – four adjectives – *paka* (ripe/mature), *kacha* (raw/immature), *boro* (big), *chhoto* (small) – and two verbs – *kata* (cut), *bosa* (sit) – are used in separate sentences to demonstrate how the same word is used in multiple senses to express different sentiments and contribute to the beauty and scope of the language.

**Noun:**

**Chokh (Eye)**

1.  At first, I couldn't understand the person's true nature, but later your words opened my eyes (gained true knowledge).
2.  Didibhai showed me the problem of the shop's scale with evidence (showing with proof).
3.  While walking under the tree, a coconut fell on my head, and I saw stars before my eyes (feeling disoriented).
4.  Keep an eye on the child so that he doesn't fall (close observation).
5.  Grandmother was extremely surprised (excessively astonished) when we arrived at her house early in the morning without prior notice.
6.  The neighbors are envious (feeling jealous) seeing Ramesh building a new house.
7.  By donating only five taka for flood relief, Ram Babu proved that even though he is rich, he has no shame (shamelessness).
8.  Reshma's performance as Chitrangada in the neighborhood's Rabindra Jayanti program was eye-catching (worth seeing).
9.  Gradually, the naughty boy became Kaki's eyesore (unfavorable person).
10. The landlord's agent, threatening (oppressing) the poor tenants, declared that they had to pay double the tax.

**Matha (Head)**

1.  The army, accepting the general's order humbly (accepting with utmost respect), marched victoriously towards the enemy camp.
2.  Shyam Babu is acting as if he has bought everyone's head (dominating attitude) by donating more money at the neighborhood event.
3.  Seeing his son's arrogant behavior in front of everyone, he was deeply ashamed (humiliated).
4.  Grandma has spoiled (ruined the nature) the youngest grandson by giving him excessive pampering.
5.  Rehan, holding his friend's hands, said, "I swear (taking an oath) on your head, don't quarrel with them."
6.  Due to the exorbitant increase in the price of goods, it has become a headache (cause of worry) for Borda to run such a big family.
7.  Since the secondary exam is only two months away, stop thinking about other things (worrying/thinking) and concentrate on studies.
8.  The elderly Rahim Chacha is respected by everyone because he is the head (chief person) of the village.
9.  Everyone loves Peter because he has a very good head (intelligence) in studies from a young age.
10. You can't achieve your own goals for long by deceiving (breaking a jackfruit on someone else's head) others.
11. To earn their living, the Sherpas work very hard (shed sweat from their heads to their feet).

**Hat (Hand)**

1.  Amal stole the jar of pickles by bribing (bringing under control) his brother with the promise of sharing.
2.  Everyone knows about his open-handed (generous) nature when it comes to helping the needy.
3.  Due to a family dispute, our neighborhood's dilapidated Mallikbari has changed hands (ownership change) five times.
4.  A self-respecting person never begs (asking for help) from others, even in great distress.
5.  Due to Dr. Babu's skillful hands (expertise), the widow's only child was saved from the jaws of death.
6.  The new employee's habit of stealing (stealing habit) became known within a few days.
7.  Even though I have a sincere desire to help the poor, my hands are tied (helpless) by the law.
8.  When the workers protested against the injustice, the factory owner closed the factory and financially harmed them (causing financial harm instead of beating).

**Buk (Chest)**

1.  The sick child rested his head on his mother's chest (bosom) and fell asleep peacefully.
2.  Swami Ji's heart breaks (heart breaks) seeing the suffering of the oppressed people.
3.  The motherland has affection, love, and compassion for her martyred (spiritually pure) sons.
4.  Kuntabir alone is protecting the fake Bundigarh with his life (giving his life).
5.  Don't scare him, he has a brave (courageous) heart like a dacoit.
6.  Unity in diversity has been built on the heart (in the middle) of India.
7.  The mother's heart has turned to stone (silent with grief) after the death of her only child.
8.  There is a chestful (immense) of love for you.
9.  We will protect the country's independence by joining hands and hearts (bond of affection).
10. You, as the elder brother, must be brave (gather courage) in this time of crisis.

**Adjective:**

**Paka (Ripe/Mature)**

1.  Even though life is unbearable due to the scorching heat, ripe (mature) mangoes and jackfruits bring water to the tongue.
2.  You will reach your destination by walking straight along the paved concrete road (concrete) alongside Sagardighi.
3.  Even though he is young, the boy talks very maturely (prematurely).
4.  Raptankaku hugged William and made a firm (final) promise that they would cut the cake together on the 25th of December.

**Kacha (Raw/Immature)**

1.  The village's temporary (temporary) road is slippery with rain and mud.
2.  You can't get to the bottom of the problem because you are young (immature).
3.  The national highway is used for the import and export of raw materials (natural product).
4.  Kumbhakarna woke up with a loud scream as his light sleep (incomplete) was broken.
5.  Don't do such a wrong (mistake) thing as to provoke a conflict with the cunning Mejo Babu.
6.  Such poor writing (low quality) should never be printed in newspapers.
7.  First, write down the calculation in the rough book (draft), then finalize it.

**Boro (Big)**

1.  Everyone talks big (arrogant speech) without understanding the actual situation.
2.  A big guest (brother-in-law) has come to the house today, so delicious food is being cooked.
3.  I saw that the boy who committed the mistake was defending himself loudly (yelling).
4.  Kanu Babu of our village is a big man (rich person) but very stingy.
5.  Great people (great personalities) like Vidyasagar, Rammohan, Haji Mohsin, and Mother Teresa are our ideals.
6.  The head clerk (chief person) of the head office is very friendly and funny as a person.
7.  Sarala's mother wiped her tears and blessed Mizanur, saying, "Be great" (famous).
8.  By mingling with friends in college, the boy has learned the manners of the rich (affluent people's manners).
9.  Only Javed and his parents live in such a big house (size).
10. He has spoken the words so boldly (aspiring) that I could not ignore them anymore.

**Choto (Small)**

1.  Don't look down (contempt) on the helpless, poor, and ignorant countrymen.
2.  The small (size) cottages of the village are covered by the pleasant shade of green leaves of the trees.
3.  Narendra was fearless, truthful, and empathetic to the suffering of others from a young (age).
4.  I didn't realize that you would behave like such a small person (rude) even after studying so much.
5.  The settlement of this minor land-related issue will be done in the lower court (lower court).
6.  I have become humiliated (loss of respect) in front of everyone because of your lies today.
7.  Mejo Kaka's face has shrunk (shrunk) and become small as a result of a day's backbreaking work.
8.  It is possible to do good for the country even through small (minor) works.
9.  Don't belittle (despise) him because he is physically weak; his mental strength is immense.

**Verb:**

**Kata (Cut)**

1.  It is not right for two friends to argue (debate) over trivial matters.
2.  Shyamal starts commenting (giving opinions) on anything that is discussed.
3.  The landlords have had a new pond dug (excavated) to solve the problem of drinking water shortage in our village.
4.  Sitting in the mud courtyard, Satu and Naru are drawing (drawing) in their notebooks.
5.  While in the theater group, Tarapad learned to cut his hair in a new style (styling hair).
6.  Once you get used to being alone, you will see that all those ghost fears will disappear (courage being built).
7.  A lot of sweets will be needed for Bhojharibabu's daughter's wedding, so the confectioner has prepared (prepared) twice the amount of cheese.
8.  Peter and Robin have given (written/made) a large check to help the flood victims.
9.  Although the beginning of the eighth-grade Wahida's speech in impromptu elocution was good, it ended abruptly (harmony broken).

**Bosa (Sit)**

1.  The yogurt hasn't set yet (congealed).
2.  Will life go on if you keep sitting (being idle) like this?
3.  The key is not fitting (becoming suitable) in the lock.
4.  The village council meeting will be held (to be held) tomorrow.
5.  The baby is swinging (being in place) in the cradle.
