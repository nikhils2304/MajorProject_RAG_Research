Budhua's Bird

By Alokeranjan Dasgupta

Do you know whose house this is? City folks were here yesterday,

Terrible moss has covered its windows and walls today,

As if its debts have terribly increased,

That's why no bird even sits here.

Our hut is far better than this, far better.

Flocks of blue birds on the smooth, clean courtyard—

Budhua wonders in amazement.

Budhua walks in wonder,

Forgetting the name of the rustic path,

Suddenly climbing a hill, face raised like a mountain,

He thinks: Whose house is that, so blue,

Better than my house, even better,

Its courtyard so clean, a huge bird-perching wall.

I will go there too, who can take me?

In this way, with Budhua's call every day,

The pitcher of the path brims with light,

Those who carry Budhua's laughter on silvery wings.
