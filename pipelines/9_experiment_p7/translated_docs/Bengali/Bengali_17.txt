In 1200 Bengali Era, on Wednesday morning, the 11th of Ashwin, Rasmani was born in the Mahishya caste in Halisahar. She was the daughter of a poor man. Her father was locally known as Haru Gharami. Rasmani was Haru Gharami's daughter. Her mother's name was Rampriya. The mother named her fair-skinned daughter Rani, which would later become Rasmani. The villagers combined both names and started calling her Rani Rasmani. One day, she would truly become a queen. Like Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi, Rani Rasmani of Bengal would fight against the English rulers.

Her father, Harekrishna, known as Haru in the community, had a little education. He taught his daughter as well. In the village where they lived, only his house hosted readings of the Ramayana, Mahabharata, and Puranas at night. Villagers would come with lanterns to listen. It was a beautiful sight! Young Rasmani would watch in amazement -- a row of lights from the dark path crossing the field, as if floating in the air, going further and further away. They had come with light to the pilgrimage, now everyone was returning together. It was as if there were two people inside every person. Lawsuits, quarrels, factions, fights, poverty, illness were all there, but the eagerness to recognize the path of the past in the night light was no less. Mahabharata-Bhagavata's Srikrishna, Ramayana's Rama, Lakshmana, Sita__ everyone was there, would always be there. A feeling of happiness came to Rasmani's young heart. It was a great feeling.

The Bairagi family. Tulsi garlands around their necks, tilak on their foreheads. Applying tilak took time for everyone. Let there be religion! Rasmani also meticulously drew tilak on her nose, forehead, and arms. It made the beautiful girl even more beautiful. Rasmani's mother, Rampriya, did not live long. Rasmani turned seven and saw that her mother was gone. Rampriya passed away from a fever of just eight days.

Rasmani was eleven years old. Strikingly beautiful. At that time, there were many rich and important people in Kolkata. On one side, Prince Dwarkanath at Jorasanko Thakur Bari, on the other side, the Dutta family of Maniktala __ where Swami Vivekananda would come. Foreigners had also arrived __ David Hare had closed his watch business and dedicated himself to the spread of education. Alexander Duff was keen on spreading Christianity. Raja Rammohan Roy was at the bottom, Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar at Sanskrit College, Debendranath was growing up in the Jorasanko Thakur Bari. The doors of the Brahmo Samaj would open this time. It was a time of Bengali awakening in religion, literature, and culture. Bengal had also entered into business. Opportunities for profit had come. The sea was calling the daredevils of Kolkata. Many, many years ago, John সাহেব had built a market here. It became known as “Janbazar” in popular speech. It was next to the present Free School Street, New Market __ . One rich Bengali in this Janbazar was Sritiram Das. He was poor, but had become wealthy. No one could do it for him, he did it himself. Sritiram was born into a poor family in 1753. He had learned a little Bengali and mathematics at the guru's school, when both his father and mother died. Sritiram was then fourteen years old. What would happen now! He was not alone. Two younger brothers __ Ramtanu and Kaliprasad. The fourteen-year-old elder brother was very worried. The test of fate!

At that time, the famous landlord Mannababu was living in Janbazar. Sritiram took shelter with his aunt along with his two brothers in this Mannapari family. The enterprising Sritiram was not born to live a life of poverty. Money was flying all around in the history's Kolkata. One could become rich if they could catch it. Mannababu's wife helped him get a job. Sritiram quickly learned some broken English and started working as a broker and supplier for the English soldiers at Fort William. A high-ranking English officer at the fort took a liking to Sritiram. The wheel of fortune turned. He went to Dhaka with that সাহেব. On the সাহেব's recommendation, he was appointed to a high position in the Natore Raj estate. He made a lot of money and returned to Kolkata in 1777, when he was twenty-four. Young Sritiram. With enough wealth and influence, he married the eleven-year-old daughter of the Mannapari family who had sheltered him. As dowry, he received several houses in Janbazar and sixteen bighas of land. He had two sons in quick succession. In 1779, his first son Harichandra, and in 1783, his second son Rajchandra.

Sritiram started a business of import and export, which progressed rapidly. In 1787, he became the agent of Burn Company. Agent is an Arabic word, meaning principal representative in Bengali. Several parganas under the Natore king were put up for auction in Kolkata due to unpaid rent. In 1800, Sritiram bought the Makimpur pargana for ninety thousand rupees with the help of Dewan Shibram Sen. He appointed Kalaprasad as the Naib of this pargana. He started shipping bamboo and timber from there. Sritiram opened a warehouse in Beleghata to sell these goods. In those days, bamboo was tied together and floated down the river to Janbazar. This was commonly called “Bansher Maar” (Bamboo Raft). He also established a salt trading depot in Beleghata around this time. Orphan Sritiram became the number one rich man in Kolkata.

The awakening of philanthropy. He arranged the marriages of both his sons. Within a year of his wife's death, he remarried. Astonishing! Yes, astonishing indeed. He educated his younger son Harichandra according to the times. Harichandra could not enjoy so much happiness and wealth for long and passed away prematurely. In 1802, the marriage of his youngest son Rajchandra was arranged. This time too, the same wedding preparations.

An extraordinary woman, a form of power, would come as Rajchandra's wife. She would enrich this family of Janbazar and the whole of Bengal with her contributions. Sritiram would give Rajchandra another marriage. The search was on. He would occasionally go on boat trips to Tribeni to bathe in the Ganges. During one such boat trip, he saw a very beautiful girl standing from afar. Returning to Janbazar, he told his family about her. When matchmakers started searching, news came that the girl's father was Harekrishna Das. Sritiram sent a proposal for marriage to the poor Harekrishna’s son. It was as if Harekrishna had received the moon. Could this be true!

Rajchandra was educated, restrained, and religious. Sritiram would connect a bright chapter of Bengal's renaissance with this marriage.

What a thing! The daughter of a poor man with the son of Kolkata's richest man! Still, it must be said that in her childhood, when she was very young, Rasmani’s mother had said, “What is it, Rani! You will surely be a queen one day!” Her dream of seeing the flower of destiny had come true.

In 1804, Rasmani was married to Rajchandra with great pomp and ceremony. Was it just a wedding? At first, it seemed so to Rasmani. Sritiram, not Sritiram, Rajchandra, Janbazar, but the nineteenth century and the Rani.

Sritiram passed away in 1813. In 1817, Rajchandra started the construction of the present-day vast family estate. At that time, the value of his movable and immovable property was around six and a half lakhs of rupees.

Sritiram Das’s worthy son Rajchandra. Educated in modern education, modern in thinking, free from superstition, of generous nature. Taking over his father’s business and property, he appointed Irwin Company as an agent in England and started exporting silk clothes, musk, opium, indigo, etc. to England. He was a shrewd businessman, and also very enterprising. He sold opium, purchased at twenty-five thousand rupees in auction, for one lakh and twenty-five thousand rupees on the same day. An example of one day making one hundred thousand rupees profit. Gradually wealth, people, splendor, and pomp increased. Everyone believed that Lakshmi had come home in the form of a worthy wife. Her name was Rasmani.

Rasmani and Rajchandra had two daughters – Padmamani and Kumudini. Four years later, a youngest daughter Jagadamba was born. Later, her role would be very important in history. In just one year, Rasmani had her third daughter, Karunamayi. The following year, her eldest daughter was married. Soon everything was moving towards an invisible historical future. Rasmani will be written in history. In 1819, Rasmani had a son, but he died.

In 1831, the third daughter, Karunamayi, passed away. She left behind her husband, Mathuramohan Biswas, and her only son, Bhupal Chandra. This Mathuramohan would one day become a historical character. The day was approaching. In the dressing room of the future, several characters were preparing themselves. That is why Mathuramohan did not get freedom from Janbazar.

In 1833, Rajchandra married his youngest daughter Jagadamba to Mathuramohan. This man was a devotee of new Bengal, a devout follower of Sri Ramakrishna Paramhansa. He spent a lot of money on charity, feeding ten to twelve students and bearing all the expenses of the college at his request. His conjugal life also reached perfection with his wife.

He spent a lot of money to build a beautiful bathing ghat on the Ganges, which is now famous as "Babughat."

He donated money for the excavation of canals, the construction of the old ghat and road in Nimtala, and the construction of the Metcalfe Hall in Beleghata, and to the famine relief fund. He helped the Ahiritola residence. In recognition of these benevolent public works, the East India Company awarded Rajchandra the title of "Roy."

But in just three years, this dutiful, hardworking, and generous social worker said goodbye to the world.

In the same year, Gadadhar Chattopadhyay, Paramhansa Sri Ramakrishna, came to Kamarpukur.

And what a wonder, Rasmani's power was revealed this time.

In those days, 55 thousand rupees were spent on her husband's post-death rituals. Then she took the responsibility of managing the vast property into her own hands. Many people suspected, could she do it! Maintaining a huge zamindari scattered across different parts of the country. On the one hand, there was no shortage of wicked zamindars, their thugs, murderers, thieves, robbers, the English ruling class, oppressive English indigo planters, countless problems of society, the miserable poverty of the farmers, famine, epidemic, floods, endangered Bengal, endangered Bengali. The condition of women was even more pathetic.

But Rasmani's prudence, courage, and diplomatic intelligence would soon shine. Rajchandra had given two lakhs of rupees as a loan to his special friend, the renowned Prince Dwarkanath. Dwarkanath could not repay that loan.

Sitting behind the screen, Rani Ma was communicating with Mathuramohan. One day, Dwarkanath Roy came to Rani Ma. After much conversation, he said, "Rani Ma, I would be very grateful if you could now return the two lakhs of rupees that my husband gave you."

The veiled woman was not what he thought. Her strength was limitless.

The aristocratic Dwarkanath was stunned. He felt very embarrassed. He realized, "This is no ordinary woman. She is power itself. Bold, fiercely courageous, like a thunderbolt!

Rani Ma then expressed her desire to supervise the zamindari herself, saying, "It may be good to keep Mathur in charge, but where will I find trustworthy people like you?"

Fearless Rani Ma now clearly said, "That would be very good. However, I have not yet learned how much money is owed to my husband and by whom. If my husband had taken a loan of two lakhs from you, I would have received it."

It was not possible for him to repay the two lakhs in cash at that time. He immediately wrote the Swaroopur Pargana, which was located in the districts of Rangpur and Dinajpur, in Rani Rasmani's name. The annual income of that pargana at that time was 36 thousand rupees.

He again approached Rani Ma through Mathuramohan and expressed his desire to take over the supervision of the zamindari. Rani Maa acknowledged, "It is presumptuous for an ordinary widow like me to supervise such a vast estate. I have understood that only my sons-in-law who are as capable and aristocratic as you can do this work. Dwarkanath later admitted that the zamindars of that time did not have a very good reputation. They used various tactics to increase their wealth. They brutally collected revenue.

The name of one such zamindar was Jagannathpur's zamindar, Iimratan Roy. The tenants of Mathurapur were fed up with him. Looting, arson, murder; he was creating terror to bring the tenants under his control. How could Rani tolerate the oppression of the tenants? She was their protector. By her order, a group of club-wielders led by a chieftain named "Mahabir" came to Jagannathpur to protect the tenants.

Mahabir was Mathurbabu's most beloved club-wielder. He was serious, powerful, and kind. Initially, Ramratan's club-wielders retreated a little at Mahabir's arrival, but they ambushed and killed Mahabir from behind. The murder committed by Ramratan could not be tolerated. Janbazar prepared for battle. From twenty-four parganas including Nadia, Burdwan, Hooghly, and other places, the brave soldiers, pikemen, etc., of Rani Ma's army came under the leadership of the Jagannathpur Naib, ready to fight.

On the other side, Ramratan Roy's hired warriors, armed with axes, spears, lances, sticks, swords, etc., stood ready to shed blood. "Victory to the Mother, victory to the Rani" -- this terrifying roar in the name of the Rani shook the sky and the air and stunned the opposition. Rani Ma chose the club-wielders one by one from the estate. The strength and determination of the Rani were still unknown. Now it was time to show it.

The riot never happened. The lives of many people were saved because of the glory of Rani Ma's name. Ramratan's desire had yet to be fulfilled.

Rani Ma, with her power, filed a case in the court against this zamindar. He started forcibly planting indigo in the fields instead of paddy after losing the case. The tenants were now protected by Rani Ma.

The English indigo planters were destroying the rural life by torturing the tenants in Makimpur, which was part of his zamindari. They forced them to plant indigo instead of paddy. It was not easy to get justice from the English courts against the English. Rani Ma found a way to teach these indigo planters a lesson. The most oppressive among the indigo planters in Makimpur was named Donald. Rani Ma's club-wielders beat up Donald and left him half-dead. Seeing no other option, the indigo planters went to court.

The case was dismissed. Rani Ma did not find it convenient to follow the path of justice. The treatment for teaching these indigo planters a lesson was the club. The court could do nothing. When the English sahibs went to the court, Rani Ma ordered her employees to drive all the indigo planters far away from her area. No indigo planters could buy or sell land there.

This was the beginning of Rani Rasmani's continuous "legal struggle" against the English rulers. For the protection of the tenants, the fishermen, and the people, she sought help from the prominent citizens of the city. Among them, Radhakantadev Bahadur saw that first the poor tenant farmers would be ruined, and then the turn of others would come. Then he said, "In this danger, we have only one person -- Rani Ma."

No one else had the courage to speak up. The government did not understand anything but money. Rani knew how to win over the hearts of common people with her intellect and wisdom. Rani Ma leased the part of the Ganges from Ghusuri in Howrah to Metiabruz from the then judge Mr. Samner for ten thousand rupees. The English government understood nothing but money. Rani had paid them the money. She ordered her employees to enclose the part of the Ganges taken on lease on both sides with thick iron chains. This stopped the movement of ships and boats. Oh ho! That was the real game.

The government was alerted by the obstruction of navigation, which was causing inconvenience to traders.

A notice came from the government office, "Why has the waterway been blocked? Show cause. Why should the damage to the movement of goods not be considered a crime?"

Fearless Rani Ma was fighting against the mighty English government. She skillfully replied, "I have leased the Ganges by paying the prescribed tax, so legally I can block this route. It is causing inconvenience to my tenants. If ships keep moving constantly in this part, and there is a port in Kolkata, fish will not stay here due to the sound of the ships in the Ganges. I cannot allow ships to come here."

No answer came out of the government's head to this irrefutable argument. Then a compromise was reached. Rani Ma said, "If the government allowed my tenants to fish without paying any tax, I would open the route again."

Caught in a clever trap, the government was forced to withdraw the "water tax." The Ganges was freed from the blockage. The fishermen got the right to fish tax-free, which is still in effect today. The whole of Bengal praised Rani Ma. A song was on everyone's lips __

Blessed is Rani Rasmani, a woman among women.

You have kept a good name in Bengal.

Seeing the suffering of the poor, you cried.

You saved lives by giving away your money for others.

What a name you have made as a woman.

Your name is sung day and night in every home.

The Durga Puja was celebrated with great pomp at the magnificent seven-storied house of Janbazar. Rajchandra had built the road from Janbazar to Babughat. On the morning of Saptami, priests were taking the নবপত্রিকা (collection of nine plants) for bathing with drums and fanfare from Janbazar's property. The sleep of an Englishman in a house on the road was disturbed.

He shouted, "Stop it, stop it." Rani Ma was not at all disturbed by this protest. She ordered, "What is happening will happen, and it will happen even more." For three days, there was a loud uproar on that road early in the morning. The music was even louder. The insulted Englishman filed a case against Rasmani. Rani Ma's lawyer showed the deed approved by the Garrison Officer in court. He said that the army had given permission to build the road for Rajchandra Das. Rani Rasmani sent a message, "It is my own road, I will do whatever I want. If anyone obstructs it, I will demolish the road at twice the cost of building it."

Despite the government order, Rani Ma did not remove the blockage. Instead, she strongly fenced off both sides of the entire road from Janbazar House to Babughat with thick teak wood. Connection to other roads was cut off. The government understood that this was not an ordinary woman and that it was not possible to win against her. So they sent an invitation. The government realized that it would have to treat this woman with respect. This woman was worth a hundred on her own. They again opened the fence for public use while keeping their own property intact. Then Rani Babughat's road and its fame became her own. No sooner had she requested than the fine was also returned. The government came to know again and again that it would have to treat Rasmani with respect. A rhyme about her became popular:

"The eight-horse carriage runs Rani Rasmani,

Could not block the road, the Company"

1857. The time of the Sepoy Mutiny. The English were shaken. A big shock. Rani Ma understood politics, society, and diplomacy very well. She knew that it would not be easy to dislodge the English from this country. They had a strong political sense. On March 29, 1857, at the Barrackpore cantonment, Sepoy Mangal Pandey rebelled.

It was Rani Ma's firm belief that the English would not leave India due to this scattered and religiously confusing rebellion. Rani Ma's advisors asked her to sell all the Company's papers and shares as the British company's lifespan was coming to an end.

Rani Ma's vision of the future came true. The rebellion subsided. The rule of the Company came to an end. In 1858, Queen Victoria of England took over the administration of India. Strict British rule was established.

Military garrisons were established in different places across the country. A military barracks was established near Rani Ma's house in Free School Street. The number of soldiers there was 200/250. Most of them were uneducated, fierce, and disobedient. The only commander over them was the drunken Major Atkinson. Since they had nothing to do after the rebellion subsided, what would these two hundred and fifty soldiers do? In a drunken state, they would oppress passersby on the public roads and sometimes forcefully enter shops, houses, and homes and loot freely.

One evening, some drunken soldiers were passing by Rani Ma's house on Janbazar Road. The guards tried to stop them from entering the house without any reason. One of the soldiers was injured in the skirmish.

Now, they returned with 50/60 enraged soldiers. Swords were shining in their hands. A massacre began. The guards tried to resist, but were forced to retreat due to the sword attacks. Two of them lost their lives. No men were present in the house at that time.

On hearing the news of the Goras' rampage, Rani Ma sent the women of the house to Mannababu's house. Now, she herself took on the form of a fierce warrior. She stood in front of the door of the family deity Raghunathji's temple, with a shining sword in her hand. She would sacrifice her life to protect the deity.

On the other side, vandalism and looting continued. Gobinda, a loyal servant, was injured in the waist by a sword. He lost consciousness. The trusted soldiers were forced to retreat under the pressure of the Rani's terrifying form and her sword. The soldiers also cut the house's pet birds into pieces.

After this, around ten o'clock at night, Mathuramohan returned home from the Kalinga market with the local police inspector after hearing about this terrible incident. Commanded by the Commanding Officer, everything became calm.

Government compensation was paid.

So many good deeds in one lifetime! Social ill of polygamy was prevalent. Rani Ma was in favor of this movement to stop the Kulin system. At that time, in the then legislative society, a petition was submitted to stop polygamy.

The list of her public welfare works is long. For example, building a wide road from the other side of the Subarnarekha River to Puri, constructing a bathing ghat in her birthplace in the village of Bahirgachi, providing rest houses for pilgrims on their way to the Ganges at Nimtala crematorium and Kalighat, and setting up a market in Buriganga. Giving huge amounts of money.

The best of all works was her establishment of the temple.

She became fixed on this world by building a palace-like ghat on the banks of the Ganges. And many ghats were built. digging the "Kala Canal" to connect the Madhumati River with the Ganges. Providing financial assistance to various colleges.

In that garden, the Bengal Renaissance would take place. From that place, the flow of movements would spread all over the world. Her name would shine brightly in the history of time. In 1847, she decided to go to Kashi. A fleet of boats was prepared. She received a divine command. "Kashi is everywhere, so why go to Kashi? Install the deity on the banks of the Ganges."

At that time, there was a severe famine and epidemic in Bengal. Rani Rasmani vowed that all the food items stored in the barges would be distributed among the poor.

Rani Rasmani entrusted her son-in-law Mathuramohan with the selection of the land and the construction of the temple.

The land was found on the banks of the Bhagirathi in Dakshineswar. East India Company's gunpowder depot, James Hastie's garden to the south, James Hastie Sahib's house to the east, the land of Kashinath Choudhuris to the south of the gunpowder depot, the Ganges to the west, the government's barudkhana to the north. This vast garden was previously owned by John Hastie. It was called "Sahebhan Bagicha." He lived in a bungalow. His intention was to build a jute mill here.

Rani Ma bought this property from them. Muslim burial grounds, Gazipir's shrine, ponds, and mango orchards were all included in Rani Ma's property. This large property with "Kurmapristhakriti" (turtle-shaped) shape was bought on September 6, 1847. According to the Shastras, it was the best place to establish a Shakti temple.

The responsibility for building the temple was given to the well-known British contracting firm "Mackintosh and Burn." It took 7/8 years to build the temple. The construction was completed in 1854. There were many scriptural obstacles.

The temple was inaugurated with great pomp on May 31, 1855 (18th Jaishtha 1262 Bengali Era, the day of Snan Yatra).

Over a hundred thousand Brahmins had come from all over the country including Navadwip, Bhatpara, Mulajore, Noakhali, Vikrampur, Chittagong, Kashi, Puri, Pune, Madras, Kannauj, and Mithila to participate in the festival.

According to the Hindu scriptures of that time, the deity could not be offered Annabhog (cooked rice offering) because Rasmani was not a Brahmin.

Why would the divinely inspired Rani Mata accept this rule? She had been observing vows since the day the temple construction began. She had led the life of an ascetic. She had bound herself to strict rules and regulations. At the last moment, Pandit Ramkumar of Kamarpukur found a way to resolve the issue. He offered Annabhog. Rani Rasmani joyfully and enthusiastically performed "Annadan Yajna" (feeding of the masses). Such arrangements had never been heard of before, let alone seen. For example, Dadhi-Puskarini (pond of curd), Payes-Samudra (sea of rice pudding), Khir-Hrida (lake of condensed milk), Dugdha-Sagara (ocean of milk), Taila-Sarobar (lake of oil), Ghrita-Kupa (well of ghee), Luchi-Pahar (mountain of fried bread), Mistananna-Stupa (heap of sweets), Kadalipatra-Rashi (pile of banana leaves), Mrinmayapatra-Stupa (heap of earthen pots). In those days, 9 lakh rupees were spent.

From the day before the temple's inauguration, various festivals began __ Jatra, Kalikirtan, Bhagavatpath, Ramayan Gaan. Sri Ramakrishna was present with his elder brother Ramkumar on the day of the festival. Later, while describing the night of the festival, he had said that it was a non-stop celebration. The entire temple was as bright as day with countless lights. It felt as if Rani had raised a silver mountain.

The revolutionary era of Sri Ramakrishna would begin in the courtyard of this temple and the surrounding garden in a few days. As many faiths, so many paths. A group of enlightened upper-class youths inspired by English education would come to this temple in response to the call of the mystic, the Avatar Paramhansa. Swami Vivekananda would emerge from this place of penance. He would establish Indian religion in the court of the world. A new era began with his Sri Ramakrishna. This temple would overshadow all religious activities and become an unquenchable flame. Dakshineswar, Maa Kali, Rani Rasmani, Mathuramohan, Sri Ramakrishna were an amazing combination. What would not happen in this Dakshineswar temple! One of the biographers of Ranimata, the revered Bankim Chandra Sen, wrote, "Dakshineswar is a perpetual pilgrimage, a busy pilgrimage, it has become the greatest pilgrimage not only of India but of the world."

"Rani Ma came as the embodiment of the Yuga Devi, and Thakur came as the embodiment of the Yugavatar, and both manifested the sweetness of the divine Leela in the Sri Mandir of Dakshineswar..."

Thakur used to say that Rani Ma was the universal mother Jagadamba. She had come to earth only to spread her Leela.

Gopinath Das, on behalf of the Dakshineswar Debottar Estate, writes, "Ever since the establishment of the Dakshineswar temple, Rani spent her days especially with religious activities and worship. Most of the time she would spend at the Dakshineswar temple, sometimes alone and sometimes with her family. She would not stay at home. After the passing away of her elder brother (he had handled the responsibility of the priest in this new temple for only eleven months), Ramkumar would listen to religious discourses sitting near Rani Ma, even though he was a priest. Many times, Rani Ma would respect and admire Ramkrishna for his extraordinary religious feelings and would listen to bhajans and other religious songs from the mouth of Ramkrishna. For his immense religious sentiments, she would respect him a lot. She would say, "Rani Ma is the incarnation of the goddess herself."

A life full of hustle and bustle, a life completely dedicated to work, religion, service, and public welfare. Now it was time to leave. Everything could not be seen. Dakshineswar remained, Maa Bhabatarini remained with her Sri Ramakrishna, the silver chariot remained, everything would be as it was during Dol Durgotsav. Rani Rasmani would not be there. On February 19, 1861 (9th Falgun 1267 Bengali Era), she breathed her last in the Kalighat garden house, "Kalipada Abhilashi Sriramani Dasi".

This was her seal __ কালিপদ অভিলাসী শ্রীরাষমণী দাসি .
