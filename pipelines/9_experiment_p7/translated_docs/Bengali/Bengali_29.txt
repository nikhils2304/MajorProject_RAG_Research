You learned about force in grades six and seven. You have seen that we cannot do anything without applying force. To move a stationary object, to change the direction of motion of a moving object, to make that motion faster or slower, we have to apply force. Is that all? Force is also needed to change the shape of an object. It is not possible to compress a spring to make it smaller or extend it to make it longer without applying force. It is surprising to think how many forces work in so many places within our body! When we stand, it is because of the forces acting in various places within the muscles and bones of our entire body.

You will enjoy many more such things. You will remember the three formulas that scientist Isaac Newton told us about force and motion. You can stand because force is working somewhere. If you discuss among yourselves the various events happening around you, you will see how force is acting, and you will have fun thinking about it.

You also learned about it in seventh grade. Let's see once again what we have learned from those formulas.

From the first formula, we learn that to understand whether a force is acting on an object, we have to see if the object's velocity is changing. If the object's velocity does not change or if the object is stopped, it means that no force is acting on the object, or the sum of the forces acting on the object is zero.

From the second formula, we learned that the stronger the force applied on an object, the greater the change in the object's velocity per second, meaning the greater the acceleration. If the force is doubled, the acceleration produced by that force will also be doubled.

From the third formula, we understood that when an object applies a force on another object, the second object also applies an equal force in the opposite direction on the first object. If one of these two forces is called action, then the other force is called reaction.

Now the question is, how will this force be measured? How will we know which force is greater and which is smaller?

In grades six and seven, you learned that to know the amount of force, you have to calculate what happened due to the effect of that force. If you push a book on a table once with your hand and once your friend pushes it. The push that creates more velocity in the stopped book, definitely created more acceleration while applying the force. So, definitely more force has been applied to the book in that push. The amount of force applied on an object of a specific mass is calculated by looking at the effect of the force, i.e., by measuring the acceleration. We have also got an equation in this regard from Newton's formula.

Force = Mass of the object * Acceleration produced in the object due to the force
[i.e., F = ma (where F = force, m = mass, and a = acceleration)]

The amount of force that, when applied on an object of one kilogram mass, creates an acceleration of one meter/second², is called one Newton force. This one Newton is the unit of measuring force in SI units. Newton is denoted by N. When you hold a one-kilogram weight in your hand, the weight applies a force of approximately 9.8 Newtons on your hand.

We saw that if we have to measure force using the equation given by Isaac Newton, we need to know the value of acceleration.

But the weight held in hand or your hand is both stationary, there is no acceleration. How to measure force in this case? Or suppose, you have tied a brick to a string (or rope) and hung it. And you are holding the other end of the string. You see that the brick is stationary, it has no acceleration. Yet you can feel the brick pulling the string downwards while holding it. Your hand is being pulled. How will force be measured in this case?

The earth's pull is downwards on both the weight held in hand or the brick hanging from a string. This pull is what we call the gravitational force or the weight of the object. In sixth grade, you learned from Newton's formula that acceleration will be created if force is applied.

Then think about why the weight or the brick is stationary? Why has no acceleration been created despite the application of gravitational force? Is someone else then applying an equal force on them in the opposite direction of the earth's pull?

You are right. In the case of the weight, your hand, and in the case of the brick, the string is applying this opposite force. When the weight or the brick wants to go downwards due to the earth's gravitational pull, the weight applies a force on the hand, and the brick applies a force on the string, downwards. As a result, according to Newton's third formula, the hand also applies an equal force on the weight from bottom to top, and the string also applies an equal force on the brick from bottom to top. Therefore, because two equal forces are acting in opposite directions, the total force on the brick or weight has become zero. As a result, no acceleration is created.

Even though no acceleration is created, two equal forces are working in opposite directions. What is the way to measure these two forces?

Let's learn about an experiment.

A spring is taken. It is hung from a hook. Now if a heavy weight or a piece of stone is hung from the other end of the spring, it will be seen that the spring has become a little longer. What will happen if, instead of the previous weight, a weight twice as heavy or something else is hung? Will the spring become longer than before? It can definitely be seen that the amount of expansion of the spring is also greater when a heavier object is hung.

If you look closely, you will see that the expansion of the spring also doubles when an object of double the mass is hung.

The weight or heavy stone piece hanging from the spring remains stationary, it has no acceleration. But due to the force applied by that hanging object on the spring, the length of the spring increases.

If the force applied on the spring is greater, the amount of expansion of the spring is also greater - we can use this phenomenon to measure force.

A scale and a hanging spring are taken side by side in the picture on the side. A pointer or indicator is attached to the hanging end of the spring as shown in the picture. Now, by hanging different weights from the hanging end of the spring, it is being seen which mark on the scale the pointer or indicator stays at. In this way, different readings of the indicator are possible for different weights. When no weight is hung, the place of the spring is named zero. When a weight of 1 kg mass is hung, the reading where it comes down is named 1 kg or 9.8 Newtons. That is, from the position of the indicator, it has to be understood that 1 kg or 9.8 Newtons of force has been applied on the spring. In this way, by looking at the position of the spring's indicator for objects of different masses like 2 kg, 3 kg, etc., how much force has been applied on the spring can be measured. Using this expansion of the spring due to the application of force, a force-measuring instrument called a spring balance has been created.

Suppose, you pulled the spring balance from both sides and the spring balance pointer showed a reading of 3 kg. What does this mean? This means that the amount of force with which you have pulled the spring balance on each side, is equal to the force with which the earth pulls an object of 3 kg mass. Friction and its measurement

You have learned about frictional force in grades six and seven. Here we will see how the frictional force is calculated.

A box is placed on a table. And you are pulling the box with the help of a spring balance. By pulling with the spring balance, you can measure how strongly you are pulling. Suppose, the pointer of the spring balance is at the 1 kg or 9.8 Newton mark. That is, you are pulling the box to the right with a force of 9.8 Newtons. If the box remains stationary despite this pull and does not move to the right, then according to Newton's first formula, what conclusion can you come to? Someone must have applied a force of 9.8 Newtons on the box to the left - as a result of which the total force on the box has become zero. Now the question is, who applied this 9.8 Newton force to the left?

Now if you place that box on another table, the surface of which is more smooth and slippery, then you may see that the box is moving to the right when pulled with that 9.8 Newton force. From this, it is understood that, in the case of the first table, the upper surface of the table itself had applied that force. When the table was changed and the box was placed on the slippery surface, the surface of the new table could not apply that same amount of force (9.8 Newtons). Therefore, it was understood that as soon as the box is pulled, a force is created at the contact of the bottom surface of the box and the upper surface of the table. As soon as the box is pulled, that force starts acting in the opposite direction of the pull and tries to prevent the box from moving. In the first case, the value of that force and the value of the pull are equal, 9.8 Newtons. So, in the first case, the sum of the two forces has become zero. And in the second case, the value of that force is less than the pull. As a result, the box has moved in the direction of the pull. This force created at the contact of two surfaces that is created against motion or the attempt to create motion is called frictional force.

Now tell me, what is the value of that frictional force in the first case?

You are right. The value of the frictional force in the first case is 9.8 Newtons, i.e., equal to the force with which it has been pulled to the right. Even when pulled parallel to a surface, if an object remains stationary on that surface, only then can we say that the value of the frictional force is equal to the value of the pull. If the value of the pull can be known with the help of a spring balance, then the value of the frictional force can also be known.

But if the object starts moving due to the pull, then the value of the frictional force cannot be determined in this way by just looking at the reading of the spring balance. Although frictional force acts in that case as well.

When an object remains stationary on a surface despite being pulled by applying force, then the frictional force that acts is called static friction. And when the object starts moving due to the pull, then the frictional force that acts is called kinetic friction.

Let's write down what we have learned about frictional force.

Frictional force

Frictional force is active when an object is tried to be moved or when the object is moving over a surface.

Always acts parallel to the two surfaces in contact.

The frictional force that acts when an object remains stationary on a surface despite the application of push, pull or any kind of force like shove, hit, etc. is called static friction.

Static friction has a maximum value.

This maximum value remains the same for a specific object, on a specific surface.

The greater the action-reaction force acting vertically between two surfaces, the greater the maximum value of the static frictional force acting parallel to that surface.

Density

Take two plastic bottles of the same size. Now fill one bottle with water. Fill the other bottle with heavily salted water.

See which one is heavier.

The two bottles are exactly the same in every way in their empty state. The amount of space inside the two bottles is the same. Then why did one become heavier than the other?

The heavily salted water is definitely heavier than regular water. That is why a bottle full of heavily salted water will be heavier than the same size bottle full of regular water.

Similarly, a spoonful of heavily salted water will definitely be heavier than a spoonful of regular water. In the same way, a bowl of glycerin will also be heavier than a bowl of regular water.

Therefore, the weight of the same volume of heavily salted water is more than that of regular water. So, it can definitely be said that the weight of the same volume of heavily salted water is more than the weight of the same volume of regular water. The weight of the same volume of heavily salted water is more than the weight of the same volume of regular water. We call this volume “unit volume” whose value is one (1). For example, 1 liter, 1 cubic centimeter, 1 gallon. They are all unit volumes. The weight of unit volume of an object is called the density of that object.

Therefore, the density of heavily salted water is more than the density of regular water. That is why the weight of one bottle of heavily salted water is more than the weight of one bottle of regular water of the same size, as a result of which the bottle of heavily salted water is heavier.

Suppose, the volume of the bottle is 1 liter. Before filling it with water, the empty bottle was hung on a spring balance and the pointer of the spring balance did not move down. Therefore, it can be assumed that the weight of the bottle is so less that it can be assumed to be zero. Now, after filling it with water, the bottle was hung from the spring balance again. This time the pointer of the spring balance came down to the 1 kg mark. The weight of 1 liter volume of water was found to be 1 kg, i.e., the density of water is 1 kg/liter. Now, if we use the units gram for measuring weight and cubic centimeter for measuring volume, then what will be the density of water?

1 liter of water = 1000 cubic centimeters of water.

1 kg of water = 1000 grams of water.

Therefore, we can write,

The weight of 1000 cubic centimeters of water is 1000 grams.

The weight of 1 cubic centimeter of water = 1000/1000 gram.

Therefore, density = Weight of water / Volume of water = 1000 grams / 1000 cubic centimeters = 1 gram/cubic centimeter.

Liquid Pressure

You learned about the pressure of liquids in the sixth grade. You also learned that the amount of force acting on a unit area is called pressure.

A block of 10 kg weight is placed on the table on the side. With how much force is the earth pulling the block downwards?

The earth pulls an object of 1 kg weight with a force of 9.8 Newtons. So, the earth is pulling an object of 10 kg weight with a force of (9.8 * 10) Newtons or 98 Newtons.

Can you tell why the iron block remains stationary despite being pulled downwards by the earth with a force of 98 Newtons?

Since the earth's pull on the block is downwards, the block pushes downwards on the table. The value of that push is equal to the weight of the block, i.e., 98 Newtons. If we call this push of the block on the table action, then the reaction force given by the table on the block will also be 98 Newtons and its direction will be upwards.

As a result, the sum of the 98 Newton downward force given by the earth on the block and the 98 Newton upward force given by the table is zero, which is why no acceleration has been created in the block. That is why the block remains stationary.

Now if the question is asked what is the amount of pressure created on the table due to the block?

We saw that the block is applying a force of 98 Newtons on the table. Now it needs to be known, over how much area on the table has this force been applied?

If the area of the contact surface of the table and the block is 0.25 square meters, then the force given by the block on the table has been applied over an area of 0.25 square meters.

If the amount of force given over an area of 0.25 square meters is 98 Newtons,

Then, the amount of force on 1 square meter area, i.e., on a unit area = 98 Newtons / 0.25 square meters = 392 Newtons/square meter.

Therefore, the pressure given by the block on the table = Force / Area = 98 Newtons / 0.25 square meters = 392 Newtons/square meter.

Now look at the tub on the side, which contains 10 kg of water. The area of the floor of the tub is 0.25 square meters. Determine the amount of pressure that the water applies on the floor of the tub, i.e., on the bottom of the tub.

The earth is pulling the 10 kg of water downwards with a force of (10 * 9.8) Newtons or 98 Newtons. That is, the weight of 10 kg of water is 98 Newtons. But the water remains stationary. Therefore, the floor of the tub has given an upward force of 98 Newtons on the water. According to Newton's third formula, the water has also given a force of 98 Newtons on the floor of the tub.

As a result, the amount of pressure on the floor of the tub = Force / Area = 98 Newtons / 0.25 square meters = 392 Newtons/square meter.

In the case of a solid, we calculated pressure from the force exerted by the block on the table. In the case of a liquid, we calculated pressure from the force exerted by the water on the bottom of the tub.

The liquid water not only applies force on the bottom of the tub but also applies force on the walls of the tub. If any hole is made in the wall of the tub, then water comes out through it. It can be understood just by seeing this that, in the case of liquids, pressure should also be calculated on the side walls. Now we will try to know more about the pressure of liquids.
