Condensation
If you put ice inside a glass, water droplets form outside the glass. This is the reverse process of evaporation. The event of vapor turning into liquid is called condensation.
Clouds, dew, and fog are formed because of the condensation of water vapor in the atmosphere.
Clouds: Water evaporates from ponds, rivers, lakes, seas, wet soil, and living bodies. This:
Water vapor-filled air is lighter than cool and less water vapor-filled air.
That's why warm and moist air rises. As the height increases, the pressure decreases, so the particles of that air increase the distance between themselves and the air cools.
Then the water vapor present in the air condenses around dust particles and other particles floating in the air and floats in the air as water droplets. This is what we call clouds.

Air saturated with water vapor: Take some water in a tea cup and add a little salt. Stir with a spoon. After a while, you will see that the solid grains of salt are no longer visible. That is, the salt has dissolved in the water. If you continue to dissolve salt in this way, there will surely come a time when the amount of salt that can be dissolved in that amount of water will be dissolved. That solution is then called a saturated solution of salt. If you add more salt, it will settle at the bottom of the solution. But if you add some water to that solution again, that salt will dissolve back into the solution. Or if you increase the temperature of the solution, that salt will also dissolve in the solution. Now suppose you are given a saturated salt solution at a temperature of 50°C. If you gradually decrease the temperature of that solution, what will happen? Surely some salt will settle.

Similarly, if a specific amount of air at a specific temperature is filled with the most amount of water vapor that it can hold, then that air is said to be saturated with water vapor.

Fog: Fog is seen in the morning in winter. As the day progresses, the air in a large area nearby gradually cools and becomes saturated with water vapor. That water vapor condenses and accumulates as water droplets on dust particles floating in the air and floats. This is called fog.

So why does the fog disappear as the day progresses? Why does smog form in big cities or industrial areas?

Can you guess why?

Dew: Many of you have seen dew accumulate on leaves or blades of grass when you wake up in the morning in winter. Dew does not fall in the evening, but during the late night or early morning.
During the day, objects near the earth's surface get heated by the sun's heat. As a result, the air layer adjacent to those objects also gets heated. After sunset,
the earth's surface starts to cool by releasing heat. Then the air layer adjacent to the earth's surface also slowly cools. As the temperature decreases, the air adjacent to the earth's surface becomes saturated with water vapor. What will happen if the temperature decreases further? Just as salt settled from the water, water vapor will separate from the air as water droplets and form dew. It takes quite some time for this suitable condition to form for dew to fall. That is why dew does not fall in the evening, but dew falls late at night.
