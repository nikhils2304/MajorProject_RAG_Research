Unit 5

Migration

Learning Objectives

*   Understand the concepts of migration.
*   Know the prevalence of migration in India and Tamil Nadu.
*   Analyze and understand the reasons for migration.

Introduction

Migration based on residence is called the conceptual pattern of migration. Population changes in all types of residential areas, whether village or town, due to births, deaths, and migration. Births and deaths are measurable in these three components of population change. At the same time, migration creates more difficulties in calculation and definition. It is not easy to define which is the movement for migration as almost everyone keeps moving from one place to another.

In the Indian population census, migration is generally calculated on only two bases.

(i) Based on place of birth -

If the place of residence at the time of the census is different from the place of birth, it is called lifetime migration.

(ii) Based on residence -

The place of residence at the time of the census is called the concept of migration if the place of last residence is different.

5.1 Prevalence of Migration in India and Tamil Nadu

According to the 2011 population census, 45 crore people out of India's total population of 121 crore were counted as migrants based on residence. Similarly, according to the 2011 census, 3.13 crore people out of the total population of 7.2 crore in Tamil Nadu were counted as migrants. That is, while the country's migration was 37%, Tamil Nadu's migration was at its peak of 43%.

Generally, migration in the Indian population census is associated with urban areas. However, the prevalence of migration in India and Tamil Nadu is higher in rural areas than in urban areas. In 2011, 37% of the rural population in India was counted as migrants, while the number of migrants in urban India was 27%. In Tamil Nadu, the 2011 population census reveals that 41% are migrants in rural areas and 35% are migrants in urban areas. That is, the movement of people is higher in rural areas than in urban areas.

Furthermore, migration is mostly associated only with men than women. However, when analyzing the data, a very high percentage of migration is seen only in women than men. In 2011, 53% of the total number of migrants were women and 23% were men. A similar situation is seen in Tamil Nadu during the same period. That is, 52% are women migrants and 35% are men migrants.

Why is there a high number of female migrants? In India, 70% of women and in Tamil Nadu, 51% of women mention marriage as the reason for their migration. Marriage and related relocation in both India and Tamil Nadu is a major factor for the high number of female migrants. Work and employment serve as a driving force for migration among men. In 2011 in India, 28% of men and in Tamil Nadu, 26% mention work as the main reason for migration.

Overall, according to the 2011 census, two out of five people in Tamil Nadu are migrants. Migration is very high in rural areas and among women.

Tamil Nadu has a history of migration. People from Tamil Nadu have migrated to many countries for trade, commerce, employment, and various other reasons. During colonialism, workers migrated to other colonial countries in search of work and income. In recent times, workers from Tamil Nadu have migrated to Gulf countries, the United States, and Australia in large numbers. A study conducted by volunteers in 2015 to understand the level, nature, and methods of migration in Tamil Nadu reveals the following details to us.

*   65% of the total migrants in Tamil Nadu have migrated from Tamil Nadu to foreign countries, and 35% have migrated within our country.
*   Chennai district ranks first in the record of the highest number of expatriates. Following Chennai, Coimbatore, Ramanathapuram, and Trichy districts have captured the next places.
*   Cuddalore, Karur, Tiruvannamalai, Vellore, Namakkal, Salem, Dindigul, Krishnagiri, Nilgiri, and Dharmapuri districts have recorded the lowest number of expatriates.

This study also provides us with several information about the destination and gender division of migrants in Tamil Nadu.

*   20% of those who migrate abroad have chosen Singapore. 18% have migrated to the United Arab Emirates, 16% to Saudi Arabia, and 13% to the United States.
*   Malaysia, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Australia, and England have been mentioned as destination countries for migrants in the 2015 study.
*   15% of international migrants are women and 85% are men.

The answer to the question about the educational qualification of the migrants from Tamil Nadu in 2015 is that 7% are illiterate, 30% have completed 10th standard, 10% have completed up to 12th standard, 15% are professionally trained, 11% have a bachelor's degree, 12% have a professional degree, and 11% are postgraduates.

This study also reveals the nature of the work that the migrants undertake. They are involved in highly skilled jobs, ordinary jobs, and medium-level jobs.

5.2 Factors for Migration

The nature of migration in a society and its extent is determined only by considering the progress that the society has made. The forms of migration are determined only by the rate of growth caused by the economy and industries and the level of its efficiency.

Even though agriculture and industry in India and Tamil Nadu have grown in recent years, this growth has not been in a way to equalize the inequalities in the distribution of property and income. Furthermore, poverty remains a persistent problem.

The economic, social, and spatial inequalities created by developmental trends determine the migrations that occur due to development.

Therefore, the migration movement consists of various types of internal movements. For example, if we take the rural-urban migration movement, it has internal aspects such as reasons for rural poor and rich, migration method, and consequences that may occur due to migration. Poor people migrate from the distressful situation in rural areas to restore their livelihoods. At the same time, people with some facilities migrate not to recover from their distress or for livelihood but to improve their living conditions.

When viewed based on area, whether it is urban or rural, those who have a tendency to accumulate in the developed areas are migrants.

The pattern of migration is a complex one including several movements.

*   From rural to rural, from rural to urban, from urban to rural, and from urban to urban migrations.
*   Short, medium, and long distance migrations.
*   Long-term permanent migration and short-term cyclical migrations.

*   The Mexico - United States migration route is the largest migration route in 2010.

*   The Arctic Tern is the bird that migrates the longest distance in the world.

The developmental trend has created area-based inequality through developmental regions. In developing economies such as India,

Each migration movement contains different types of class-based immigrants. Each migration has its own reasons. The extent and nature of migration depend on the following:

*   The pressure and desires experienced by people at the place where migration starts.
*   The restrictions imposed on the movement of people in the place of origin.
*   The opportunities available at the destination and information about them.
*   The cost of immigration.

5.3 Migration Policies

The basics for creating policies to address migration-related issues in developing countries like India are:

*   Ways to reduce the extent of migration: High levels of migration reflect the poverty and insecurity found in rural areas. Therefore, special attention should be given to villages. Rural development schemes that reduce poverty and insecurity will help reduce high levels of migration.
*   Redirection of Migration Flows: Policies that change the direction of migration converging towards metropolitan areas are desirable policy decisions. Spatial designs that spread urbanization are appropriate strategies to reduce inequalities caused by area-based migration.

*   The nature and extent of migration in a society is determined only by considering the progress that the society has made.
*   Poor people migrate for their livelihood, and people with facilities migrate to improve their standard of living.
