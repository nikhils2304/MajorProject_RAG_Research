4.2.1 Unitary Government and its features
Unitary Government is a type of government where sovereignty is vested only in one institution and it governs as one unit. The central government is very powerful. Administrative systems will execute only the powers entrusted by the central government. England, France, Japan and Sri Lanka are examples of the Unitary system of government.

The term government is derived from the old French word governer (governer) and the Latin word gubernare, which means to control, govern, guide and rule.

In the Unitary system, all powers are centralized in one place. But, in the Federal system, the power is shared between the central government and its units. There may be decentralization of power in the unitary system of government. But it should not be considered as a Federal system.

Merits of Unitary Government

• Suitable for small countries
• No conflicts between powers and responsibilities
• The Unitary system is immediately effective and acts quickly.
• The Unitary system is less expensive
• Constitutional amendments can be easily made.
• Includes unity, uniform law, policy and administration.
