Learning Objectives:

*   To understand the history of the Earth's origins.
*   To understand the evolution of the human race.
*   To learn about the protohistoric history of Tamil Nadu up to the Iron Age.

Introduction:

Palaeoanthropologists and Archaeologists are digging into the soil and rock strata of the Earth and collecting evidence about human ancestors. All the accumulated knowledge that we have today did not emerge suddenly. The foundation for this modern life was laid in the Palaeolithic period and continuously shaped by the intelligence of our ancestors.

The evolution of human history and the stages of Palaeolithic people's human and creative capabilities are being scientifically calculated. Through the evidence collected, people are trying to understand the origins and the periods of human history.

Paleoanthropology:

Study and understanding of human ancestors' physical structure and their evolutionary development through fossil evidence.

Archaeology:

The study of the human past through analysis and interpretation of material culture.

1.1. The Origin of the Earth and Geological Periods

It is impossible to separate human history from the history of the Earth. Geological, archaeological, and biological records are embedded in the Earth's layers. These are very important for understanding the history of the Earth and various living organisms. In these layers, the bones of human ancestors are buried as fossils.

The Earth is estimated to have formed about 4.54 billion years ago. Over time, the conditions for the emergence of life gradually developed. Following the emergence of plants and animals, the foundation for the emergence of human life was laid. Geological researchers divide the long geological history of the Earth into Era, Period, and Epoch.

1.2. The Study of the Origin of the World and Past Human History

Speculative Phase

Human beings are the only beings that understand the world and the universe, attempt to gather information, and explain the world scientifically. Humans began to think and question nature, the living beings and the world around them. Initially, they regarded nature as a god. They created and worshiped their own understandings of natural forces such as the sun and the moon. Some of these were not scientific. Ancient writings and religious texts reveal the lack of scientific knowledge about the origin of the world.

BC/BCE - Before Common Era

The scientific foundation for geological, biological, and archaeological studies. Writing past history began in the Greek era. Herodotus of Greece (484–425 BCE) is considered the father of history, as his writings are human-centered and rational. The scientific studies of the origin of humans became possible due to the following factors:

*   Increased interest in collecting antiquities and the opening of museums after the European Renaissance.
*   Development of stratigraphic and geological concepts.
*   Charles Darwin's theory of biological evolution.
*   Discovery of human and animal fossils, stone tools, and artifacts of ancient civilizations.
*   The ability to read early scripts.

Stratigraphy - The study of the origin, nature, and relationship of rock and soil layers formed by natural and cultural activities.

The world's oldest museum - Ennigaldi-Nanna's museum was established in Mesopotamia in 530 BCE. Princess Ennigaldi was the daughter of Nabonidus, the last king of Neo-Babylon. The Capitoline Museum, established in Italy in 1471 CE, is probably the oldest museum still functioning today. The Ashmolean Museum at the University of Oxford in England is the world's oldest university museum. It was established in 1677 CE.

The biological evolution theories of Herbert Spencer (1820–1903 CE) and Charles Darwin's (1809–1882 CE) concepts of natural selection and adaptation (survival of the fittest) contributed to understanding the origin of humans scientifically. Charles Darwin published "The Origin of Species" in 1859 and "The Descent of Man" in 1871.

Natural Selection – The process by which organisms better adapted to their environment tend to survive and produce more offspring.

Survival of the Fittest - a species showing greater genetic variation in its offspring to ensure survival in the next generation

Fossils - Remains, prints, or traces of animals and plants that lived in the past preserved in a petrified form. The study of fossils is called Paleontology. Mineralization is the process by which animal bones are preserved.

Stone Age – the early period of human history when tools and weapons were made of stone

Bronze Age – the period when metalworking (extracting metal from ore) developed to produce bronze tools and objects.

Iron Age – the period when tools were made from iron extracted and smelted.

From the nineteenth century, scholars have been studying the origins of Palaeolithic humanity and ancient civilizations by using scientific methods and conducting thorough research. The theoretical ideas that have been formed today owe much to this. Today, the theory of human evolution (gradual development) is generally accepted.

1.3. Palaeolithic Era: From Australopithecus to Homo erectus to the Development of Homo sapiens

Who are we? What is the name of our race? We are Homo Sapiens, belonging to the modern human race living in this final historical era.

The evolution and migration of humans. Along with humans, chimpanzees, gorillas, and orangutans are classified as Great Apes. Among these, chimpanzees are genetically very close to humans.

A genetic study of chimpanzees' DNA reveals that their characteristics are 98% similar to those of humans!

Human ancestors are called Hominins, who first appeared in Africa. Later, they evolved and spread to other parts of the world, a theory accepted by scholars. This hominin species emerged about 7 to 5 million years ago. The earliest species of this group, Australopithecus, has skeletal evidence discovered in Africa.

Paleolithic Cultures

Lucy

Fossil evidence of human ancestors has been found in several paleoarcheological sites in the Great Rift Valley of Africa.

Human bones are found in Olduvai Gorge, Africa.

This valley stretches about 6,400 km from northern Syria to central Mozambique in East Africa. This geological structure is visible from space. Also, many prehistoric sites are found in East Africa.

Based on anatomy, human ancestors are divided into different species.

Hominid: All modern and extinct great ape species are called Hominids. This includes humans.

Hominin refers to the zoological tribe of human ancestors and related modern humans (Homo Sapiens). This includes Neanderthals, Homo Erectus, Homo Habilis, Australopithecus, etc. In this tribe, only the human race continues to live. This species walks upright on two legs. This species has a larger brain. They use tools. Some Australopithecines can communicate. Gorillas are not included in this tribe.

Homo Habilis, who lived in Africa about 2.6 million years ago, were the first human ancestors to make tools. About 2 million years ago, Homo erectus/ergaster emerged. This species made handaxes. Between about 2 and 1 million years ago, this species spread to various parts of Africa and Asia.

Anatomically, modern humans (Homo sapiens, the wise human) emerged in Africa about 300,000 years ago. It is believed that these modern humans migrated and spread to various parts of the world about 60,000 years ago.

Early Stone Tools

Early stone tools made by human ancestors have been found at Lokalelei in Kenya. These are 3.3 million years old. Oldowan tools have been found in the Olduvai Gorge in Africa. These are 2 to 2.6 million years old.

Human ancestors (Australopithecines) used hammerstones, and also made and used stone flakes called "flakes" as tools. These tools were used to cut and prepare food.

It is unlikely that human ancestors had a language ability like what we have today. Perhaps they used some sounds or words. Mostly they may have used sign language.

They were intelligent enough to select stones for making tools, break and shape rocks with hammerstones, design tools, cut, split, and prepare food. They hunted animals and ate the meat left behind by animals. They collected and ate plant foods such as tubers, seeds, and fruits.

The Chopper tool found at Lokalalei in Kenya, 3.3 million years old

Lower Paleolithic Culture

The culture of Homo habilis and Homo erectus is referred to as the Lower Paleolithic culture.

They designed handaxes and many other types of tools by chipping large stones into flakes. These tools have been found in the continents of Africa, Asia, and Europe. These are estimated to be about 1.8 million years old. They made various tools, including handaxes and choppers, for their living needs. These tools are called bifacial tools. They have a symmetrical shape. Also, they express the intellectual capacity of our human ancestors. This culture is called the Lower Paleolithic culture. Handaxe tools are called Acheulian tools. These tools were continuously in use from 250,000 to 60,000 BC.

Acheulian - This type of stone (‘Lith’) technology: These handaxes were first found in Saint Acheul in France. Hence, they are called Acheulian tools.

Bi-faces - These tools were named so because they were carved on both sides.

Survival Needs of Palaeolithic People

Food and water were the primary needs of Paleolithic people. They may not have had the sophisticated tools that we have today.

During the Oldowan period in Kenya, 2.3 million years ago, the Chopper tool was used to cut, chop and process.

Raw material - are stones used for making stone tools.

Core - is the main stone. Flakes are broken off by a stone hammer.

Flake – a small piece of stone detached from a larger stone or core by flaking.

Middle Paleolithic Culture

Around 398,000 years ago, there were further changes in stone tool technology in Africa. During this period Homo erectus lived. Anatomically modern humans are said to have emerged about 300,000 years ago.

Lithic technology - the methods and techniques involved in the creation of stone tools.

During this period, handaxes were further refined and beautified. Many small tools were also created. The core was well prepared, and then tools were created by extracting flakes from it. Pointed tools and scraping tools were used. Small cutting blades and knives were made.

A bifacial tool (Cleaver) Handaxe, flintstone from London, The British Museum

Pointed tools Scraping tools and other found in Olduvai Gorge Middle Paleolithic tools

As a result of human evolution, from the time the genetic tradition emerged to modern humans about 300,000 years ago, modern humans first emerged in the Sahara sub-region of Africa. This species spread to various parts of Asia about 60,000 years ago. They may have driven out those who already lived there. During this period, Cro-Magnon humans lived in Europe. Horns and tusks were used to make tools and artifacts. Bone needles, fishhooks, spears, and javelins were used.

They wore clothes. They ate cooked food. The dead were buried with their hands placed on their chests.

Upper Paleolithic Culture

The culture that followed the Middle Paleolithic culture is called the Upper Paleolithic culture. The new techniques in stone tool technology are one of the special features of this culture. Long stone cutting blades and burins were created. They used various types of silica-rich stones for tool making. Various paintings and art objects were created during this period. The various artifacts crafted reflect the advancement in their intellectual skills and the emergence of languages. Microliths came into use during this period.

Burin – a stone tool with a chisel-like edge

Lascaux cave paintings, western France, 17000 years old

Ice Age – The period up to 8,000 years ago when many parts of the world were covered with ice and glaciers.

Mesolithic Culture

The period between the Paleolithic and Neolithic periods is known as the Mesolithic period. People mostly used small microlithic tools. Following the warming of the Earth after the Ice Age, people who were mostly hunters-gatherers began to spread to various environmental regions (coast, mountain area, river basin, arid land).

Rock paintings found in Bhimbetka

Mesolithic people used microlithic technology. They created small artifacts, less than about 5 cm in size. They made arrowheads, scraping tools, and arrowheads. They also made tools with geometric shapes such as lunate, triangle, and trapeze. These tools were used with wooden or bone handles.

Microlith: Microliths are artifacts created from very small stones.

Neolithic Culture and the Beginning of Agriculture

Neolithic sites

Agriculture and domestication were introduced in the Neolithic period. This is an important stage in history. The Fertile Crescent, which includes Egypt and Mesopotamia, the Indus Valley, the Ganges Plain, and the fertile regions of China, is also referred to as the Neolithic Revolution.

In this period, permanent houses were built. Large villages were formed. Therefore, these are known as the Neolithic Revolution.

Early evidence for the Neolithic period is found. Agricultural activities have already started.

Wheat, barley, and peas were cultivated 10,000 years ago. Fruit and nut-bearing trees have been cultivated for yield since 4000 BC. Figs, olives, peaches, pomegranates, and grapes are included.

The region comprising Egypt, Israel-Palestine, and Iraq is crescent-shaped. It is known as the 'Fertile Crescent Region'.

Since new techniques of smoothing and polishing stones for making stone tools were used, it is called Neolithic.

Neolithic people also used carved stone tools from the Paleolithic period. Until the Mesolithic period, people relied on hunting and gathering for survival. Very little food was available through hunting and gathering. As a result, only a small number of people could live in a specific area.

Then came cultivation.

The cultivation of crops and the domestication of animals were introduced. This led to the production of abundant human and animal food.

Rivers deposited fertile alluvial soil, which helped to increase agriculture. If there was good agriculture and natural adaptation, people preferred to live on riverbanks. These new processes led to food surpluses. This food surplus is an important element in the formation of ancient civilizations.

Permanent houses were built during this period. Larger towns were formed. This, therefore, is called the Neolithic Revolution.

1.4. Palaeolithic Tamil Nadu

Lower Paleolithic Culture

Stone tools belonging to the Paleolithic period were created in Tamil Nadu by the human ancestor species called Hominin. These Paleolithic tools have been found in the areas around Chennai, especially in Attirampakkam and Gudiyam. Archaeological excavations at Attirampakkam and the artifacts found there, when subjected to cosmic ray dating, show that humans lived there about 1.5 - 2 million years ago. Thus, Tamil Nadu is one of the most important places where human ancestors lived in the world.

Rhyolite rocks: These are volcanic or igneous rocks. Volcanic rocks are formed from molten lava that emerges from under the earth.

Archaeological excavation

'Archaeological excavation' is the process of excavating stone tools, pottery, animal bones, pollen, etc. buried in the soil to understand the past lifestyle of human beings.

Cosmic-ray exposure dating – a method of determining the age of a sample by measuring the amount of cosmic rays that have been exposed to it.

In 1863, Robert Bruce Foote, an English geologist, first discovered Paleolithic tools at Pallavaram near Chennai. This is where such tools were first discovered in India. Therefore, the handaxes found here are called Chennai stone tool industry. The tools discovered by him are in the Chennai Museum.

Paleolithic people hunted wild animals. They gathered naturally available fruits, tubers, seeds, and leaves. They did not know about making iron or pottery. All that was discovered much later in history.

Handaxes and cleaving tools were the most important tool types in the Lower Paleolithic period. For this, they selected quartzite-type pebbles. These tools are found in sand dunes and riverbanks. They have been found at Pallavaram, Gudiyam Cave, Attirampakkam, Vadamadurai, Erumai Vettipalayam, Parikulam, etc.

Lower Paleolithic tools have also been found in the North Arcot and Dharmapuri regions. The people of this region used 'bauxite' volcanic rocks for artifacts.

It is estimated that the Attirampakkam Lower Paleolithic culture is about 2 - 1.5 million years old. This period lasted until 60,000 years ago in other parts of India.

No evidence has been found for the Middle Paleolithic culture in southern Tamil Nadu and Sri Lanka.

Middle Paleolithic Culture - Tamil Nadu

Middle Paleolithic culture developed between 385,000-172,000. During this period, changes occurred in the types of tools. Smaller artifacts were used. The core, stone flakes, scraping tool, knife, hole punch, Levallois flakes, handaxes, splitting tool, etc. are the tools of this period. Compared to the previous stage, these are smaller in size.

Evidence of the Middle Paleolithic culture is found in some parts of Tamil Nadu. Central Paleolithic tools have been collected in N. Pudupatti and Seevarkkadu in southern Tamil Nadu. Such tools have also been found near Tanjore and Ariyalur.

Lunate tools: Tools that are shaped like a crescent.

Neolithic Culture - Tamil Nadu

The culture of domesticating animals and doing agriculture is called the Neolithic culture. People of the Neolithic culture used polished stone axes called Celts. Cattle rearing was their main occupation. They lived in small villages. The houses were thatched. The walls were built by applying clay on sticks. Evidence of Neolithic villages has been found in Paiyampalli in Vellore district and some places in Dharmapuri district.

Neolithic humans must have first made pottery.

They fashioned pottery by hand or with a manually rotating wheel. Before firing the pottery, they polished them with pebbles. This is called burnishing.

The town of Paiyampalli is located in Vellore district of Tamil Nadu. The Archaeological Department of the Indian government has conducted excavations here. For the first time in Tamil Nadu, pottery and evidence of agriculture have been found. Pulses such as Foxtail millet, horse gram, and green gram have been found here.

Iron Age - Megalithic Period

The cultural period following the Neolithic period is called the Iron Age. As the name suggests, the people of this period used iron technology. This is the pre-Sangam period. The Iron Age was a period of great cultural development. It was during this period that the foundation for the Sangam period was laid. During the Iron Age, people had settled in many parts of Tamil Nadu. Interpersonal relationships grew.

People knew about metallurgy and pottery.

Tamils and the Lemuria

Some researchers believe that the Tamils originated in the submerged continent of Lemuria. This theory about the Lemuria continent was put forward in the 19th century. Due to advances in plate tectonics, scholars now present various views on this theory.

Tamil literary references mention the sea receding. These say that some parts around Kanyakumari were submerged underwater. Before 5000 BC, some parts of Sri Lanka and Tamil Nadu were connected by land. However, due to sea level rise, some land near Kanyakumari and the Sri Lanka-India connection may have been submerged. Further deep-sea studies are needed in this area.

Excavations reveal that the human race continuously lived in Tamil Nadu and other parts of South India from the Mesolithic to the Neolithic periods.

They used iron, bronze objects, and gold jewelry. They used shell jewelry. Carnelian and quartz beads were also used. Evidence of the Iron Age has been found in many places, including Adichanallur in Thoothukudi district, Sanur near Maduranthakam, and Sittannavasal near Pudukkottai. Megalithic burials are found throughout Tamil Nadu.

Types of Megalithic Burials

Since people used large stones to bury the dead, the Iron Age is also called the Megalithic Age.

Burials are classified as dolmen, cist, menhir, cairn, rock-cut caves, and urn burials.

The types of Kodakkal or Kudakkal (umbrella type), Thoppikkal, and Pathikkal are found in Kerala. Dolmens, like Namre, were erected as memorials of burials.

Cists are like stone boxes buried in the ground. They are formed by erecting four stone slabs on all four sides and covering them with a stone slab on top. Urns are pottery pots. These were used to bury the dead. Sarcophagi are like baked clay coffins. These are sometimes prepared by placing several legs on them. Menhirs are pillar-like tombstones erected as a memorial of burial.

“Porthole” holes are placed on one side of stone caves (Cists) and dolmens. These served as their entrances. There is also a belief that they were placed for the soul to come and go.

Tombstones (Menhirs) may have been built for warriors in the Iron Age. Sangam literature mentions various customs regarding burials. Megalithic burial monuments are classified as Kodakkal or stone umbrellas, cists, menhirs, tumuli, rock-cut caves, and urn burials.

Urn burials were done in this way during the Iron Age.

Agriculture and Animal Husbandry

Iron Age people had also improved agriculture. They also raised sheep and cattle. Some groups were still hunting and gathering food. Millets and pulses were cultivated. Irrigation management became important during this period. Because many megalithic sites were located near rivers and ponds.

Irrigation technology developed. Traces of burying paddy in urns have been found in megalithic sites in Adichanallur in Thoothukudi district and Porunthal near Palani.

Society and Politics of the Iron Age

In the Iron Age, there were agricultural communities, cattle breeders, hunter-gatherers. During this period, handcraftsmen, potters, metal craftsmen (goldsmiths) were entrepreneurs. There were many groups in society. The sizes of the stone tombs and the differences in the burial objects show that there were many social groups in this period and that they had different customs. Some of these formed themselves into societies under a leader. Herding led to wars and encroachments. Borders began to expand during this period.

Pottery

Pottery is an important evidence found at archaeological sites. People of the Iron Age and Sangam Age used black and red colors for pottery. Pottery was used for cooking, storing things, and eating. Black and red pottery appears black on the inside and red on the outside. The outside is polished.

Iron Technology and Metal Tools

Many iron objects have been placed as burial objects in megalithic stone tombs. Tools such as swords and daggers. Axes, chisels, lamps, tripods have been found.

Robert Bruce Foote, India's first Paleolithic instrument discovered at Pallavaram near Chennai

Some of these objects have bone or wood or horn handles attached. Iron tools were used for agriculture, hunting, food gathering, and warfare.

Bronze bowls, animal and bird-shaped vessels, bronze mirrors, beads have also been found.
