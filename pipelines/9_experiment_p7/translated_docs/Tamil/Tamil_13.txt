2.1 Weathering

The interaction of atmospheric phenomena directly
with the surface of the Earth leads to
Weathering. As a result, rocks undergo
disintegration and decomposition. These
activities are called Weathering.

Weathering is of three types.

*   Physical Weathering
*   Chemical Weathering
*   Biological Weathering

Granular disintegration

Physical disintegration occurs when rocks break
down without any chemical change. The
breakdown of rocks without any change is called
physical weathering.

Exfoliation

Due to the variation of heat and cold, the surface
of the rounded rocks peels off in layers like onions.

This process of rock disintegration is called
Exfoliation. Sheeting and shattering are different
types of rock exfoliation.

Chemical Weathering

Chemical weathering occurs more in humid
regions, tropical regions and temperate regions where sedimentary rocks are found.

Oxidation, Carbonation, Solution, Hydration cause
chemical weathering. Oxygen, Carbon dioxide
hydrogen and water are important factors for
chemical weathering.

Block Disintegration

Rocks expand during the day and contract at night.
As this process continues, pressure is exerted on
the joints of the rocks and the rocks disintegrate.
This disintegration is called Block Disintegration.

Information bit

Weathered rocks undergo weathering
and become soil. The mixture of weathered
fine rock particles and decayed organisms
(Humus) is called soil.

Oxidation

Iron in rocks reacts with oxygen in the atmosphere
to form iron oxide. This process is called oxidation.

Oxidation weakens the rocks and causes them to
disintegrate.

Carbonation

Carbon dioxide in the atmosphere dissolves in
water and turns into carbonic acid. When water
mixed with carbonic acid falls on limestone rocks,
carbonation occurs and the rocks disintegrate.
Carbonation causes the formation of caves.

Solution

The process by which soluble rock particles in
rocks dissolve in water is called Solution.
Disintegration occurs in rocks due to dissolution.

Hydration

Hydration occurs more in regions with humid
climates. Minerals inside the rock absorb water
and expand in size. This causes cracks in the rocks
and disintegration occurs. This process is called
Hydration.

Biological Weathering

The roots of plants penetrate through the cracks in
the rocks and cause the rocks to expand. Rock
disintegration by earthworms, animals (rats and
rabbits) and human activities is called biological
weathering.

2.2 Gradation

Natural factors such as rivers, ground water, wind,
glaciers and ocean waves level the surface of the
Earth. This activity is called Gradation. The
activities of the above-mentioned factors gradually
create different landforms on the surface of the
Earth. Landform gradation has two activities:
degradation and aggradation.

Gradation is the process by which natural agents
level the surface of the Earth.

Aggradation is the process by which natural agents
form landforms.

Degradation is the process by which the surface of
the Earth is worn away.

Gradation Activities = Erosion + Transportation + Deposition

I. Agents of Gradation

Rivers

Rivers form a large number of degradation activities. Rivers are fed by rainwater, glaciers, springs and lakes. The place where rivers originate is called the Source of the River, and the place where they join the sea is called the Mouth of the River.

Stages of Rivers

Rivers originate in the mountains and join the sea or a lake. As the river flows, its path is called the Course of the River. The course of the river is divided into three stages:

(i) The upper course

(ii) The middle course and

(iii) The lower course

(i) The upper course

‘Erosion’ is the main activity in the upper course
of the river. In this stage, rivers meander through steep mountain slopes. Due to the high velocity of the river on these slopes, it erodes the valleys wider and deeper as it flows. Landforms formed by rivers in the upper course are: - ‘I’ shaped valleys, ‘V’ shaped valleys, gorges, canyons, interlocking spur, rapids, potholes and waterfalls.

(ii) The middle course

In the middle course, rivers reach the plains. In this stage, tributaries join the main river and increase the volume of river water and the load of materials it carries. Transportation is the main activity in the middle course. Deposition also occurs in places where the velocity of the river suddenly decreases. Landforms formed in the middle course are alluvial fans, flood plains, meanders, oxbow lakes.

(iii) The lower course

The materials eroded and transported in the upper
and middle courses are deposited in the low-lying plains. As the low-lying plains are filled with sediments, the main river divides into tributaries. ‘Deposition’ is the main activity of this stage. Deltas and estuaries are landforms formed in this stage.

Waterfall

If hard rocks are on the upper layer and soft rocks
are on the lower layer, the soft rocks in the lower
layer are eroded quickly by water. As a result, the
hard rocks on the upper layer, which are not
eroded, protrude and cause waterfalls in the
course of the river. When the velocity of the
waterfall is high, it erodes the rocks at the place
where it falls, creating a structure like a pit. This
is called a plunge pool.

The highest waterfall in the world is Angel Falls
(Venezuela). Its height is 979 m.

*   Tributary - All the small rivers that join the
    main river are tributaries. (E.g.) Bhavani River
*   Distributary - Rivers that branch off from the
    main river. (E.g.) Kollidam River

A. Erosional landforms of rivers

Gorges & Canyons

When rivers flow through mountain areas with hard
rocks, they create valleys with steep sides. These
are called Mountain gorges. The Indus and
Brahmaputra rivers form this type of landform in
the Himalayas.

When mountain gorges with vertical slopes extend
for hundreds of kilometers, they are called Canyons.

The Grand Canyon (USA) formed by the Colorado river is the best example of this.

Pothole

Vertically dug round pits in the riverbed are called
Potholes. Their diameter and depth vary from each
other.

V-shaped valley

'V' shaped valley is a deep and wide landform formed in the mountains by the vertical erosion activity of the river.

Oxbow Lake

Meanders

As rivers wind, they widen over time and
eventually become a complete loop. These complete
loops are completely cut off from the main river
and appear like a lake. This is called Oxbow lake.

Kanwar Lake in Bihar (India) is the largest
freshwater oxbow lake in Asia. Lake Chicot in
Arkansas, USA is the largest oxbow lake in the
world.

B. Depositional landforms of rivers

Alluvial fan

Materials carried by rivers are deposited in the
shape of a fan-shaped cone at the foot of the
mountains. These deposits are called Alluvial fans.

Flood plain

Fine deposits deposited on the banks of the river
due to floods form flood plains. These deposits
make the area fertile. Rivers continuously raise
the banks by depositing sediments on the banks.
The landform formed by this elevation is called
Levees.

Estuary: Forms at the mouths of rivers. Usually,
deposition does not take place here like in deltas
due to the erosion of the waves. (Example) Narmada
and Tapti in India.

Delta

When deposits increase in the river, its velocity
decreases. As a result, rivers meander. Sediments
are deposited in a triangular shape at the mouth of
the river. The landform formed by deposits in this
triangular shape is called a delta. Alluvial deposits
in deltas are fine and mineral-rich. (Example) Cauvery
Delta - Tamil Nadu

Information bit

The Sundarbans Delta is the delta formed by the
Ganges and Brahmaputra rivers. It is the largest
delta region in the world.

The Old Faithful geyser (Yellow Stone National
Park) in Wyoming, USA is the world's most
famous geyser.

II Karst Topography

Ground water creates various types of landforms in
limestone regions due to the erosion and deposition
processes. These are called karst topography. These
are found in rock regions such as limestone, dolomite,
gypsum, etc., which dissolve easily in water. The
karst topography in western Slovenia covers an
area of approximately 480 km long and 80 km wide.

This landform is called 'Karst' in the Slovenian
language. ‘Nullarbar’ located on the coast of Great
Australia is the largest limestone karst topography in
the world.

Places where limestone regions are found in the
world:- Southern France, Spain, Mexico, Jamaica,
western Cuba, central New Guinea, Sri Lanka and
Myanmar.

A. Erosional Landforms of Underground Water

Dissolution is the main reason for the erosion
activity. When rainwater mixed with carbon
dioxide falls in limestone regions, it chemically
reacts with the limestone and dissolves and
disintegrates it. As a result, landforms such as terra
rossa, lappies, sinkholes, swallow holes, dolines,
uvalas, polje, caves and caverns are formed.

Terra Rossa

(Italian for "red soil")

The remaining red soil in limestone regions after the
limestone soil has dissolved and disintegrated is
deposited. This soil is red in colour because of the
iron oxide in it.

Lappies

When ground water flows through rugged
limestone rocks, long erosion grooves occur. These
grooves are called lappies.

Sinkholes

Funnel-shaped pits caused by the dissolution of
limestone rocks are called Sinkholes. Its average
depth is 3 to 9 meters.

The world's deepest sinkhole is Xianozhai tienkang in China at 2172 feet deep. There are more than 15000 sinkholes in Illinois, USA.

Caves and Caverns

Caves and caverns are underground landforms
found in limestone regions. A cavity caused by
carbonic acid reacting with limestone rocks is called
a cave. These vary in shape and size. The floor of
the caverns will be uneven. Example- Guptadam in
West Bihar.

Speleothems are landforms formed by deposition in
caves and caverns. Travertine, tufa and drip stone
are speleothems. Caves, uvalas, wadpolines, poljes
are limestone karst topographies found in other
parts of the world.

B. Depositional Landforms of Underground Water

Landforms are formed due to deposition on the roof,
floor and walls of caves and caverns in limestone
regions.

Stalactite, Stalagmite and Column

When water mixed with calcium carbonate drips
from the roofs of caves, it looks like lime protrusions.
This is called Stalactite. When water mixed with
calcium carbonate falls on the floor and grows
upwards, it is called Stalagmite. A stalactite growing
downward and a stalagmite growing upwards join
together to form columns or pillars.

III. Glacier

A glacier is a large mass of ice moving slowly from
the accumulation area. The place where snow
accumulates is called a Snow field. The limit of the
permanently snow-covered area in high altitude and
high latitude areas is called the Snow line. As we
go higher in latitude, the snow line is closer to sea
level.

The permafrost line in the Alps mountains is 2700
meters. But the permafrost line in Greenland is 600
meters. Find out the reason.

A. Erosional landforms of glacier

Glaciers are an excellent agent of erosion.
Landforms formed by erosion are cirque, arete,
Matterhorn, 'U' shaped valley, hanging valley, Fjord.

These types of landforms are mostly found in
countries like Switzerland, Norway.

Cirque

Valleys are formed when glaciers erode the vertical
sidewalls of mountains. These valleys shaped like
armchairs are called cirques.

Arete

When two cirques are located on opposite sides, their
back and sidewalls are eroded. Such eroded cirques
appear like a knife-edged ridge.

Pyramidal peak

When three or more cirques join, it gets a pyramidal
shape with sharp sides. This landform is called a
pyramidal peak (E.g.) Matterhorn.

'U' shaped valley

As glaciers move along the valleys of rivers, the
valleys are eroded further deeper and wider,
creating ‘U’ shaped valleys.

Hanging valley

The valley of the tributary glacier located on the valley of the main glacier is the hanging valley.

Fjord

Glacier valleys partially submerged in the sea are called glacier fjords.

B. Depositional landforms of glacier

Glacial deposits are deposits made up of fine
sediments, rock particles and gravel carried by
glaciers. Landforms such as moraines, drumlins,
eskers, kames and glacial outwash plains are formed
when these deposits are deposited in low-lying areas.

Moraine

The deposits deposited by glaciers are called
moraines. Moraines are classified into several types
based on the place of deposition, such as ground
moraine, lateral moraine, and medial moraine.

Drumlin

Drumlins are moraines that look like an overturned
large spoon or a half-cut egg.

Esker

As glaciers melt, the gravel, pebbles and sand they
carry are deposited as a long, narrow ridge parallel
to the glacier. These narrow ridges are called eskers.

Outwash plain

As glaciers melt, the transported sediments are
deposited at their ends. These deposits are called
glacial outwash plains. These deposits are a vast
plain composed of sand, gravel and silt.

IV. Wind

Wind is air moving horizontally over the surface of
the earth. The activity of wind is more prevalent in
arid regions. Erosion, transportation and deposition
are the main activities of wind. This activity of wind
is also called aeolian activity.

A. Erosional landforms

Erosional landforms formed by wind are mushroom
rock, inselberg and yardang.

Mushroom rock

In rock layers containing both soft and hard rocks,
the soft rocks found at the base of the rocks are
continuously eroded by the wind, and the rocks
acquire a shape like a mushroom. These rocks are
called mushroom rocks or pedestal rocks. These
types of rocks are found in Jodhpur in Rajasthan.

Inselberg

Inselberg is a German word. Its meaning is island
mountain. Inselbergs are isolated, elevated
landforms in arid regions that are not subject to wind
erosion. (Example) Uluru or Ayers Rock in Australia.
A mountain with a wider base and cone-shaped top.

Yardang

In arid regions, some vertically aligned rocks consist
of alternating layers of hard and soft rocks. In this
sequence, soft rocks are easily eroded by wind.
Hard rocks that are not eroded by wind appear like
irregular crests. These types of landforms are called
yardangs.

B. Depositional landforms

Landforms formed by the deposition of wind are
sand dunes, barchan and loess.

Sand dunes

Sandstorms in deserts carry large amounts of sand.
When the velocity of the wind decreases, the
transported deposits are deposited in the shape of a
hill. The landform that looks like a hill is called sand
dunes. Sand dunes are divided into various types.

Barchan

Crescent-shaped isolated sand dunes are called
barchans. They have a gentle slope on the windward
side and a steep slope on the leeward side.

Transverse dunes

Transverse dunes are found in an asymmetrical
shape. Transverse dunes are formed when the wind
blows at a rapid and moderate pace in the same
direction.

Seif dunes

Seif dunes are long narrow ridges of sand. These
sand ridges are parallel to the direction of the wind.
These are called Seifs in the Sahara.

Loess

Soft and fine deposits deposited in a vast region are
called Loess. Places where loess is found:- Northern
and western China, Pampas of Argentina, Ukraine
and the Mississippi Valley in North America.

V. Waves

Sea water rising and falling is called a sea wave. It
has an upper crest and a lower trough. Sea waves are
an important agent of landform gradation. Erosion,
transportation and deposition are the main activities
of sea waves. Its activities are found only along the
coastline.

A. Erosional landforms

Sea cliffs, wave-cut platforms, sea caves, sea arches,
sea stacks, beaches and spits are landforms formed by
wave erosion.

Sea cliffs

As sea waves hit the rocks facing the sea, the rocks
are eroded and steep rocks with gentle slopes are
formed. These rock formations that look like vertical
walls are called sea cliffs.

Sea cave

As sea waves continuously hit the sea cliffs, the base
is eroded and creates a tunnel-like structure. These
are called sea caves.

Sea Arch

As the promontories near the islands are further
eroded, they join together to form an arch-like
structure. These arches are called sea arches.

Sea stack

When sea arches are further eroded, the arches break
and fall. A part of the fallen sea arch that stands
facing the sea looks like a pillar. This is called a sea
stack.

Wave cut platforms

As waves hit the cliffs, an erosion plane is formed at
a certain height. Wave-cut platforms are also called
bench, shelf, terrace and plain.

B. Depositional landforms

Beach

Sand and gravel eroded by sea waves are deposited
on the seashore. This is the most important
constructive activity of sea waves. (Example) Juhu
Beach in Mumbai, Marina Beach in Chennai, Puri
Beach in Odisha.

Bar

A long landform formed by sand deposits in the sea
is called a bar. This bar is usually parallel to the
coastline.

Spit

One end of the sand bar is connected to the land
and the other end extends towards the sea. This long
landform is called spit.

Long sandbars are usually found in tidal estuaries.
