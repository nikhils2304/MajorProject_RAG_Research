5.2 Biodiversity

Biodiversity refers to the various types of organisms (plants, animals, and other microorganisms) that live in a habitat. It is subject to the climate, topography, and trends of human activities. This biodiversity reflects the strength of a place's biological resources.

Each element in biodiversity plays an important role in the ecosystem. It serves as an ecological balance and social sustainability of an area, i.e., tourism, education, and research.

5.2.1 Loss of Biodiversity

The extinction of species (plants and animals) due to human and natural processes is called loss of biodiversity. A healthy ecosystem provides clean water, air, fertile soil, food, raw materials, and medicines. Therefore, this ecosystem should be protected.

5.3 Biomes

A biome is a very large ecological structure in the Earth's environment where various types of plants, birds, and animals live together. Here, plants and animals interact with each other and live together in the biosphere as a group.

Biomes are determined by factors such as topography, climate, and vegetation. The biome can be classified into two main categories. Namely, terrestrial biomes and aquatic biomes.

Terrestrial biomes.

All organisms are interdependent and create energy levels, food chains, and food webs. The functions of the ecosystem depend on the structure of the energy flow. This energy flow helps in the distribution and cycling of inorganic and organic matter in the ecosystem. Energy flow mostly occurs in a hierarchical order in different levels of the ecosystem. These levels are called Energy levels. The process of energy transfer from one group of organisms to another through various energy levels is called a food chain. Interconnected food chains form a Food Web.

If more than 70% of the forest in an ecological zone is lost, that place is considered as a biodiversity hotspot that needs to be protected.

 The Himalayas, the Western Ghats, the Indo-Burma region, the Sunda Land region, etc., of India are biodiversity hotspots.

 There are 34 places in the world that are considered as biodiversity hotspot.

5.3.1 Terrestrial Biome

A terrestrial biome is when a group of organisms lives together in accordance with the land environment they live in. Here, heat and rain determine the living environment. The major terrestrial biomes of the world are:

a) Tropical forest biome

b) Tropical Savanna Biome

c) Desert Biome

d) Temperate Grassland Biome

e) Tundra Biome

a) Tropical Forest Biome

The tropical forest biome is formed by various sub-biomes. They are tropical evergreen forests and seasonal deciduous forests.

Tropical forests are habitats for various species, and are primary farming lands.

The Amazon basin, the Congo basin, and the Indonesian islands, etc., are the most important tropical forest biomes. Since these areas have very dense forests, they are economically very important. Therefore, settlements are scattered here. Logging, fishing, and shifting cultivation are the livelihoods of the people living here. Since the environment is humid in this biome, there is a tendency for tropical diseases like malaria and dengue fever to occur. Rubber, bamboo, ebony, etc., are the main trees found here. Bats, colorful parrots, leopards, monkeys, etc., are the major birds and animals found here.

About 70% of the plants used to cure cancer are found in rain forests, according to the United States National Cancer Institute.

b) Tropical Savanna Biome

Tropical grasslands are mostly found between tropical forests and deserts. This biome is found between 10° and 20° north and south latitudes. These grasslands are found in the Sahel, the southern part of the Sahara in East Africa, and Australia, etc. This biome is generally hot and dry, with moderate rainfall. Therefore, the grasses that grow here are tall and sharp. Therefore, the main occupation of the people living here is cattle grazing. The indigenous people living here are nomads.

Most of the Savanna grasslands are being converted into agricultural lands. This is threatening the various types of animals found here. (e.g., the number of animals like leopards and lions is decreasing significantly).

Animals like lions, leopards, tigers, jackals, zebras, and giraffes are found here. Parasitic, Barad oats grass, and lemon grass, etc., are found in this biome.

c) Desert Biome

Desert regions are mostly found on the western edges of continents. This biome is found between 20° and 30° north and south latitudes. Here, the average annual rainfall is less than 25 cm. Therefore, due to lack of rainfall and dry climate, plants grow sparsely here. However, the unique plants found here are called Xerophytes. Because the soil here is sandy and saline, it is not suitable for agriculture. Drought-tolerant shrubs, scrubs, and thorny plants, etc., are found here. The indigenous people living here are engaged in food gathering and hunting. These people move grazing lands from one place to another. Camels are used for transportation here as transportation is very difficult here.

A desert oasis is a fertile freshwater area found in arid desert regions in the environment. Desert oases get water from springs. Crops such as dates, figs, citrus fruits, and maize are grown near the desert oasis.

Desert plants are drought-tolerant and use water efficiently. Snakes, lizards, and ants etc. are abundant here.

d) Temperate Grassland Biome

Temperate grasslands are found in the interior of continents. Here, there is a moderate summer, a long winter, and winter rainfall. The grasslands in these regions are completely dependent on rainfall. High rainfall helps tall grasses to grow, and low rainfall helps short grasses to grow. These regions are suitable for wheat cultivation. Agriculture is mechanized to a large extent here due to the lack of farmers. Livestock farming is an important occupation here. Therefore, industries such as animal slaughtering for food, meat processing, and dairy farming are practiced here. Grasshoppers, jackals, prairie dogs, etc., are found here.

e) Tundra Biome

This vast low-lying region is mostly frozen. Northern parts of Asia, Canada, Europe, Greenland, the Arctic, and Antarctica are included in this biome. This region is also called a barren land. Winter is long and harsh, and summer is mild. Due to very low temperatures, only short seasonal plants can be found here. Therefore, the primary production efficiency is very low. People living in this region live as nomads. Hunting and fishing are their main occupation. The population density is very low here. Harsh climate often changes their habitats. They live in “Igloo” houses in winter and in tents in summer.

Mosses, arctic willows, and lichens, etc., grow in arctic regions. Polar bears, jackals, arctic foxes, and eagles are found in the polar regions.

The temperate grassland is called by different names in different parts of the world. They are:

Prairie – North America

Steppe – Eurasia

Pampas – Argentina and Uruguay

Veldt – South Africa

Downs – Australia

Canterbury – New Zealand

Manchurian – China

5.3.2 Aquatic Biome

In the aquatic biome, organisms interact with each other and adapt to their environment, energy sources, and location. Like terrestrial organisms, aquatic organisms are also influenced by abiotic factors. The aquatic biome is classified into freshwater biome and marine biome.

a) Freshwater Biome

The freshwater biome includes lakes, ponds, rivers, streams, and swamps. This biome is subject to abiotic factors such as water volume, water flow, oxygen level, and temperature. Lily, lotus, and aquatic plants grow here. Turtles, crocodiles, and fish are found in this biome.

b) Marine Biome

The largest aquatic biome on earth is the marine biome. This biome is a habitat for various types of plants and animals found in seawater. Secondary marine life forms such as coral reefs are present in it. Aquatic plants that grow in a mixture of fresh and marine water are also present in coastal areas and estuaries. Water stability is helpful for the movement of marine life forms. Aquatic organisms migrate to different parts of the world more diversely and specially than terrestrial biomes.

Besides animals, a large number of plant species such as seagrass, sea algae, and floating plants are found in this biome. The aquatic biome is very important not only for plants and animals but also for human beings. Humans use this biome for water, food, leisure.

Some of the problems found in this biome are overfishing, environmental pollution, and sea level rise.

5.3.3 Conservation

The biome ranges from deep sea trenches to evergreen forests. The role of human species is very high for the energy distribution found in it. Human activities are the primary factors for the extinction and migration of some organisms due to artificial disasters. Due to the ever-increasing population, species resources are heavily exploited and affected. This has a severe impact on the plants and animals found on earth. Some parts of the world have high biodiversity and high biodiversity loss. Therefore, it is the prime duty of human beings to protect the organisms and keep the earth as a better living ecosystem.

Biosphere Reserves

Biosphere Reserves are a special ecosystem or a unique protected area that includes plants and animals. There are eighteen major biosphere reserves in India.
