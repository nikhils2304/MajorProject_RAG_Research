B. Scale

The scale is used to draw the
entire world on a paper. Scale is the ratio of the

distance between two points on the map and the

distance between the same two points on the earth's

surface. Scales are shown on the map in three ways.

They are: Statement Scale method, Representative Fraction

method, Linear Scale method.

Statement Scale

Comparing the distance on the map and

the actual distance on the earth and expressing it in

words is the Statement Scale method. That is, one

centimetre is equal to ten kilometres. It is expressed as '1 cm = 10 km'.

Representative Fraction

In this method, the comparison of the map and

actual distances is expressed as a ratio or fraction. It

is usually abbreviated as R.F. It is expressed as 1/100000

(or) 1:100000. This means that one unit on the

map represents 100,000 units on the earth. This unit

can be an inch or a centimetre or any other

unit. So, the scale in ratio or fraction method is

G. Conventional signs and symbols

A map is a universal

language. It must be drawn to suit international standards. Conventional signs are

permanent symbols used on the map. In addition,

their meanings are explained in the map legend

section. It contains various information about the

topographical and cultural features. These elements

are shown as symbols in different colours. This

maintains the clarity of the map.

7.1.3 Survey

Measuring the angle, direction, area, height,

and distance of a place on the earth's surface using

instruments is called Surveying. Surveying methods are

used to prepare maps and to obtain location-based

information. Surveying helps in preparing

topographic maps especially.

Modern surveying instruments used by geographers

to draw maps are Chain, Prismatic Compass, Plane Table, Dumpy Level,

Abney Level, Clinometer, Theodalite, Total Station

and Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) are used to

measure the distance, angle, height, and area of

a place.

7.2.1. Aerial Photography

Aerial photography involves capturing

information about places or objects or events using

photographic equipment mounted on low-flying

birds, balloons, helicopters, aeroplanes, and drones.

Aerial photographs are continuously

taken at regular intervals of 10–30 seconds from a

fixed height. Each photograph has a slight overlap

with the previous photograph. A three-dimensional

(3D) image of the study area can be prepared by

removing the overlapping areas and creating a mosaic

of the joined photographs.

B. Geographic Information System – GIS

Geographic Information System is a computer

based tool. It is used to collect more specific

details about a given geographical area. It uses

remote sensing, global positioning system, and other

data sources.

Geographic Information System is a combination

of computer hardware, software, geographic data, and

personnel.

G – Geographic

I – Information

S – System

Geographic Information System (GIS) was introduced

in 1950 by Waldo Tobler and then by Howard

Tomlinson.

Google Maps, Yahoo Maps, and Google Earth

are some of the prime examples.

The most important element linked to the

internet is location. In a map, we can link

information from an unfamiliar location to an

address that we know and integrate the information.

Each type of information of a region is stored as

separate layers of a map. In GIS, layers can be used

if needed, or they can be removed if not needed.

For example, hospitals, schools, water bodies,

parks, and ATMs. With the help of remote sensing,

global positioning system, and linked databases,

computers can create a map.

7.3 Bhuvan

The Sanskrit word Bhuvan means ‘Earth’.

The free online platform for computer-based

applications was created by the Indian Space

Research Organisation (ISRO) on August 12, 2009.

Through this, it is possible to analyse

Indian Remote Sensing (IRS) images taken by

the seven satellites of the Indian Space Research

Organisation for public service. This also includes

images taken by the Cartosat I and Cartosat II

satellites.

Using Bhuvan, which is linked to the

internet, a person can explore desired locations,

events that have taken place in the news, or any part

of the world that they cannot visit, or search for

names of places using latitude and longitude.

Scientists, scholars, academics, policy makers, or

the public will find Bhuvan very useful.
