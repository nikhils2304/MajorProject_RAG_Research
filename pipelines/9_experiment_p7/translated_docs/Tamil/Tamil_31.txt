Introduction

The year 1914 A.D. in world history marks a turning point. Political and social activities that began in 1789 reached a climax in the First World War, which erupted in 1914 and determined the course of the 20th century. Therefore, historians call this period the "Long Nineteenth Century". This war was a total war that impacted the economies of the world, and was also the first war that affected a large part of the civilian population. The political map of the world was redrawn by this war. At the end of the war, three empires lay shattered. They were Germany, Austria-Hungary, and the Ottoman Empire. The most important outcome of this war was the Russian Revolution. It is unique in world history and is the first of its kind among revolutions. For the first time, world nations tried to establish world peace through the League of Nations.

In this lesson, we will discuss the circumstances that led to the outbreak of the First World War, the Russian Revolution, and the consequences of the formation of the international organization called the League of Nations.

1.1 Competition for Colonies

Competition for Markets among Capitalist Countries

The aim of capitalist industries is to produce surplus. The surplus wealth thus produced was used to establish more and more factories and also to set up stock exchanges and build steam ships or for other similar purposes. The revolutions in communication and transport in the late nineteenth century facilitated European expansion in Africa and other regions.

One aspect that attracted attention in the nineteenth century was the emergence of Japan as an European-style dominant power and Asia and Africa were colonized and exploited. England held an incomparable place among the European countries and was the leader of world capitalism. As demands for markets and raw materials grew, capitalist countries raced around the world to expand their empires for exploitation.

The Rise of Monopoly Capitalism

After 1870, Free Competition Capitalism changed into Monopoly Capitalism. One of the important features of imperialism was that both Industry and Finance merged, and sought profit in markets for their goods and capital. This became clear in the later part of the 19th century. The old concept of free trade collapsed. Joint stock companies emerged in America and trade unions in Germany.

A joint stock company is an industry-related establishment engaged in the production and distribution of goods. The company has more control over the distribution, price, etc. of goods in order to benefit the shareholders.

Imperialism and Its Main Features

Capitalism inevitably leads to Imperialism. Lenin says, "Imperialism is the highest stage of Capitalism". Not only markets for the goods produced on a very large scale but also raw materials were needed. Imperialism is not only about colonies, but also about complete militarization and complete war.

1.2 Rivalry of the Great Powers

Europe

In the nineteenth century, the European powers colonized the other large and small countries of the world and exploited them for their own benefit. By the 1880s, most of the Asian countries had been colonized.

Africa was the only exception. Between 1881 and 1914, Africa was captured, divided and colonized. After 1870, England, France, Belgium, Italy, and Germany in Europe participated in the colonial race.

Conflicts between the Great Powers

Although England held the primary place in industrial growth after 1870, England did not have sole control over the vast empire. England had to compete with Germany and America. Because those countries produced cheaper goods and thereby captured the market of England. This competition between countries often led to conflicts between the great powers in Asia, Africa, and Europe.

Asia: The Rise of Japan

In Asia, Japan during this period followed the Western countries in many areas during the Meiji Restoration (1867-1912). Japan became a leading nation in the industrial sector and also an imperial power. Though the rulers held feudal ideas, Japan adopted Western education and machinery technology. Japan emerged as a leading country in the industrial sector with a modern army and navy. In 1894, Japan forcibly waged a war on China. In this Sino-Japanese War (1894-1895), Japan, a small country, defeated China and surprised the world. Ignoring the warnings of great powers such as Russia, Germany, and France, Japan merged the Liaodong peninsula with Port Arthur. Through this action, Japan established itself as a militarily powerful state in East Asia.

Due to the heavy pressure given by the European states, Japan relinquished its claim over Port Arthur. Taking advantage of this situation, Russia sent a large army into Manchuria. In 1902, Japan, after entering into an agreement with England, forced Russia to withdraw its forces from Manchuria. Russia underestimated Japan. War broke out between the two countries in 1904. In this Russo-Japanese War, Japan defeated Russia and regained Port Arthur. After this war, Japan attained the status of a superpower.

Japan's Rude Diplomacy and Wisdom

In the years after 1905, Japan controlled Korea's domestic and foreign policies. Japan annexed Korea in 1910 as a result of the assassination of the Japanese Resident General. Following the fall of the Manchu dynasty in China in 1912, the turmoil in China once again offered Japan an opportunity for expansion. Japan demanded that Germany hand over to her the rights Germany had in the Shandong region of China. This aggressive political wisdom caused animosity between Japan, China, and the European countries. However, no one was in a position to oppose Japan.

Colonization and Its Consequences

In 1876 only about 10 percent of Africa was under European rule. By 1900 the whole of Africa was colonized. England, France and Belgium divided the continent among themselves. A few places were ceded to Germany and Italy. England, France, Russia, and Germany established their own "Spheres of Influence" in China. Japan appropriated Korea and Taiwan. France occupied Indo-China. America took the Philippines from Spain. England and Russia agreed to divide Iran.

The initial attempts by Europeans to establish colonies in Africa resulted in bloody wars. France had to wage a long and intense war to capture Algeria and Senegal. England was defeated by the Zulus in 1879 and by the Sudanese forces in 1884. The Italian army suffered a devastating defeat at the Battle of Adowa in 1896 against the Ethiopian forces, but the European forces finally emerged victorious.

1.3 Causes and Course of the First World War and its Consequences

(a) Causes

Alliances and Counter-Alliances of European Countries

By 1900 the European great powers were divided into two armed camps. One camp consisted of the Central Powers Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy. Under the guidance of Bismarck, they formed the Triple Alliance in 1882. According to this, Germany and Austria would help each other. In another camp France and Russia were members. According to the agreement entered into in 1894, both the countries would support each other if any one of them was attacked by Germany. In this way England was isolated. In order to come out of its isolation, England approached Germany twice and failed. When Japan's hostility towards Russia increased, France being a friendly country to Russia wanted to ally with England (1902). The Anglo-Japanese agreement prompted France to make an agreement with England. It wanted to resolve the issues related to its colonies Morocco and Egypt. As a result, a friendly agreement was reached between the two countries in 1904. France agreed to recognize England's annexation of Egypt, provided France was allowed to act independently in Morocco. Subsequently, England entered into an agreement with Russia regarding Persia, Afghanistan, and Tibet. Thus the Triple Entente consisting of England, France and Russia was formed in 1907.

Aggressive Nationalism in the Balkans

With the growth of patriotism a mind-set of “My country right or wrong I support it” developed. It caused hatred of one country for another. The Jingoism of England, the Chauvinism of France, and the Kultur of Germany all contributed to aggressive nationalism that determined the outbreak of war.

Germany's Expansionist Mind-set

German Emperor Kaiser William II proclaimed "Germany will be the leader of the world." Following Napoleon's defeat at the Battle of Trafalgar in 1805, naval authority was considered to be England's monopoly. In this situation, England decided that Germany's aggressive political diplomacy and her rapidly building naval fleet were against her. The estrangement between the two countries further increased when England also entered the naval expansion race.

France's Animosity with Germany

France and Germany were old enemies. France, defeated by Germany in 1871, had to lose the Alsace and Lorraine regions to Germany. Memories of this defeat were etched in the minds of the French. Germany's interference in the Moroccan affair further increased this bitterness. German Emperor Kaiser William II demanded that an international conference be convened to decide on the future of Morocco while England supported France in the Moroccan affairs.

Imperialist Politics in the Balkans

In 1908, the Young Turk Revolution took place in Turkey as an attempt to create a strong, modern state. This gave Austria and Russia an opportunity to resume their activities in the Balkan regions. In this regard, Austria and Russia met and discussed. According to this, Austria can annex both Bosnia and Herzegovina, and Russia can take its warships freely through the Bosphorus and Dardanelles straits. Subsequently, Austria announced that it had annexed Bosnia and Herzegovina. This announcement provoked strong opposition in Serbia. Germany assured Austria of her support in this regard. Germany went to the extent of announcing that "if Russia helps Serbia if Austria invades Serbia, then I will support Austria". This animosity between Austria and Serbia became the cause for the outbreak of war in August 1914.

Balkan Wars

In the first half of the eighteenth century, Turkey was a powerful country in Southeast Europe. Its empire stretched from the Balkans, across Hungary to Poland. The Turkish Empire contained many non-Turkish peoples in the Balkan regions. Turkish and non-Turkish people belonging to the Balkan region were involved in horrible massacres and atrocities. The Armenian genocide is a horrific example of this.

Taking advantage of the unstable political and economic situation of the Turkish Empire in the latter part of the eighteenth century, various countries were trying to liberate themselves from the control of Turkey one after the other. Macedonia comprised people belonging to various ethnicities. Therefore, there were competitions between Greece, Serbia, Bulgaria and later Montenegro to bring Macedonia under their control. In March 1912 they formed an association called the Balkan League. The League decisively defeated the Turkish forces in the First Balkan War (1912-1913). Subsequently, problems arose in dividing the captured territories. According to the London Agreement signed in May 1913, a new country called Albania was created. Macedonia was divided among the other Balkan countries. Turkey was reduced to a state with territories around Constantinople alone. Bulgaria felt betrayed by the division of Macedonia between Serbia and Greece. However, Bulgaria was easily defeated. The Second Balkan War ended in August 1913 with the signing of the Bucharest Agreement.

Immediate Cause

The climax of these events that took place in the Balkans was the assassination that took place in Sarajevo, Bosnia. On June 28, 1914, Austrian Emperor Francis Ferdinand, heir to the throne, was assassinated by a Bosnian Serb named Princip. Austria considered this as an opportunity to capture Serbia. Russia mobilized its troops to support Serbia. On the first day of August, Germany declared war on Russia. Although there were no disputes between Germany and France, Germany planned to wage war against both France and Russia, since there was a coalition between France and Russia. Germany wanted to take advantage of this situation. England was forced to participate in this war when Germany attacked Belgium, ignoring its neutrality.

(b) Course of the War

Two Warring Camps

Allied Powers

The warring countries were divided into two groups. The Central Powers consisted of Germany, Austria, Hungary, Turkey and Bulgaria. Italy, which initially aligned with Germany and Austria, later withdrew. The reason for Italy's withdrawal was that Germany did not support Italy's attempt to reclaim the city of Trentino, which was located in northeastern Italy and was under the rule of the Austro-Hungarian Empire and where many Italian people lived. Therefore, Italy remained neutral when the war broke out. However, Italy decided to join the war with the aim of gaining the territory in the northeast. A secret agreement was signed in London in April 1915 between France, England, and Italy. According to this, Italy agreed to participate in the war against the Central Powers on the basis that the territory it desired would be given to it after the war.

Allied Countries

The Allied nations that opposed the Central Powers were Russia, France, Britain, Italy, America, Belgium, Romania, Serbia, and Greece. Romania and Greece declared war against the Central Powers in 1916 and 1917 respectively. But they participated very little in this war. As most Americans wanted their country to remain neutral, America provided moral support to the Allied nations and provided huge amounts of economic assistance to England and France for the first three years.

Failed Peace Efforts

Russian Emperor Nicholas II suggested that all the countries should meet and create a situation for world peace. In response to his call, two peace conferences were convened in The Hague, Netherlands in 1899 and 1907. But no result was achieved.

Western or French Front

Germany realized that it would have to fight on two fronts. It was waiting for an opportunity to launch its attack on France. Germany invaded Belgium to reach France. The Germans expected an easy victory. However, the French forces defeated the Germans in the Battle of the Marne (early September 1914). In this way Paris was saved. The Battle of the Marne was the beginning of trench warfare.

Battle of Tannenberg and Marne

Verdun Battle

Between February and July 1916, the Germans attacked Verdun, an important fortress in France. In the Battle of Verdun that lasted for five months, two million soldiers participated. About half of them were killed.

England's attack against Germany took place near the banks of the River Somme. England lost 20,000 soldiers on the first day of the four-month war. However, the Battle of Verdun determined that the Allied countries would win the First World War.

Eastern Front or Russian Front

On the eastern front, Russian forces repeatedly defeated the Austrian forces. At the same time the Russian forces were defeated by the German forces. Although the Russian army was poorly trained, it suffered huge losses due to lack of adequate war preparations. In 1917, the Tsarist regime was overthrown by the October Revolution. As Russia needed peace, it withdrew from the war on March 3, 1918, by signing the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk with Germany.

Smaller War Arenas

Middle East

Turkey joined the war with the Central Powers. Although it initially won victories, Turkey was eventually defeated after the Allied nations suffered setbacks (especially in Mesopotamia and Gallipoli). The Turks tried to attack the Suez Canal. But it was repulsed and driven away. England fought the Turkish forces first in Iraq and then in Palestine and Syria.

Far East

China joined the Allied forces. Japan captured the Qiaochow region that Germany had ceded to China in the Shandong province. There were no other wars in the Far East. Japan took advantage of this situation and forced and intimidated China and obtained more favorable concessions and rights.

Balkans

The Austrian-German forces completely crushed Serbia with the integration of Bulgaria. Serbia came under German rule. Romania, which was monitoring the course of the war, joined the Allied forces in August 1916. But Romania was also captured by the Austrian-German forces.

Position of German Colonies in Africa

The German colonies in East and West Africa were attacked by the Allied nations. Although all these colonies were at a great distance from Germany, they were forced to surrender to the Allied nations as they could not get any immediate help from Germany.

Italian Inability to Face Austrian Aggression

Italy joined the Allied forces in May 1916. Italians who fought against Austria continued their resistance. But Italy collapsed when the Germans landed to help Austria.

Places Where Central Powers Won

The Central Powers successfully captured Belgium and the northeastern part of France, Poland, Serbia, and Romania.

Naval War and American Participation in the War

The heart of the war was on the western front and on the seas. The Allied nations held an incomparable position in the seas. As the Allied nations controlled the sea routes, they were able to stop the food and other goods that had to go to the Central Powers. Due to this, women and children in Germany and Austria suffered from starvation and poverty. Germany launched an aerial attack on England. Bombs were dropped on London and the industrial areas. Later, civilians were targeted and bombed.

Germany introduced poison gas. Later both sides began to use poison gas.

Naval Wars and American Participation in the War

In 1916, a naval war (Jutland War) took place in the North Sea. England won this war. After this the Germans started their submarine war. They harassed the Allied ships. The famous ship Emden dropped bombs on Chennai.

As a countermeasure to the actions that interrupted and blocked the movement of their ships, Germany announced in January 1917 that neutral ships would also be sunk. The American ship Lusitania was attacked and sunk by Germany. As a result, many Americans who traveled in it died. Because of that America was overwhelmed with great grief and anger. President Woodrow Wilson declared war on Germany in April 1917. With enormous economic and military strength, America entered the war. It made the Allied victory a foregone conclusion.

(c) Armistice and the Treaty of Versailles

Although Germany made exceptional efforts, it was unable to resist and eventually surrendered in November 1918. The ceasefire came into effect from November 11. Due to the political crisis in Germany following the resignation of German Emperor Kaiser William II, Germany was forced to accept harsh conditions.

Paris Peace Conference

The Paris Peace Conference began in January 1919, two months after the armistice was signed. Woodrow Wilson (American President), Lloyd George (British Prime Minister), and Clemenceau (French Prime Minister) played a key role in the deliberations.

American President Woodrow Wilson put forward his Fourteen Points Plan for the Allied nations to follow. The most important of these was that he emphasized that "a general assembly of nations should be created which can provide mutual assurance to the political independence and territorial integrity of all nations, without distinction between big and small nations."

The German government was forced to accept the agreement for fear of having to face another war. The Peace Treaty was signed on 28th June 1919 in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles.

Clauses of the Agreement

1. Since Germany was responsible for initiating the war, Germany had to pay reparations for the war losses and all the Central Powers were forced to pay the war indemnity.

2. The German army was reduced in size to only 1,00,000 soldiers. A small navy was allowed to be maintained.

3. The unification of Austria and Germany was prohibited.

4. All the colonies of Germany were made protectorate countries of the League of Nations.

5. Germany was forced to take back the Brest-Litovsk agreement made with Russia and the Bucharest agreement made with Bulgaria.

6. The Alsace-Lorraine regions were returned to France.

7. Finland, Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania, which were previously territories of Russia, were allowed to function as independent nations.

8. Northern Schleswig was given to Denmark and small districts to Belgium.

9. Poland was re-established.

10. It was announced that the Rhineland would be under the occupation of the Allied forces and the area east of the Rhine River would be a demilitarized zone.

News Report

Although the Sultan accepted the Sevres Agreement with Turkey, it was opposed by Mustafa Kemal Pasha and his followers and was therefore rejected.

Consequences of the First World War

The First World War had a profound impact on European society and politics. Compulsory military service enabled more people to participate in the war and aerial attacks caused more casualties than in the past. In four years, 8 million people died. About twice as many people were injured. Many became permanently disabled. Subsequently, several million people died of influenza in 1918. As a result, the gender balance was upset in all the countries and there was a shortage of men. Soldiers were placed in a higher position than the general public.

The First World War and its aftermath was a time in history that turned everything upside down. These are all eye-catching. The rise and integration of Soviet Russia, which came to be known as the U.S.S.R. or the United Soviet Socialist Republics. America, which entered the war as a debtor nation, emerged after the war as a nation lending to the world.

One of the other major events during this period was the complete awakening of the colonial countries and the intense efforts they made to gain freedom. Mustafa Kemal Pasha played a key role in Turkey's rebirth as a nation. Kemal Pasha not only obtained freedom for that country. He also modernized Turkey and obtained recognition for it.

Impact on India

The First World War had a significant impact on India. The British recruited a large force consisting of Indians to serve in the war zones in Europe, Africa, and West Asia. After the war ended, these soldiers returned with new ideas. Those ideas impacted Indian society. For war expenditure, India paid 230 million pounds in cash, 125 million pounds in loans and 250 million pounds worth of materials needed for the war front. As a result, large-scale economic hardships occurred in India. Riots broke out due to food shortages and other people looted shops. India was also in great distress due to the epidemic that spread all over the world as the war ended.

The conditions of war led to the emergence of the Home Rule Movement in Indian politics. The divided Congress party reunited during the war. Indians and India actively participated in this war hoping that England would appreciate and reward India's loyalty. But only disappointment awaited. Thus the First World War had several impacts on Indian society, economy, and politics.

1.4 The Russian Revolution and its Impact

Introduction

The most important outcome of the First World War was the Russian Revolution, which is unique in world history. The casualties and distress caused by the First World War brought the social, political, and economic conditions prevailing in Russia to their peak. Two revolutions took place in 1917, one in March and another in November. Following the abdication of the Tsar, the Bourgeoisie, who took power, wanted to continue the war. But the people were against it. Therefore, they launched the second great revolution under the guidance of their leader Lenin. After capturing power, Lenin established a communist government in Russia.

Causes of the Revolution

Social Cause

In Russia, both Peter the Great and Catherine II carried out European modernization without changing the social structure. Russian peasants were bound to the landowners as serfs to the estates. After Russia was defeated in the Crimean War some reforms were introduced. In 1861 Tsar Alexander II abolished serfdom and freed them. But they were not given enough land to live on. These farmers became a gunpowder depot of revolution. As the country industrialized, the number of workers also increased. They were underpaid and unhappy.

Role of Revolutionaries

At the same time, as revolutionary ideas spread among the intellectuals and the Tsar suppressed them, students who were committed to the policy of Zemstvo spread their ideas among the peasants. Soon new ideas took shape based on Marxist ideology. Also the Social Democratic Labor Party was formed.

Tsar's Autocracy

Tsar Nicholas II, of the Romanov dynasty, had little experience in governance. His wife Tsarina Alexandra was a dominant personality who favored Rasputin, who had considerable influence over the royal family.

In order not to be left behind in the race to seize colonies, Russia promoted the expansion of Russia in Manchuria. This prompted Japan to wage war in 1904. The defeat that Russia suffered in this war led to strikes and riots.

The Role of Radicals in 1905

On January 22, 1905, a priest named Gapon led a procession of men, women, and children towards the Tsar's Winter Palace in St. Petersburg. A national assembly of people's representatives, reforms in the fields of agriculture, industry etc. were their demands. But the police and army opened fire. Hundreds were killed. Thousands were injured. The events that took place on this day, which was called 'Bloody Sunday', led to strikes, rebellions, and violence.

Tsar Nicholas gave an assembly and was forced to establish a parliament called Duma. But this did not satisfy the left-wing parties. They formed an organization called Soviet (group) consisting of workers' representatives in Petersburg. Trotsky was its leader.

Opposition to the Tsar, the Dissolution of the Duma

As Russia allied with France and England with the outbreak of the First World War, the Russian monarchy temporarily strengthened. As rumors spread that there would be a palace revolution with the aim of replacing the Tsar, Nicholas declared himself the head of the forces and went to the battlefield. A few days before 1916 came to an end, Rasputin, an influential official who was a favorite of the Tsar and Empress, was assassinated by a member of the Tsar's family. The Soviet members of the city of Peterburg were arrested. Whenever Duma opposed the Tsar's intentions, the Duma was dissolved and new elections were held. Without any change in the policies of the government, the fourth Duma came to an end with the revolution of 1917.

Uprisings of the Public

In this situation, the hardships of the women working in factories (whose husbands were serving in the army) forced them to strike. They marched through the areas where there were many factories in Petrograd, the capital of the Russian Empire. Thousands of female workers marched with war fervor, shouting "Bread for labor." When they waved their hands at the workers working in the factories, they shouted "Come out" "Stop work" and 4,00,000 workers of the city joined the struggle on the next day (February-24).

The Tsar's Abdication

The government used its troops to break up the strike. But on the fourth day of the strike, mutinies of soldiers broke out in the barracks. The Tsar, who was at a great distance, was leading his army as its commander-in-chief. His orders were not broadcast in the city. There was no one there to do that task. After this the Tsar tried to return to Petrograd. Railway employees stopped the train he was traveling in on the way. Shocked by such events, some commanders and leaders in Petrograd persuaded the Tsar to abdicate. Tsar Nicholas II abdicated on March 15 after the public revolts.

Provisional Government

There were two organizations that jointly carried out the government administrative duties. One, an organization consisting of Bourgeois politicians belonging to the ownership class, which was an association of the old Duma. The other, a group of workers' representatives or the Soviet. A provisional government could be established by the Duma with the approval of the Soviets. While the Mensheviks dominated the Soviets, the Bolsheviks were fearful and unable to make decisions. This situation changed with the arrival of Lenin.

Failure of the Provisional Government

When the revolution broke out, Lenin was in Switzerland. He wanted the revolution to continue. His slogan "All powers to the Soviets" attracted the workers and leaders. The people who had been subjected to great distress due to the war-time shortages were attracted by the slogan "Bread, Peace and Land". But the provisional government made two important mistakes. One was postponing the decision to be taken on the demand for the redistribution of lands. Another was the decision to continue the war. The peasant-soldiers who were disappointed with the Russian revolution left their duties and got involved in land acquisition. This event further intensified the uprising that took place in Petrograd under the leadership of the Bolsheviks. The government deployed troops and arrested the Bolsheviks. Trotsky was also arrested.

Bolshevik Party under the Leadership of Lenin

Seizure of Power

In October, Lenin persuaded the central committee of the Bolshevik Party to decide on an immediate revolution. Trotsky prepared an elaborate plan. They were also given complete support by the industrial laborers.

Lenin was born in 1870 to educated parents near the middle Volga region. Attracted by the ideas of Karl Marx, he believed that the struggle of the masses was the way to freedom. Lenin and his supporters, who had the support of the majority (Bolshevistvo), became known as the Bolsheviks. The minority opposed to him were called Mensheviks.

On November 7, key government buildings, the Winter Palace, and the Prime Minister's headquarters were captured by armed factory workers and revolutionary forces. On November 8, 1917, the new communist government in Russia took over. The Bolshevik party was renamed the Russian Communist Party.

Consequences of the Revolution

The Russian Communist Party was able to eradicate illiteracy and poverty in Russia in a short period of time. The industry and agriculture of Russia attained exceptional growth. Women were granted equal rights, primarily suffrage. Factories and banks were nationalized. Land was declared the property of the community. Lands were distributed to the other farmers. As not withdrawing from the war was the main reason for the fall of the provisional government, Lenin immediately undertook efforts for peace. He withdrew from the war in order to focus on the formation of the new government, without worrying about the harsh conditions of the Central Powers. The Treaty of Brest-Litovsk was signed in 1918.

The global impact of the Russian Revolution

The Russian Revolution ignited the imagination of the world's people. Communist parties were formed in many countries. The Russian Communist government encouraged the colonies to fight for liberation and also fully supported them. Discussions on land reform, social welfare, labor rights, gender equality, etc., which are essential, began to take place at the global level.

Pravda is a Russian word. It means "Truth". It was the official daily newspaper of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union from 1918 to 1991.

1.5 The League of Nations

Organization and Members

The charter for the League of Nations was drafted at the Paris Peace Conference and added to every treaty made after the First World War. This task, which posed challenges, was fulfilled by the pressure exerted by the American President Woodrow Wilson. Britain and America gave opinions in creating the political organizational rules for this organization.

The League, which was started in 1920, had five components. They were the General Assembly, the Executive Committee, the Secretariat, the International Court of Justice, and the International Labor Organization. Each member country was represented in the General Assembly. The Executive Committee was the organization that carried out the decisions. Britain, France, Italy, Japan, and America were initially declared as permanent members of this organization. Each member had the right to cast one vote.

As all decisions had to be taken unanimously, even the small countries had veto power. The Secretariat of the League of Nations was located in Geneva. Its first Secretary General was Sir Eric Drummond of Britain. The Secretary General would appoint the Secretariat employees on the advice of the Executive Committee. The International Court of Justice was established in The Hague. This Court comprised fifteen judges. The International Labor Organization had a secretariat and four members from each member country participated in its general conference.

Goals of the League of Nations

One of the two goals of the League of Nations was to prevent wars and establish peace in the world. Another was to improve international co-operation in social and economic matters. The League of Nations wanted to resolve problems in the beginning itself by being a mediator and peace maker. If wars broke out even after the arbitration, the League would first impose economic sanctions and then military sanctions on the country that caused the war.

As the three superpowers America (non-member), Germany (defeated nation), and Russia did not participate in this organization, the difficulties in achieving these goals further increased. Germany and Russia had initially joined the League in 1926 and 1934, respectively. Germany withdrew in 1933. Russia was expelled in 1939.

Functions of the League

Between 1920 and 1925 the League of Nations was called upon to resolve several disputes. The League was successful in resolving three issues. In 1920, an issue arose between Finland and Sweden as to who owned the Aland Islands situated between the west coast of Finland and the east coast of Sweden. The League of Nations ruled that the islands belonged to Finland. The following year a border dispute arose in the Upper Silesia region between Poland and Germany and when the League was called upon to resolve the issue, the League successfully resolved the issue. In 1925 the third issue arose between Greece and Bulgaria. When Greece invaded Bulgaria, the League of Nations ordered a ceasefire. After an investigation, the League blamed Greece and ruled that Greece should pay war reparations.

In 1925, the League of Nations functioned successfully until the Locarno Agreement was signed. According to the Locarno Agreement, Germany, France, Belgium, England, and Italy assured each other of peace in Western Europe. After this, Germany joined the League of Nations. A permanent seat was also given on the Security Council. Two years later, America and Russia began to participate in the non-political activities of the League.

Violation of Commitment

One of the major problems that the European powers faced was how to make disarmament possible. The conference on Disarmament issues organized by the Security Council of the League in 1925 met only in February 1932. When Germany was denied permission to have weapons equal to France in this conference, it refused. In October, Hitler withdrew Germany from the conference and from the League of Nations.

In 1931 Japan attacked Manchuria. While the League condemned Japan's action, Japan also withdrew from the League following Germany's action. In the case of Italy attacking Ethiopia, the League imposed sanctions against Italy. When the sanctions came into effect, Italy withdrew in 1937 in protest. The League remained a silent witness to the events that took place without interfering in the subsequent disputes of Rhineland, Austria, Czechoslovakia, and Poland. Russia's expulsion in December 1939 for attacking Finland was the final definite action of the League. The League's General Assembly did not meet after that. Finally, the League of Nations was dissolved in 1946.

Reasons for Failure

The League of Nations was seen as an organization of the victorious nations in the First World War.

Since the League did not have an army of its own, it could not implement the decisions it took.

Those who created this organization for peace had not realized the inherent power of nationalism. The concept of 'collective security' could not be put into practice. Italy, Japan, and Germany, led by dictators, refused to abide by the League's orders, and only England and France were firmly in a position to act.
