Introduction 4.1 Divisions of those engaged in agriculture

Even after India gained independence, for the next fifty years, a majority of people in Tamil Nadu depended on agriculture. That situation is changing in recent times and a large number of people are becoming landless laborers. According to the 2011 census, the number of farmers in Tamil Nadu has decreased over the past ten years. However, not everyone who owns land owns the same amount of land. Many have land in small plots. But some have a lot of land.

According to the 2001 census, 49.3 percent of the workforce was in the agricultural sector. Ten years later, this figure decreased to 42.1 percent. In 2011, there were a total of 3 crore 29 lakh workers in Tamil Nadu. Of these, 96 lakh were agricultural workers. Approximately 55 percent of women were involved in agriculture in 2011, while slightly more men (35.3%) were involved in the sector.

The number of agricultural laborers has also decreased in Tamil Nadu. The number of people cultivating land in 2015-16 was 79,38,000. However, five years ago, there were 81,18,000 farmers. In these five years, their number has decreased by 1,80,000.

Due to this, the area under cultivation has also decreased from 64.88 lakh hectares to 59.71 lakh hectares. That is, on average, Tamil Nadu has lost 1,03,400 hectares of cultivated land per year in the last five years.

The area under cultivation in Tamil Nadu is about 45,44,000 hectares. This area varies from year to year. This area will increase if there is normal rainfall during the rainy season. This area will decrease if there is excess or less rainfall. Only a small portion of this area provides an opportunity to cultivate more than once a year. This area also changes from year to year depending on water availability. This area was 9 lakh hectares the previous year. The following year, it decreased to 6 lakh hectares due to lack of rain. This area will remain somewhat stable only if there is a stable and reliable source of water.

Most of the farmers in Tamil Nadu are marginal farmers (cultivating in an area of less than one hectare). Marginal farmers account for 78% of the total number of farmers. However, the area of land they cultivate is only 36% of the total cultivated area. Next are small farmers (cultivating 1-2 hectares) who account for 14%. The area they cultivate is 26%.

Not only in Tamil Nadu but also throughout India, the area under cultivation is shrinking. The number of marginal farmers is increasing. In contrast, their number is decreasing in Tamil Nadu. This indicates that people are moving away from agriculture.

4.2 Land use types

The total geographical area of Tamil Nadu is one crore thirty lakh thirty-three thousand hectares. Only about one-third of this area is used for cultivation (45,44,000 hectares). 17 percent of the land is used for non-agricultural purposes. Almost the same amount of land (2,125 thousand hectares) is under groves. Slightly less than 4 percent of the total land area is unusable. The amount of land lying fallow for the time being is one-tenth of the total area. Other fallow lands account for 13 percent. Thus, one-fourth of the land is left fallow. We should pay attention to the fact that the area of land left fallow is increasing. Apart from this, dry lands and lands where forests are grown account for slightly more than 5 percent of the total land area.

In 2012–13, food crops were cultivated in approximately 72 percent of the total cultivated area; Other crops were cultivated in the remaining areas.

4.3 Water resources for agriculture

There are no perennial rivers in Tamil Nadu. Tamil Nadu gets its water from the Northeast and Southwest monsoons. If the Southwest monsoon is abundant, the Cauvery River will swell in Tamil Nadu as the reservoirs in the Cauvery River basin in Karnataka will be full.

The area under irrigation in Tamil Nadu is 57 percent of the total cultivated area.

The major source of water for Tamil Nadu is the Northeast monsoon (October – December). The Northeast monsoon water is stored in reservoirs, tanks and lakes and used for agriculture. Canals, lakes, ponds and wells provide water for agriculture in Tamil Nadu. There are 2,239 canals in Tamil Nadu with a length of approximately 9,750 kilometers. There are 7,985 small lakes and 33,142 large lakes. There are 1.5 million open wells. Besides this, 3,54,000 bore wells are being used. Agriculture is being carried out in Tamil Nadu with these resources.

The area of land irrigated by lakes is very small. Approximately 3.68 lakh hectares of area receive water through lakes. Canals provide water to 6.68 lakh hectares. Bore wells provide irrigation to 4.93 lakh hectares and open wells to 11.91 lakh hectares.

Hidden Water

The term hidden water was introduced in 1990 by Arnon Hoestra. Hidden water is defined as the water consumed in the production of agricultural or industrial products.

Hidden water means that when food or other manufactured goods are traded from one country to another, the water used to grow them also goes implicitly. This is hidden water. For example, the global average water requirement to produce one metric ton of wheat is 1,340 cubic meters. This means that when one metric ton of wheat is exported to a foreign country, 1,340 cubic meters of water used to grow it also goes with it.

India is one of the largest users of fresh water in the world. This is a very alarming level. India is the fifth largest exporter of hidden water in the world.

As the amount of water drawn from the lakes for irrigation increases, the water level goes down. There is a possibility that either the water may dry up completely or it may not be enough for irrigation.

The Central Ground Water Board continuously monitors the quantity and quality of ground water.

From this we know that

1. Tamil Nadu agriculture depends on ground water.

2. It is very urgent and necessary to regulate the use of ground water.

3. This is very important for sustainable agriculture.

4.4 Irrigation and Crop Types

The crops grown in Tamil Nadu can be classified as food crops and non-food crops. 57 percent of the total area under food grain cultivation in Tamil Nadu receives irrigation. In 2014–15, food crops received 59 percent irrigation and other non-food crops received 50 percent.

The total area under cultivation in Tamil Nadu in 2014–15 was 59 lakh 94 thousand hectares. Other non-food crops were cultivated in 76 percent of this area.

Paddy is cultivated on a large scale accounting for 30 percent. These are cultivated in 12 percent of the area under food crops. Small grain cultivation is done on a smaller scale. Sorghum is cultivated in 7 percent of the land area, pearl millet in one percent of the land area and finger millet in 1.7 percent of the area. These small grains were cultivated in 6 percent of the area in 2014–15.

Micro Irrigation Technology

Micro irrigation technology is a good solution to irrigation water shortage. This technology leads to higher yields in crops than traditional irrigation methods. Since water is applied moderately at regular intervals, production efficiency and water use efficiency are increased, labor costs are significantly reduced, and weed growth is controlled. By irrigating fertilizers through micro irrigation, fertilizer use efficiency is increased and a quality product is obtained. Since Tamil Nadu is a water deficient state, the government is focusing on intensively implementing the micro irrigation scheme in agricultural crops that require a lot of water.

4.5 Ten Year Agricultural Development

In Tamil Nadu, the largest amount of food grain produced in 2014–15 was one crore 27 lakh 35 thousand tons. Paddy alone yielded nearly 80 lakh tons. Paddy accounts for 62 per cent of the total food grain production. Maize accounted for 20 percent. Sorghum yielded 7 percent and finger millet 3 percent. Black gram yielded three percent. Other food crops were cultivated on a small scale.

The amount of production also varies depending on the area under cultivation.

4.6 Production Capacity

Crop yields depend not only on the area under cultivation but also on the production capacity of the crops.

The production capacity of paddy, which is widely cultivated in Tamil Nadu, was 4,429 kg per hectare in 2014–15. This capacity was 3,039 kg in 2010–11. From this we can know that production capacity has increased.

Following paddy, maize was cultivated on a large scale in 2014–15 with a yield of 8,824 kg per hectare.

I. Choose the correct answer

The production capacity of crops is constantly increasing. For example, the production capacity of paddy was 1,409 kg in 1965–66. It rose to 2,029 kg in 1975–76. It rose again to 2,372 kg in 1985–86. Ten years later, the production capacity of paddy was 2,712 kg. In 2014–15, the production capacity was 4,429 kg. In the last fifty years, the paddy production capacity in Tamil Nadu has increased more than three times. If we look at food grain production capacity, it can be seen that it has increased by approximately 3.5 times between 1965–66 and 2014–15.
