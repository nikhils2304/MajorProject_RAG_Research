8.1 Disaster Response
Restoring the natural environment, providing rehabilitation to the affected people, rebuilding the lost livelihoods and reconstruction efforts including restoring the affected basic infrastructure are included in disaster response. Responding to disasters includes saving lives, providing first aid, streamlining transport and communication, improving affected structures, and meeting basic needs such as food, water.

What to do during an earthquake?
Be aware that some earthquakes are tremors that occur before major earthquakes. We must reduce the movement and go to a nearby safe place and wait until the seismic activity is over to ensure life safety.

If inside the building
1. Kneel under the table, covering your head, holding the leg of the table with one hand and covering your head with one hand. If there are no furniture in the room, kneel in the corner of the room and cover your head with both hands.
2. Protect yourself by sitting in the corner of the room, under the table or under the bed.
3. Stay away from glass windows, doors, walls and easily falling hanging lights and furniture.
4. Stay safe inside until the earthquake is over. After that, it is safer to leave.

If outside the building
1. Stay away from buildings, trees, lights and power lines.
2. If you are in the open, stay there until the earthquake is over. The most severe risk is in the exit areas and exterior walls of buildings.
Most earthquake-related deaths occur from collapsing buildings, flying glass objects, and falling objects.

If in a running vehicle
1. Stop the vehicle immediately. Do not park the vehicle near buildings, trees, power poles and under overpasses.
2. Cross the roads carefully after the earthquake stops. Avoid roads, bridges, and slopes damaged by earthquakes.

8.1.2 Tsunami
Tsunami causes loss of life and property. A series of ocean waves caused by earthquakes, underwater landslides, volcanic eruptions, and asteroids is called a tsunami. A tsunami can travel at a speed of about 700–800 km/h at a height of 10–30 meters. It causes flooding. It affects power, communication, and water supply.

How to face a tsunami?
1. First, identify whether the place where you live, school, workplace, frequently visited places, etc., are prone to tsunami impact.
2. Plan the exit route from your home, school, workplace, and frequently visited places that are subject to tsunami impact.
3. Watch the local radio or television to learn about tsunami-related information.
4. Discuss the tsunami with your family. Everyone should know what to do during a tsunami.
Discussing tsunamis in advance can help reduce fear of tsunamis in an emergency and save valuable time.

What to do after a tsunami?
1. Listen to the radio or television for the latest tsunami-related information.
2. Find out if you are injured and get first aid. Then help people who are injured or trapped.
3. If anyone needs to be rescued from the tsunami, call experts with the right tools for help.
4. Assist those who need special assistance, such as children, the elderly, those without transportation, large families who need help in emergencies, and the disabled.
5. Stay away from the building if it is surrounded by water. Floods caused by tsunamis can weaken the foundations of buildings and flood the buildings, cause cracks in the head, or break the walls and cause them to collapse.
6. Check for gas leaks. If you smell gas or hear a hissing sound, immediately open the windows and evacuate everyone.
8.1.3 Riot

Riot is a very dangerous, unpredictable event like a natural disaster, even though it seems like a drama. Thousands of people die in riots worldwide. Riots occur due to various ethnic, national, political, economic and social causes. It is usually not planned in advance. According to a riot-related study conducted by the Pew Research Center on April 11, 2015, Syria ranks first in the list of 198 countries in the world, followed by Nigeria, Iraq and India.

Even if you cannot immediately run away from the place where the riot is taking place, you must take steps to protect yourself from danger. Follow the instructions below to know how to protect yourself from a riot.

8.1.4 Fire

Wildfires occur in hot and dry weather in areas with dense forests. It occurs in areas such as forests, grasslands, bushes and deserts. Also, it spreads rapidly through the air. Fire can destroy buildings, farms and camps, power lines and communication lines, oil and fuel depots. It can cause injuries to people and animals. Lightning, sparks in dry areas, volcanic eruptions, and human-caused fire accidents are some of the causes of fire.
Residential areas are affected by smoke due to wildfires. When fire smoke spreads in the air, it causes respiratory problems.

In India, about 25,000 people die due to fire and fire-related accidents. Of this, 66% are women. In India, 42 percent of women and 21 percent of men die from fires every day.
