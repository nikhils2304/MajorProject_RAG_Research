3.2 Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR)

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights, prepared by representatives from different parts of the world with different legal and cultural backgrounds, is a milestone in the history of human rights. This declaration, adopted by the UN General Assembly held in Paris on December 10, 1948 (General Assembly Resolution 217A), is a common standard of achievement for all nations and all peoples. This first declaration, aimed at protecting fundamental human rights globally, has been translated into various languages.

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights contains 30 articles. It guarantees the right to freedom and also provides civil, political, social, economic, and cultural rights. These rights apply to all people, regardless of race, gender, or nationality, because all human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights.

protecting freedom. They ensure a person's ability to participate in the civil and political life of the society.

'Civil Rights' refers to the rights that are given to every human being by the law of the government without any categorizations such as race, nationality, color, gender, age, or religion. 'Political Rights' are the rights used to form and administer a government, and these are given to citizens through law. These rights give citizens the power to participate directly or indirectly in the administration of the country.

3.3 Fundamental Rights in India

The rights that are essential for the overall development of a human being are called fundamental rights. These provide citizens with the right to speak, the right to live in a place of their choice, and some other rights, making human life meaningful.

Fundamental Rights
• Right to Equality
• Right to Freedom
• Right against Exploitation
• Right to Freedom of Religion and Conscience
• Cultural and Educational Rights for Minorities
• Right to Constitutional Remedies

3.3.1 Right to Equality

Everyone is equal before the law, and everyone has equal protection through the law. It is against the law to differentiate or exclude anyone on the basis of religion, race, gender, or place of birth. If someone is treated that way, they can approach the court.

Equality, human dignity, and non-discrimination are included in this.

3.2.1 Social, Economic, and Cultural Rights

Social, economic, and cultural rights are part of the law of human rights that was formed after the consequences of World War II.

Social rights are the rights required to participate fully in a society. Economic rights are the rights that ensure every human being has the economic status to fulfill their needs. Economic equality and freedom within the law in a country are protected by this.

Cultural rights are the rights that ensure everyone has the right to practice their culture. This includes equality, human dignity, and non-discrimination in cultural enjoyment.

3.2.2 Civil and Political Rights

These rights protect an individual from the excesses of the government, social institutions, and private entities,

3.3.2 Right to Freedom

The six types of freedom are given in our constitution.

They are:
• Right to freedom of speech.
• Right to assemble peacefully without arms.
• Right to form associations.
• Right to reside in any part of India.
• Right to move freely throughout India.
• Right to practice any profession or carry on any occupation, trade, or business.

3.3.3 Right against Exploitation

It is illegal to employ children under the age of 14 in mines or other hazardous industries. No contractor or employer can force a worker to work against their will.

3.3.4 Right to Freedom of Religion and Conscience

Citizens have the right to accept and follow any religion they wish.

Citizens are given the right to accept and follow certain religious beliefs or to live according to their conscience without religious beliefs.

3.3.5 Cultural and Educational Rights

The constitution has given the right to protect and promote culture. We have the right to form educational institutions and to protect and promote our tradition and culture. People can establish religious-based educational institutions. The government gives grants for this. However, admission should not be denied to anyone in these types of institutions, citing differences in caste, color, race, or religion.

3.3.6 Right to Constitutional Remedies

Fundamental rights are guaranteed by the Constitution. If a citizen's fundamental rights are denied, he can approach the court under the Right to Constitutional Remedies. The court orders the government to restore the rights of that citizen. This is called judicial review. If an act is considered illegal according to the constitution, the constitutional remedies provide the correct solutions. Thus, this right acts as a protector and guardian of all rights.

Prathika Yashini won her employment rights by approaching the court under the Right to Constitutional Remedies.

3.5 Indian Human Rights Commission of the commission in the states. The State Human
(National Human Rights Commission - NHRC) Rights Commission is located in Chennai.

Under the Protection of Human Rights Act, the National Human Rights Commission was established on October 12, 1993. Three members from the judiciary and four members from other departments are responsible for the NHRC. It is an autonomous body. This organization consists of a chairman and some members. The National Human Rights Commission is responsible for protecting and promoting the life, liberty, equality, and dignity of an individual guaranteed in the Constitution of India and in international covenants.

Functions of the National Human Rights Commission:
• Conduct inquiries into human rights violations or negligence by a public servant in preventing such violations.
• Intervene in human rights violation cases pending before the courts.
• Undertake and promote research in the field of human rights.
• Spread human rights education among various sections of society.
• Encourage the efforts of non-governmental organizations and institutions working in the field of human rights.

3.6 State Human Rights Commission (SHRC)

One of the ways to establish a State Human Rights Commission in every state in India is through Section 21 of the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993. The primary goal of this commission is the protection and improvement of human rights. Furthermore, the commission has streamlined the procedures in the states by formulating regulatory rules, such as making the functioning of the State Human Rights Commissions transparent, and the commission will take cognizance even if no complaint is lodged.

Functions of the State Human Rights Commission
• To investigate human rights violations related to the items specified in the State List and the Concurrent List.
• Its aims and functions are similar to those of the National Human Rights Commission. But its scope is limited to the state. There is a chairperson and two members in this commission.
• This commission has the power equivalent to the Civil Court. Therefore, it can investigate and adjudicate cases that are filed or taken up suo motu.
• Recommendations can also be made to provide compensation to the affected persons.

3.6.1 Rights for Children

We must ensure some other rights beyond the fundamental rights described by the constitution.

The United Nations defines everyone up to the age of 18 as children. This is found in Article 25 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights. Based on these principles, the UN adopted the Declaration of the Rights of the Child on November 20, 1989.
• Right to life
• Right to family environment
• Right to education
• Right to social security
• Right against sexual abuse
• Right against selling or kidnapping
• Right against other exploitation like child labor.

Right to Life

A child gets the right to live even before birth. Right to Life includes birth right, basic needs like food, clothing, shelter, and a dignified life.

Right to Family Environment

A child has the right to spend a normal childhood in a good family environment. Children who are destitute, abandoned, or have lost their parents also have the right to live. Such children can be adopted into caring families.

Right to Social Security

If parents or guardians cannot provide a decent life for children due to ill health, disability, or old age, the government should provide financial assistance to those children.

Right to Education

As per Article 21A of the Constitution, the Indian Parliament implemented the Right to Education Act in 2009 to provide free and compulsory education to children between the ages of 6 and 14.

The Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act of 2009 emphasizes that every child has the right to free elementary education. This right facilitates every child to receive free and compulsory education in a nearby school until they complete elementary education. A child who is studying should not have to pay any kind of fee.

In order to prevent child marriages and to provide punishment against those related to child marriages, the Indian government enacted "The Child Marriage Prohibition Act, 2006".

This Act defines the age limit for male and female children as 21 years for a male "child" and 18 years for a female "child". According to this Act, "Child Marriage" means a marriage in which one of the two getting married is a child. (Eg.) If parents arrange marriage for their 17 years old daughter, such marriage is referred to as "voidable". That is, if one of the people getting married is a child, they can go to court and cancel or annul the marriage. If child marriage has taken place, the people related to the event will be given a rigorous imprisonment which can be extended up to 2 years or fine up to Rupees 1 Lakh or both. Imprisonment for these offences are non-bailable.

The National Commission for Women has been established in India to review and ensure the constitutional and legal safeguards for women. This commission recommends its opinion and disagreement on all government schemes related to women's welfare and development.

President, Prime Minister, Parliament Speaker, Opposition Leader, Central Ministers, Chief Ministers and Governors many women are holding important positions of India. Ancestral Property Rights for Women

The Tamil Nadu Hindu Succession (Tamil Nadu Amendment) Act, 1989 has been implemented and has given equal rights to women in ancestral properties.

3.6.3 Right to Information Act, 2005

Right to Information Act is a revolutionary law under the Indian Constitution. This act, to bring transparency in government institutions, was enacted in India in October 2005.

According to this act, any common citizen can ask for the information needed from government institutions. The information must be provided within 30 days. If failed to do so, a particular amount will be collected as fine from the information providing officer.

Right to Information act is one of the strong laws in the country. This act is structured in a way which is very simple, and common people also can use it. Furthermore, the public information officer must help the people who do not know to read in getting the information with this act. All the government offices that is local bodies, municipalities and corporations offices, various government departments, government schools, and highway departments etc., come under this act.
