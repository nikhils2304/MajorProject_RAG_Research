3.1 Barter System

If you ask whether people have always used money,
the answer is no. Then when and in what form did
money become part of human life? How did it take
on new forms over time? Let's learn about that in this lesson.

In ancient times, people were hunters and food
gatherers, and they obtained their food. They lived in
caves and forests. Gradually, they discovered tools for
hunting and gathering food. They then learned to use
fire and practice agriculture. They built houses using
soil and started living in one place. Moreover, they made
earthenware using soil. As production increased through
agriculture, they engaged in making handicrafts. They exchanged
the surplus food items and items like earthenware they
produced with people who needed them. For example, if a
community had a lot of food items, they bartered them
with people who had surplus earthenware. Likewise, a
crop that was produced in large quantities in one region
was bartered for a crop that was produced in large quantities
in another region. The exchange of these goods can be
called the first form of trade.

3.2 Coins

The barter system was prevalent in all the areas where
civilizations developed. Not only within one civilization,
but also between different civilizations, exchange took place
through the barter system. This can be called the first form
of international trade.

Items found from the Indus Valley Civilization period
were excavated in the regions of Egypt and Iraq
(Mesopotamia).

During the later Chola period, merchants were allowed to
maintain armies. Also, there are historical evidences of
later Chola rulers providing loan assistance to small traders
and manufacturers to meet the export needs of Tamil merchants.

3.3 Natural Money

Gradually, problems arose in the barter system of exchanging
goods over time. For example, there was difficulty in bartering
for a good that one needed. One person has rice. He needs
earthenware. But the person who has earthenware doesn't
need rice. Like this, many people did not have all their
needs fulfilled. There were problems in calculating the amount
and value of barter goods.

To solve these problems, they determined a valuable
common object to exchange goods with. This was mostly
metal. So, these metals can be called the first money
used. Perhaps metal was used because it was a rare
commodity at the time, could be preserved for a long time,
and its value did not change.

During his reign, Sher Shah
Suri (1540-1546) established a new administrative
system for the citizens and the military. The silver
coin weighing 178 grams introduced during his time
was called 'Rupiya'. That coin was in circulation until the
Mughal, Maratha, and English periods.

Gold, silver, and copper metals were used instead of the
barter system. These are also called primitive money.
Like this, beads, shells, tobacco, salt, cattle, and even slaves
were given as barter, according to economists.

3.4 Paper Money

Over time, problems also arose with natural money.
There was no stock of gold and silver available to meet
the expansion of trade. At the same time, gold and silver
were limited in mines. Therefore, coins were made with
low-value metals. These were used to buy and sell small
items. These were used as money for the common people.

Because of this, these low-value coins were produced
in large quantities. The next stage of this was the introduction
of paper money. This intangible money and depositing it
in banks came into widespread use. The economic recession
that occurred all over the world at that time was one of the
reasons for this.

Money has become an inseparable part of everyone's life.
In the global economic environment, money has changed
into many forms. In the electronic world, money
transactions take place in many ways.

3.5 Electronic Transactions

Instead of going directly to the bank and filling out a
form or providing a check to withdraw money deposited
in the bank, an automated teller machine card is used to
withdraw the required money through an ATM. Through
this, we can withdraw the money we need from our account
at any time through the ATM established everywhere.

Like this, some banks have the facility to deposit money
into our account without going to the bank.

• Similarly, credit cards are used where money is received
first and paid later.

• Instead of using checks or demand drafts for money
transfer, online transfers are currently in practice.
Through this, money can be transferred in an instant
to anyone in any part of the world at the desired time.

• Similarly, mobile-based electronic transfer technology is in
widespread use.

3.6 Role of the Reserve Bank

The responsibility of maintaining and monitoring money
transactions rests with a government. Generally, hoarding
money should be avoided in economics. Therefore, money
is deposited in banks. A large portion of the money deposited
in banks is used for industrial development, economic
growth, and the welfare of the poor.

Medium of Exchange

Money must be readily accepted in a country for all exchange of goods and services.

Unit of Account

Money must be a common, standardized unit for calculating the value of all consumer goods, products, and services in a country. For example, if the price of a book is `50, then the price of that book is equal to 50 monetary units. Money is used to measure financial transactions and to maintain accounts in a country.

Storing Value

3.9 Loan

We can see farmers taking out loans during the seasons for seeds, inputs, and expenses they need. Similarly, small business owners and traders take out loans for their needs. Even large industrial companies take out loans for their new projects.

Institutions where loans are available

• Formal financial institutions such as nationalized banks, private banks, and cooperative banks.

• Informal financial institutions

• Loans obtained through self-help groups Microloans are obtained in three ways.

As far as nationalized banks and cooperative banks are concerned, the interest on loans is low. The security for the items pledged is high.

Informal Financial Institutions

Easy access to informal private institutions by customers and convenience are considered positive aspects. However, there are many problems at the same time in terms of high interest rates, security of pledged items, and collection methods.

Groups of people living in a particular area or engaged in a particular occupation form and engage in small savings. These groups are called self-help groups. Government banks provide loan assistance to such self-help groups. Self-help groups benefit small traders, fishermen, especially women, and marginalized people.

There are 10,612 bank branches in Tamil Nadu. Approximately 15 lakh crore Rupees of money transactions take place in the 2017-2018 financial year.

How is money printed in India?

In India, one Rupee and two Rupee notes were first manufactured and issued in 1917.

In 1935, all monetary responsibility came to the Reserve Bank of India. After that, it introduced the 500 Rupee note. In 1940, it again released one Rupee notes. Until 1947, coins bearing the image of George VI were in circulation. After independence, Rupee notes were issued by the Indian government.

The English government established a printing press in Nashik, Maharashtra in 1925 and started printing Rupee notes after three years.

Another printing press was started in Dewas, Madhya Pradesh in 1974. In addition to these two printing presses (Security Printing and Minting Corporation of India Ltd.), the Reserve Bank of India started two more printing presses in Mysore, Karnataka and Salboni, West Bengal in the 1990s to print Rupees and related documents.

The Reserve Bank of India decides how much money needs to be printed and how to safely transport the money to the appropriate locations.

Although the Reserve Bank of India had the power to print money up to ten thousand Rupees, it currently prints money only up to a maximum value of two thousand Rupees.
