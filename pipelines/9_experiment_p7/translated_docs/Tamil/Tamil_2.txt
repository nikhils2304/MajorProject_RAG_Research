Introduction

When it became essential for humans to live in close proximity in numbers, they brought with them ancient hunting – gathering groups and new Stone Age agricultural societies more organized than settlement planning, systematization, uniqueness.

Planned settlements had to be created after this. A political structure was formed for this, society was organized, food and craft goods production were systematized. As civilizations began to take shape, they erected huge buildings. They created writing art. Science and technology contributed to social development, including technology information and organizational political systems (early state). The Egyptian, Mesopotamian, Indus Valley and Chinese civilizations are major civilizations. This expression 'civilization' is used, so they should not be considered superior to earlier societies. This is because every culture and civilization has its own unique aspects of life. Hunter-gatherers and pastoral societies also began to use food through interactions with these civilizations. Their food is just as important. In the same period of these civilizations, Neolithic agricultural pastoral societies and hunter-gatherer communities using microliths lived in South India.

2.1 Ancient Civilizations

Civilization is considered to be an advanced, well-organized way of life. It leads to a way of life worth considering as an adaptation to a particular environment and culture. As more people live together, urban societies developed planning, systematization, uniqueness, ancient hunting – gathering groups and new Stone Age agricultural societies were more organized than. Urban societies had social strata and well-planned cities.

2.2 Egyptian Civilization

Egypt is located in the northeast corner of the African continent. It is surrounded by the Red Sea to the east and the Mediterranean Sea to the north. Egypt is fertile by the Nile River.

The Egyptian civilization, one of the oldest civilizations, was outstanding in ancient times with its architectural, agricultural, artistic, scientific, and craft industry developments.

The Nile River, which originates in Lake Victoria and flows through Egypt, empties into the Mediterranean Sea. The Egyptian civilization depended on the fertility of the Nile River. Therefore, the Greek historian Herodotus referred to Egypt as the "Gift of the Nile." The Central Nile Valley was fertile because this river brought and deposited new alluvial soil every year. This alluvial soil helped agriculture flourish and helped with surplus production. It paved the way for the development of the Egyptian civilization. Deserts can also be seen on both sides of the Nile River.

Hyksos – Rulers belonging to the 15th Dynasty of Egypt. They may have come from West Asia.

Persians – People who came from Persia, part of ancient Iran.

Greek – Refers to the people and language in present-day Greece in Europe.

Romans – Belonged to the ancient Roman Empire. The capital of this empire is Rome in Italy.

Pharaohs, Society, and Administration

Egyptian kings were called Pharaoh. Pharaohs were considered to possess divine power. Under the rule of the Pharaoh were officials such as Viziers, Governors, local Mayors, and tax collectors. This entire system was supported by the labour of stone cutters, stone masons, potters, carpenters, copper tool makers, goldsmiths, farmers, and labourers, including craft artisans. The land belonged to the king. It was divided among the officials. Although there was no general slave system, prisoners were used as slaves.

Egyptians had trade relations with Lebanon, Greece, Phoenicia, Palestine, and Syria. Gold, silver, and ivory were imported. They obtained lapis lazuli, a type of blue stone, from Afghanistan.

Egyptians believed that there was life after death. Therefore, they embalmed the bodies of the dead. This method of embalming the body is called Mummification. Pharaohs built pyramids and tombs to protect their bodies. The tomb of the famous Egyptian Pharaoh Tutankhamun (who ruled from 1332 to 1322 BC (BCE)), with various valuable objects, is located in the Valley of the Kings near Luxor in Egypt. The mask of his mummy is made of gold and decorated with various precious stones. It is an important artifact of Egyptian civilization.

The Egyptian Embalmed Body (Mummy)

An embalmed dead body is called a Mummy. The Egyptians had a practice of preserving the bodies of the dead by keeping them in a type of salt called natron salt, a mixture of sodium carbonate and sodium bicarbonate. After forty days, when the salt absorbed all the moisture of the body, the body was filled with sawdust, wrapped in linen strips, and covered with cloth. The body will be protected in a stone coffin called a Sarcophagus.

Agriculture and Commerce

Egyptians cultivated wheat, barley, small grains, vegetables, fruits, papyrus, and cotton. Papyrus was used to make rope mats, sandals, and later paper. They domesticated and raised cattle, sheep and goats, and pigs. They hunted wild animals. They raised pet animals such as dogs, cats, and monkeys.

Art and Architecture

The Egyptians excelled in architecture and various art forms. Even their writing style was in the form of pictures. The countless sculptures, paintings, and creations found here are proof of the Egyptians' artistic prowess. Monumental memorials built as tombs of Pharaohs are the pyramids. The pyramids near Cairo are called the Pyramids of Giza.

The huge statue of Sphinx, a limestone statue with the body of a lion and a human face. Built during the reign of Pharaoh Khafre (2575-2465 BC (BCE)), this creation is seventy-three meters long and twenty meters high. The Sphinx is considered one of the tallest sculptures in the world.

Writing System

The Egyptians are famous for their writing system. Their writing system is called Hieroglyphic. This type of writing was used in memorials with seals and other items. Hieratic writing was used for general communication. This is a form of pictogram. It was created around 3000 BC (BCE). Numerous books and copies were written using this script. It is now on display at the British Museum in London.

Religion

The Egyptians adhered to the polytheistic doctrine. There were many gods in Egypt, including Amun, Ra, Seth, Thoth, Horus, and Anubis. Among them, the sun god Ra was the main god. Later this god was called Amun.

Philosophy, Science, Literature

The Egyptian civilization excelled in science, literature, philosophy, astronomy, mathematics, and measurement methods. Sun dials, water clocks, and glass were discovered by the Egyptians. They created a calendar based on the sun. It had 12 months with 30 days each. Five days had to be added at the end of the year. This method was created as early as 4200 BC (BCE). Literary creations include those written about mathematics, astronomy, medicine, magic, and religion. Egyptians also excelled in painting, sculpture, pottery, music, and weaving.

2.3 Mesopotamian Civilization

Mesopotamia refers to the regions of Iraq and Kuwait in West Asia. At the beginning of the third millennium BC (BCE), many kingdoms were formed around many cities in this region. Sumerian, Akkadian, Babylonian and Assyrian civilizations flourished in this region.

Geography

In Greek, "Mesos" means 'middle' and "Potamos" means 'river.' The rivers Euphrates and Tigris that flow here join in the Persian Gulf. Because it is between these two rivers, it is called Mesopotamia.

The northern part of Mesopotamia was called Assyria. The southern part is Babylonia.

Sumerians

The oldest civilization of Mesopotamia belongs to the Sumerians. The Sumerians were contemporaries of the Indus Valley and Egyptian civilizations. They all had trade relations with each other. During 5000-4000 BC (BCE), the Sumerians migrated to the lower Tigris Valley. They are believed to have come from Central Asia. They created many cities. Nippur was an important city. They created the Cuneiform writing system.

In the early stages of the Sumerian civilization, the kings were the chief priests. Their political domination came to an end around 2450 BC (BCE).

Akkadians

From 2450 BC (BCE) to 2250 BC (BCE) the Akkadians dominated Sumeria for a short period. Sargon was a famous Akkadian king. Sargon and his successors ruled for over 100 years (2334 to 2218 BC (BCE)). Akkadian cuneiform documents mention the Indus Valley Civilization. Sargon's cuneiform documents (2334–2279 BC (BCE)) state that ships from Meluha, Magan, and Dilmun stopped at Akkadian ports. Meluha is identified as the Indus Valley.

The city of Akkad was later called Babylon. It was the commercial and cultural hub of West Asia.

Babylonians

The Jewish people, called Amorites, migrated to Mesopotamia from the Arabian deserts. They made Babylon their capital. They are called Babylonians.

The Babylonian king Hammurabi expanded his dominance to the western part of Mesopotamia. Following this, powerful kingdoms of Ur (2112 to 2004 BC (BCE)) and Babylon (1792 to 1712 BC (BCE)) were formed. Gilgamesh, the hero of the world's first epic, may have been a Sumerian king. Hammurabi (1792 – 1750 BC (BCE)), the sixth ruler of Babylon, is famous for enacting great laws.

Assyrians

The Assyrian government in the 1000 BC (BCE) period was politically influential in Mesopotamia.

Ashur, the chief god of Assyria, was worshipped by the Assyrian kings. The Assyrian government was controlled by the emperor. There were provincial governors there. Ashurbanipal (668-627 BC (BCE)) was the most famous ruler of the Assyrian Empire. He created the famous library with Cuneiform documents. The Assyrians worshipped the protecting deity Lamassu.

Society, Government, and Administration

In the Sumerian civilization, there were many city-states. Their city was surrounded by agricultural lands. Sumerian cities, surrounded by walls, had a temple called Ziggurat in the centre. Temples were under the control of the priests. Priests, scribes, and nobles were members of the government. At the top of the social hierarchy were the rulers and priests. The king was also the chief priest. Scribes, merchants, and craft artisans were next in line. Scribes managed the tax collection accounts. Priests collected taxes. Temples served as storehouses for storing what was collected as tax. Sculptures were part of the Assyrian government administration. Lands suitable for cultivation belonged to the king and the upper class. Initially, the farmers who were slaves to the temples were later freed. All people were not allowed to live in the city.

Temples called Ziggurats were built on platforms in the centre of the city. They looked like steep pyramids. They were arranged with stairs to reach the top. The temple was surrounded by courtyards for ceremonies, holy places, tombs of male and female priests, ceremonial banquet halls, workshops, granaries, warehouses, and administrative buildings.

Assyria is considered the first military state in the world. The reason for them becoming a strong military power was that they used iron technology well.

Food and Agriculture

The main occupation of the Mesopotamians was agriculture. They improved irrigation methods to support agriculture. They cultivated wheat, barley, onions, turnips, grapes, apples, and dates. They raised cattle, sheep, and goats. Fish was their main food.

Trade and Exchange

Trade was an important economic activity of the Mesopotamian society. Traders facilitated the exchange of products from potters and craft artisans. They traded with Syria, Asia Minor in the west, Iran in the east, and the Indus Valley. They used ships. Their temples acted as banks and provided loans. Loans were given with or without interest and were paid back, as is known from Mesopotamian documents. This may be the world's first written evidence of charging interest on loans.

Cities and Urban Planning

The houses in the cities had walls made of clay or burnt bricks and had doors. Some lived in reed huts outside the city.

Religion

Sumerian religion was polytheistic. They worshipped various male and female gods. The Sumerians worshipped Enlil, the god of wind and sky. The temple of this god was in Nippur. Ninlil was the female deity for grains. Babylonians worshipped the god Marduk. The chief god of the Assyrians is Ashur. Ishtar is the female deity of love and fertility. Tiamat is the deity for sea and chaos. The moon god is Sin. Kings were considered representatives of the gods on earth. The Mesopotamians created numerous myths with dreams and stories. Among these, Gilgamesh is still famous as can be known through cuneiform scripts. Like the story of Noah's ark in the Bible and the stories of floods in Hindu mythology, this also has a story about floods.

Hammurabi's Code

Hammurabi's Code is an important legal document that contains laws for various offenses. It has 282 sections on family rights, trade, slavery, taxes, wages, and various specific cases. Hammurabi carved the code on a stone as if receiving it from Shamash, the sun god. This is a collection of old laws. It is based on the principle of retaliation. It is based on the principle of "an eye for an eye", "a tooth for a tooth".

Writing and Script Development

The creation of writing was a major milestone in human history. The writing system was formed in Sumer around 4000 BC (BCE). The Egyptian writing system called Hieroglyphic was formed at the beginning of the third millennium AD (BCE). During the same period, the Harappan people also followed a type of writing system. However, the Indus Valley scripts have not been read and understood till now. The Chinese civilization developed its own writing system from the beginning.

Sumerian Script (Cuneiform)

The Sumerian script is called Cuneiform. Because the letters are in the shape of wedges, it was named so. This method, which was created around 3000 BC (BCE), is one of the oldest writing methods in the world. The Sumerians used this writing system for commercial transactions, writing letters, and stories. These burnt clay tablets on which the letters were written provide a lot of information about the Sumerian civilization.

Art

Mesopotamian art includes sculptures made of stone and clay. Some paintings and colored sculptures can still be seen. They sculpted animals like sheep, goats, bulls, and lions. Mythological forms such as human-headed lions and bulls have also been carved. Huge statues were created during the times of the Assyrian and Babylonian empires.

Science

The Mesopotamians excelled in mathematics, astronomy, and medicine. They also created the concepts of multiplication, division, and cubic equations. They invented a number system based on 60. As a result, we got an hour with 60 minutes, a day with 24 hours, and a circle with 360 degrees. The Sumerian calendar had seven days a week. Their number system had place value for digits. They created a water clock and a calendar based on the moon.

A Clay Tablet with Sheep Accounts

They discovered methods for measuring area and solids. They used improved weight and measurement methods.

They created a lunar calendar with 12 months. Their ideas influenced Greek astronomy. They also developed a medical system. In the 11th century BC (BCE), they also created a manual for disease diagnosis. It lists the symptoms of diseases and their remedies. This exemplifies their scientific knowledge of herbs and minerals.

2.4 Chinese Civilization

The Yellow River, also called the Hwang-Ho, and the Yangtze River are the two major rivers of China. Because it often changes its course and causes floods, the Yellow River is called 'the Sorrow of China.'

In China, evidence of ancient Peking man (approximately 700,000 years ago - 200,000 years ago), Yuanmou man lived. From 4500 to 3750 BC (BCE), Neolithic communities lived in China. Evidence of Neolithic villages has been found in the Henan Province of the Yellow River Valley and the Yangtze River Valley. There were many city states in China. Over time, these states became parts of the empire.

Political Systems and Emperors

Shi Huangdi (meaning Qin Shi Huang - the First Emperor) founded the Qin (Chin) dynasty. This emperor had the title "Son of Heaven". He is considered the first emperor of China. This period (221 – 206 BC (BCE)) is called the imperial period of China. He conquered the nearby territories in 221 BC (BCE) and ruled as emperor until 212 BC (BCE). He defeated the feudal lords and created a powerful empire.

He has the pride of turning China into one kingdom. Shi Huangdi demolished the forts of many kingdoms and built the Great Wall of China to prevent invading nomads from outside. He set up roads to connect the empire.

Han Dynasty 206 AD (CE) – 220 AD (CE)

It was during this period that a written Chinese history of this empire was written. Among the Han emperors, Emperor Wu Ti (Han Wu, Great Han Wu 141–87 BC (BCE)) expanded the empire and created many public amenities including irrigation ponds. In 138 BC (BCE), he sent Zhang Qian as his ambassador to foreign countries. It paved the way for the opening of the Silk Road in 130 BC (BCE).

Terracotta Army

Hundreds of terracotta doll sculptures of military soldiers have been discovered in China. These are believed to represent the army of Qin Shi Huang, the first emperor of China. They are known to have been buried with the king in his tomb around 210-209 BC (BCE). They are located in the king's tomb in the foothills of Mount Lishan, 35 km northeast of Xi'an in Shaanxi Province.

Map - Chinese Civilization

China achieved numerous benefits during the time of Emperor Zhang [AD (CE) 75–88] due to the Silk Road and the resulting trade relations. In 166 AD (CE), Chinese silk was very popular among the Romans during the reign of Roman Emperor Marcus Aurelius. Chinese silk may have gone to Rome through some Tamil Nadu ports.

Philosophers and literature like Lao Tzu, Confucius, Mencius, Mo Ti (Mot Zu), Tao Chien (AD (CE) 365-427) and Chinese poets have contributed greatly to Chinese civilization. Military strategist Sun Tzu wrote the book 'The Art of War'. The Spring and Autumn Annals was recognized as the official Chinese government book. The Yellow Emperor's Canon of Medicine is considered to be the oldest medical book in China. It was systematized during the Han dynasty.

Lao Tzu (604 – 521 BC (BCE)) was the chief archivist of the Zhao government. He was the founder of Taoism. He argued that desire is the root cause of all suffering.

Confucius (551 – 479 BC (BCE)) was a famous Chinese philosopher. He was a political reformer. His name means "leader (Kung)". He emphasized reforming one's personal life. "If one's personal life is reformed, their family life will be systematized. If the family is systematized, the national life will be systematized," he said.

Mencius (372–289 BC (BCE)) is another famous Chinese philosopher. He traveled all over China and advised the rulers.

Chinese Script

From a very ancient period, the Chinese had developed a writing system. Initially, it was a pictorial writing system. Later it was formally changed to symbolic bones. The Chinese script found is the oldest form of writing.

2.5 Indus Valley Civilization

The Harappan civilization, also called the Indus Valley civilization, is called the Harappan civilization because Harappa was the first place to be discovered in India and Pakistan. As this civilization spread beyond the Indus River, instead of being called the Indus Valley Civilization, it is called the Harappan Civilization.

This civilization covered approximately 1.5 million square kilometers. This civilization has territories that border:

west: Sutkagendor on the Pakistan-Iran border;

north: Shortugai (Afghanistan);

east: Alamgirpur (Uttar Pradesh – India);

south: Daimabad (Maharashtra – India).

Many evidences for the Harappan civilization have been found in this area.

The major areas where this civilization prevailed were Gujarat, Pakistan, Rajasthan and Haryana.

Planned Cities

Harappa (Punjab, Pakistan), Mohenjo-Daro (Sindh, Pakistan), Dholavira (Gujarat, India), Kalibangan (Rajasthan, India), Lothal (Gujarat, India), Banawali (Rajasthan, India) Rakhigarhi (Haryana, India) Surkotada (Gujarat, India) are major cities of the Indus Valley Civilization.

Harappa cities had fort walls, well-planned streets, alleys, and sewers. The Harappans used burnt and unburnt bricks and stones for construction. An internal administrative organization may have controlled the planning of the cities. Some houses had floors. The Great Bath in Mohenjo-Daro is an important structure. Near this pool are many well-arranged rooms. Some excavated structures look like warehouses. Details about the Harappans' government system are not known to us. But there must have been an ancient government-like political structure. A statue found in Mohenjo-Daro is identified as the "Priest King". But it is not possible to confirm whether it is correct.

Agriculture and Cattle Breeding

The Harappan people were engaged in agriculture. They cultivated wheat, barley, and various types of millet. They also followed the double cropping system. They also knew about animal husbandry. They raised cattle, sheep, and goats. They knew about many animals including elephants. But they did not use horses. The Harappan cattle were called the Gajput. It is a species of large cattle. The figure of such a large bull is widely seen on the Indus Valley seals.

Pottery

The Harappan people used pottery with paintings. The potteries are painted with deep red color and black paintings. They made various pottery items like pedestal plates, jars for storing grain, perforated jars, goblets, 'S' shaped jars, plates, saucers, bowls, and pots. They painted pictures on the pottery. They painted various paintings like peepal leaves, fish scales, intersecting circles, horizontal lines, geometric shapes, checkerboard, and tendrils in black color.

Metal Tools and Weapons

Harappan cultural people used blades made of chert, a type of silica stone, knives, copper objects, and tools made of bone and ivory. They did not know the use of iron. Sharp tools, chisels, needles, fish hooks, knives, weighing scales, and antimony rods were made of copper. Arrows, spears, chisels, and axes made of copper were used.

Garments and Jewellery

Harappan cultural people knew about cotton and silk. They wore ornaments made of metal and stone. They made ornaments made of carnelian, copper, and gold. They made stone jewellery and conch bangles. Some of them had inlay work done. They exported these to Mesopotamia.

Seals, Scripts

Seals made of steatite, copper, terracotta, and ivory have been found in the Harappan cultural areas. These may have been used for commercial activities.

Seals with Writings

Harappan scripts have not been read so far. About 5000 small script collections have been found from Harappan sites. Some scholars firmly believe that these are Dravidian. It can be said that the Indus Valley Civilization is the foundation for the creation of Indian culture.

Trade and Exchange

The Harappan people had close trade relations with the Mesopotamians. Harappan seals have been found in West Asian regions such as Oman, Bahrain, Iraq, and Iran. Cuneiform documents reveal the trade relations between Mesopotamia and the Harappans. The reference to Meluha in Cuneiform writings refers to the Indus region.

Weights and Measures

The Harappan people used systematic weights and measures. Because they were engaged in commercial transactions, they needed systematized measures. Cubic chert weights have been found in Harappan civilization areas. Copper scales for scales have been found. The weights increased in a ratio of 1: 2: 4: 8: 16: 32.

Clay Dolls

The small terracotta sculptures, the paintings in the pottery, and the bronze sculptures found in the Harappan sites show the artistic skill of the Harappan people. The 'Priest or King' made of steatite, the dancing girl statue cast in copper (both found in Mohenjo-Daro), and the stone sculptures found in Harappa, Mohenjo-Daro, and Dholavira are major works of art in this region.

Doll carts, rattles, tops, marbles, shells, and terracotta chips for various games show the pastime games of the Harappan people.

Religion

The Indus Valley people worshipped nature. They worshipped the peepal tree. Some burnt clay sculptures seem to refer to a female deity. Fire pits have been identified in Kalibangan. It was customary to bury the dead. Evidence of cremation of the dead is rare.

Harappan People and Culture

Because the meaning of the writings of the Harappan people has not been discovered, the Harappan civilization is not fully known. One section of scholars says that they spoke the Dravidian language. Archaeological studies show that after the Harappan civilization, some may have migrated to various places in India. A definite answer to many questions can only be obtained if the meaning of their writings is discovered.

There were more than one group in the Indus Valley. Various groups including farmers, livestock breeders, hunter-gatherers – food collectors lived in the Indus Valley. The Indus Valley Civilization is divided as follows: the period from 3300 BC (BCE) to 2600 BC (BCE) is called the early Harappan. The period from 2600-1900 BC (BCE) is called the mature Harappan civilization. The later Harappan may have lasted until 1700 BC (BCE).

Decline of Indus Valley Civilization

The Indus Valley Civilization began to decline around 1900 BC (BCE). Climate change, decline in trade with Mesopotamia, drying up of the river or floods, and foreign invasions are considered to be some of the major reasons for this civilization to decline and people to migrate towards the north and east. The Indus Valley Civilization did not completely disappear after its decline. It continued in India as a rural culture towards the east and towards the north.
