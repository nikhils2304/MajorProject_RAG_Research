Lord Ripon

Documentary records about local bodies are also available during the Maurya Empire. Throughout ancient India, local bodies existed with different aspects. In the Middle Ages, due to the impact of the feudal system, the importance of local bodies decreased. According to Lord Ripon's resolution of 1882, these bodies were revived in India in the late quarter of the 19th century based on the democratic system of Western countries. Lord Ripon laid the foundation for modern local bodies and is called the 'Father of Local Bodies'.
By introducing local bodies in 1882, Lord Ripon introduced the taste of freedom to Indians.

Lord Ripon took some steps to liberalize the administration in India. He streamlined local bodies to decentralize the administration.

5.3 After Indian Independence
The Government of India Act of 1935 introduced autonomy in the provinces. This Act came into effect in 1937. Special attention was given to rural development in the provinces ruled by the Congress. The establishment of Panchayat Raj bodies from villages to cities was an important component of Gandhiji's plans. The Community Development Program (1952) and the National Extension Service (1953) served as the basis for the Panchayat Raj Act of 1957.
After Indian independence, the framers of the Indian Constitution took Gandhiji's philosophy of village self-government into consideration. The creation of village panchayats became a social movement in a country filled with villages. The reconstruction of the panchayat instilled confidence in the people during the independence struggle.

5.4 Rural Governance
The local bodies functioning in the villages are called Village Panchayats. The president and ward members are directly elected by the people. (Those who have completed 21 years of age). Their term is 5 years. The District Collector acts as the Inspector of the Village Panchayat. Every village with a population of more than five hundred has been transformed into a Village Panchayat.

Historical Continuity and Development in Local Bodies in Tamil Nadu
According to the sources found in the inscriptions of Uthiramerur in Kanchipuram District, Tamil Nadu is known to have a long history in local bodies. At that time, Tamil Nadu was a land with many village republics. Many social groups were involved in various activities for the development of their area. This system reached its peak during the Chola rule in the tenth and eleventh centuries. Village councils levied taxes; promoted community life; and maintained justice in their specific area. These village councils had strong ties with the Chola rulers. The secret election method called 'Kudavolai Method' was in use to elect the members of the village council.
After the decline of the Cholas, the village autonomy gradually declined, and the land ownership-based administrative system regained prominence. This method continued until the British rule introduced a variety of systems that were conducive to their rule over local bodies.

In independent India, as the first law of democratic decentralization, the Madras Village Panchayat Act was enacted in 1950. In continuation of the information released regarding local administrative reforms in 1957, the Madras Panchayat Act and the Madras District Development Council Act were enacted in 1958.

Gandhi's Village Swaraj
Gandhiji wanted a village swaraj with people as representatives of the rural areas. Gandhiji realized that the soul of India lives in the villages. In independent India, Gandhiji dreamed of panchayats as village republics.
Gandhiji advised that Panchayat Raj should be a government with power sharing. The villages, which form the basis of the Indian Constitution, will be responsible for their own needs. To put it simply, Gandhi's swarajya village should be self-reliant at the grassroots level. The government should accept all the needs for life, such as food, clean water, sanitation, housing, education and other needs, and provide everything including self-defense.

Problems and Challenges Faced by Local Bodies
Local bodies are the foundations of our democracy. The constitutional status of local bodies is very important for their functioning. However, there are some difficulties in their functioning in India. Their main problems and challenges are given below.
• There is no clear definition about the functions and powers of local bodies.
• There is no agreement between the allocation of funds and the assessment of needs.
• Caste, class and religion play an important role in important decisions taken by local bodies.
• Irresponsibility of officers and elected members at the basic level of democracy.
