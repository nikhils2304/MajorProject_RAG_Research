Information Box

The magnetosphere is the layer located beyond the exosphere. It is the Earth's magnetic field. This zone retains protons and electrons emitted from the sun. The magnetic field extends about 64,000 km from the Earth's surface.

Exosphere

The uppermost layer of the atmospheric layers is called the exosphere. Here the gases are very scarce. The upper part of this layer gradually mixes with outer space. Strange light phenomena called Aurora Australis and Aurora Borealis occur in this layer.

Due to the magnetic storm formed on the surface of the sun, at the time of the northern lights a multicolored light scattering scene occurs in the night sky in the polar regions by electrons expelled. This is called 'Aurora'.

Stratosphere

Above the troposphere is the stratosphere. It extends up to 50 km in the atmosphere. Because it contains more ozone molecules, it is called the 'ozonosphere'. Here, the temperature increases with increasing height. This layer is suitable for jet planes to fly. The upper limit of this layer is called 'stratopause'.

Mesosphere

The mesosphere is found in the atmosphere from 50 km to 80 km altitude. Here the temperature decreases with increasing height. Meteorites falling towards the Earth are burned upon entering this layer. The upper limit of the mesosphere is called 'mesopause'.

Thermosphere

The layer above the mesosphere is the thermosphere. It extends up to about 400 km. The lower part of the thermosphere is homogeneous in the amount of gases, so it is called 'homosphere'. However, the upper part of the thermosphere is heterogeneous in the amount of gases, so that area is called 'heterosphere'. In this layer, the temperature increases with increasing height. The 'ionosphere' is located in the thermosphere. Here ions and electrons are found. Radio waves emitted from the Earth are sent back to the Earth from this layer.

In areas located far from the equator, and in polar regions, the temperature is low as the sun's rays fall obliquely. This difference in temperature is due to the spherical shape of the earth.

Distance from the Sea

The climate of a place depends on how far it is from the sea. Due to the influence of the wind blowing from the sea, the coastal areas have an even climate. Conversely, land areas located far from the sea have a continental climate because there is no influence of the sea wind.

Information Box

During the day, the land heats up faster than the sea and the air rises upwards. As a result, low pressure is created in the areas adjacent to the sea. So the air blows from the sea towards the land. This is called 'sea breeze'. These sea breezes cause the temperature in the land areas to decrease during summer.

At night the land cools down faster than the sea. This cold air descends and creates high pressure. As a result, the air blows from the land towards the sea. This is called 'land breeze'.

Nature of the Wind Blowing

The climate of a place is determined by the place from which the wind originates and blows. Winds blowing from a warm place make a place warm, and winds blowing from a cold place make a place cold. Winds blowing from the sea towards the land cause rainfall. But the winds blowing from the land towards the sea create a dry weather.

Mountain Barriers

The climate of a place is determined by the location of the mountains. Mountain ranges are a natural factor that blocks the wind. Mountains block very cold air and protect from cold. It also blocks the monsoon winds and provides rainfall.

The mountain range on the opposite side of the wind is called the "windward side". Here heavy rainfall is received. The area hidden from the direction of the wind is called the "leeward side". Very little rainfall is received here.

Clouds largely reflect solar radiation in the atmosphere. It blocks the heat falling on the earth. Therefore, the amount of heat is high in the deserts without clouds and the amount of heat is low in the places where clouds are found.

Ocean Currents

Warm currents keep the land areas located near the seacoast warm, and cold currents keep the land areas located near the seacoast cold.

Natural Vegetation

Due to the evaporation occurring in plants, the atmospheric air is cooled. So the temperature is low in areas full of dense forests and high in areas without forests.

3.4 Wind

Horizontal movement of air on the Earth's surface is called ‘wind’. The vertical movement of air in the atmosphere is called air current. Wind always blows from high pressure area to low pressure area. The direction from which the wind blows is also the name of the wind. For example, the wind blowing from the east is called ‘east wind’.

Anemometer is used to measure the speed of the wind and wind vane is used to know the direction of the wind. The unit used to measure wind is kilometers/hour or knots.

Types of Wind

Wind is divided into four major categories.
● Planetary Winds
● Periodic Winds
● Variable Winds
● Local Winds
Planetary Winds

Winds blowing steadily in one direction throughout the year are called planetary winds. They are also called 'prevailing winds'. 'Trade Winds', 'westerlies' and 'Polar Easterlies' are 'planetary winds'.

Trade Winds

These winds blowing from the sub-tropical high pressure zone to the equatorial low pressure zone of the northern and southern hemispheres are called east winds because they blow from the northeast in the northern hemisphere and from the southeast in the southern hemisphere. These winds blow continuously and steadily throughout the year in one direction. Since it helped the sea journey of traders, it is also called 'trade wind'.

Westerlies

The westerlies are steady winds that blow from the tropical high pressure zone to the subpolar low pressure zone of the northern and southern hemispheres. They blow from the southwest to the northeast in the northern hemisphere, and from the northwest to the southeast in the southern hemisphere. The west winds are very fast, so in the southern hemisphere these winds are called "roaring forties" at 40º latitudes, "furious fifties" at 50º latitudes, and "screaming sixties" at 60º latitudes.

Polar Esterlies

Cold, dry winds blowing from the polar high pressure zone towards the subpolar low pressure zone are called polar easterlies. They blow from the northeast in the northern hemisphere, and from the southeast in the southern hemisphere. These winds are weak winds.

Periodic Winds

These winds have the characteristic of changing their direction according to the season. The reason for changing the direction like this is that the land and sea heat up at different rates. As the winds change their direction according to the season, they are called monsoon. The winds blowing in the Indian subcontinent are a good example of this.

Variable Winds

Cyclone

The word cyclone is a Greek word. It means coiled snake. The wind accumulating in a spiral shape from high pressure areas to low pressure areas is called cyclone. Due to the Earth's rotation, the cyclone blows counterclockwise in the northern hemisphere and clockwise in the southern hemisphere.

Types of Cyclones:
∙ Tropical Cyclones
∙ Mid-latitude Cyclones
Tropical Cyclones

Tropical cyclones are known by different names. They are called cyclones in the Indian Ocean region, typhoons in the western Pacific Ocean, hurricanes in the eastern Pacific Ocean and the Atlantic Ocean regions, baguios in the Philippines regions, typhoon in Japan, and wily wily in Australia. Tropical cyclones cause heavy loss of life and property in coastal areas and these cyclones weaken once they reach land.

Anticyclone and Cyclone

It is customary to name the cyclones formed in the Indian Ocean. Bangladesh, India, Maldives, Myanmar, Oman, Pakistan, Sri Lanka and Thailand participated in the consultation meeting in 2000. Then in 2004, each country gave a list of names for the cyclone. Based on this, we are using the names in the list in order whenever a cyclone is formed.

Anticyclones

The inverse structure of the low-pressure cyclone is the anticyclone. Here, the high-pressure zone is at the center, and the low pressures surround it. The wind flows from the high-pressure zone to the low-pressure zone in a spiral shape. Anticyclones are mostly found with heat waves and cold waves.

Local Winds

The wind blowing for a short period of time only in a particular place is called local wind. The effect of local wind is only found in specific areas. These are winds blowing only during a particular season. It is called by different names in every place in the world.

● Foehn (Alps – Europe)
● Sirocco (North coast of Africa)
● Chinook (Rocky Mountain Region – North America)
● Loo (Thar Desert – India)
● Mistral (Mediterranean region – France)
● Bora (Mediterranean region – Italy)

Based on height, clouds can be divided into three

∙ Mid Clouds (2 - 6 km)
∙ Low Clouds (less than 2 km)
Clouds are further divided according to their shape and structure.

Middle Clouds

Alto Stratus: Clouds that appear as a smooth, thin sheet in gray or blue are called alto stratus clouds. They consist of frozen water particles.

Alto Cumulus: Separate clouds of alto cumulus are connected to each other and appear in various shapes. Because it appears like wave clouds or comb clouds, it is called ‘Sheep Clouds’ or ‘Wool Pack Clouds’.

Nimbo Stratus: Black clouds that appear in areas near the Earth's surface are nimbo stratus clouds. These are related to rain, snow and hail.

Low Clouds

Strato Cumulus: Arranged as circular patches in gray and white. Low clouds are called 'strato cumulus clouds'. Generally, the weather is clear in that area when these clouds appear.

Stratus: Very dense clouds that appear like fog at the bottom are called stratus clouds. These give rain or snowfall.

Cumulus: It has a flat base and a dome-like upper appearance and appears in a "cauliflower" shape. It is a cloud related to clear weather.

Cumulo - Nimbus: Very dense, heavy in appearance, thunderous clouds that give rain are called cumulo - nimbus clouds. These usually bring heavy rain and heavy snowfall and sometimes hailstorms and rainstorms.

3.6 Precipitation

The event of condensed water vapor reaching the Earth in various forms of water is called precipitation. When the water droplets in the cloud reach saturation and then fall on the Earth as rain.

Factors of Precipitation
● Temperature
● Altitude
● Type of Cloud
● Atmospheric Conditions
● Precipitation Process

Rain, Sleet, Snow, Hail are various forms of precipitation.

Drizzle

When water droplets with a diameter of less than 0.5 mm steadily reach the Earth, we call it drizzle. Sometimes drizzles combine with fog and make it impossible to see the things in front.

Rainfall

The most important type of precipitation is rainfall. Moisture-laden air rises upward and forms clouds and then reaches the Earth as water droplets.

Rain falls when the temperature is above freezing. Rainfall is received in most of the Earth's places. Rainfall will occur only if there is very high humidity in the air. The diameter of a raindrop is more than 5 mm.

There are various types of rainfall:
1 Convectional Rainfall
2 Cyclonic Rainfall (or) Frontal Rain fall
3 Orographic Rainfall

1. Convectional Rainfall
During the day, the upper part of the Earth is heated more by solar radiation. The air on the Earth's surface expands and rises as it warms.

2. Cyclonic Rainfall or Frontal Rainfall

Cyclonic Rainfall
The rain that falls when dense air masses are unified and then go upwards and are cooled by a stable temperature is called cyclonic rainfall.
Rainstorm occurs in the tropical and temperate regions.

Frontal Rainfall
At the point where hot and cold air meet, the water vapor condenses and gives rain which is called the frontal rainfall in temperate regions.

3. Orographic Rainfall

Orographic rain is also known as terrain rain. The moisture-laden air is blocked by the mountain slope and rises up. The air that rises this way is then cooled and condensed and gives rain. The rainfall thus received is called Orographic Rainfall.

The mountain slope facing the direction of the wind is called the ‘Windward’ side. This area receives heavy rainfall. The mountain slope on the opposite side of the wind is called ‘Leeward side’. This area receives very little rain. It is also called ‘rain shadow’ region.

Mawsynram is the place that receives the highest rainfall in India. It is located on the windward side of the Purvachal mountains. But ‘Shillong’ on the leeward side of this mountain receives very little rainfall. Mumbai and Pune are located similarly.

3.7 Humidity

Humidity is an important factor affecting weather and climate in the atmosphere. The amount of water vapor in the atmosphere is called “humidity”. When the amount of water vapor in the atmosphere increases, the amount of humidity also increases. The amount of total water vapor in the atmosphere is called ‘Absolute humidity’. The ratio between the amount of moisture in the atmosphere and its total capacity is called ‘Relative humidity’.

When the relative humidity of air is hundred percent, the air reaches saturation. In this state, the air does not absorb water vapor. This saturation is called “Dew point”. Hygrometer or Wet and dry bulb is used to measure humidity.

Absolute humidity is how many grams of water vapor there are in every cubic meter of air. Relative humidity is calculated in percentage.
