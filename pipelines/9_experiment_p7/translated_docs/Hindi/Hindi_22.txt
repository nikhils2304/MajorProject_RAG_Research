A Strategic Rebalancing of India-Bangladesh Relations

This article is based on the article titled "Undermining India, Bangladesh relations" published in the Hindustan Times on 04/01/2026. This article highlights the recent tensions in India-Bangladesh relations, clarifying that cooperation in the areas of water, trade, connectivity, security, and people-to-people relations can strengthen regional stability and shared development. The historical, cultural, and strategic ties between India and Bangladesh, stemming from the Liberation War of 1971, are strong through continuous cooperation in trade, connectivity, and security. In recent years, Bangladesh has emerged as one of India's largest trading partners in South Asia, indicating increasing economic engagement. However, recent incidents of targeted violence against minorities in Bangladesh and law and order challenges have attracted attention at the public and diplomatic levels, affecting people-to-people contact and mutual perceptions. Some symbolic differences have also emerged in sports diplomacy, such as the BCCI's direction in January 2026 to an IPL team to release a Bangladeshi fast bowler and Dhaka's request to the ICC to relocate Bangladesh's T20 World Cup matches from India.

How have India-Bangladesh relations changed over time?

§ Establishment and the 'Mujib-Indira' Era (1971-1975): This relationship began with India's decisive military and diplomatic support during the Liberation War of 1971, which strengthened a bond based on shared secular values.

o The Treaty of Friendship of 1972 and the Land Boundary Agreement (LBA) of 1974 were signed during this period, establishing an initial legal framework for the new state.

o In addition, India sheltered approximately 10 million refugees, demonstrating initial people-centric cooperation and cultural ties with India.

§ Strategic Diversion and Military Rule (1975-1996): After the assassination of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman in 1975, military-led governments shifted Bangladesh's foreign policy towards closer relations with China/Pakistan to balance India's influence.

o Anti-India stances, disputes over the Farakka Barrage, and activities of northeastern insurgent groups from Bangladeshi soil remained major concerns during this era.

§ Democratic Transition and the "Golden Age" (1996-2024): Relations were redefined as the "Golden Chapter" during the Awami League government in 1996 and then continuously from 2009–2024. Key achievements include the Land Boundary Agreement of 2015 (exchange of 162 enclaves) and the Maritime Boundary Agreement of 2014, which resolved decades-old territorial disputes.

§ New Transition and Rebalancing of 2026 (2024–Present): The removal of Sheikh Hasina from power in August 2024 and the rise of the interim government led by Muhammad Yunus have initiated a phase of "pragmatic rebalancing."

o This has led to a significant increase in communal instability. India has consistently expressed concern over the "repeated occurrence" of targeted violence against Hindus and views their safety as an uncompromisable criterion.

o This tension has made minority rights a central diplomatic issue, with New Delhi demanding swift legal action, while Dhaka often attributes it to "political retribution" or "propaganda" rather than religious persecution.

What are the major areas of convergence between India and Bangladesh?

§ Economic and Trade Consolidation: Bilateral trade and development finance remain a central pillar. India-Bangladesh trade has been extremely robust and prioritized by both countries.

o In fiscal year 2023–24, bilateral trade was US$14.01 billion, with India's exports at US$12.05 billion and Bangladesh's at US$1.97 billion.

• In addition, India has provided several development credit lines worth more than US$7–8 billion to Bangladesh, which are helping in the construction of roads, railways, ports, and other infrastructure and demonstrate the direction of India's development partnership.

§ Connectivity and Transportation: Several projects to strengthen India-Bangladesh physical connectivity have reached the construction and operational stages.

o Key projects include the Agartala–Akhaura Rail Link, the use of Chittagong and Mongla ports for Indian cargo, the restoration of old rail routes such as Haldibari-Chilahati and Petrapole–Benapole, expansion of inland waterway trade under the Inland Water Transit and Trade Protocol, and the Maitri Setu over the Feni River (which connects Sabroom in the Indian state of Tripura to Ramgarh in Bangladesh).

§ Energy and Resource Cooperation: Energy cooperation has emerged as the most successful pillar of India–Bangladesh relations. This includes the direct supply of diesel and petroleum products through the India–Bangladesh Friendship Pipeline, which reduces transportation costs and ensures fuel security for northern Bangladesh.

o Bangladesh has committed to importing 180,000 tonnes of diesel from India's government-owned Numaligarh Refinery Limited (NRL).

o Cross-border electricity trade has continued to expand, with India exporting electricity through several grid interconnections and facilitating sub-regional electricity flow, including the recent trilateral arrangement under which Nepal supplies hydroelectricity to Bangladesh through the Indian transmission network.

• This long-term cooperation is also reflected in projects such as the Maitree Super Thermal Power Plant, which has been developed with Indian assistance and has also strengthened grid connectivity between Eastern India and Bangladesh.

§ People-to-People Contact, Culture, and Education: Initiatives such as cultural diplomacy and people-to-people contact deepen India-Bangladesh relations.

o Programs such as the Indian Council for Cultural Relations (ICCR) scholarships for Bangladeshi students maintain day-to-day goodwill.

o Exchange programs between Visva-Bharati University and Bangladeshi institutions and cross-border travel under liberalized visa arrangements (when applicable) strengthen the shared linguistic and cultural heritage.

• In addition, the Indira Gandhi Cultural Center located in Dhaka serves as an important bridge for people-to-people relations by promoting Indian art, culture, language, and shared heritage through exhibitions, festivals, and educational programs.

o Medical tourism has become a major driver of people-to-people contact between India and Bangladesh, with thousands of Bangladeshi patients visiting Indian cities every year in search of accessible and high-quality treatment.

§ Security and Defense Cooperation: Security cooperation between India and Bangladesh is based on trust and shared interests rather than formal alliances.

o Bangladesh's historical action against anti-India insurgent groups has significantly improved security and stability in northeastern India, helping the development of states such as Tripura, Assam, and Mizoram.

o Coordinated border management between the Border Security Force and Border Guard Bangladesh has reduced smuggling, human trafficking, and border tensions.

o Defense cooperation has expanded through credit lines, training, and equipment assistance, while joint exercises such as Sampriti strengthen interoperability, disaster response capabilities, and mutual trust.

§ Political and Diplomatic Relations: India and Bangladesh continue to engage in active high-level diplomacy focused on partnership rather than occasional conflict.

o The unveiling of the "Maitri Satellite" cooperation and a shared vision for green/digital/blue economy partnerships demonstrate that diplomacy is being used to promote regional cooperation (space, climate, digital).

• However, recent political and strategic differences have put pressure on this cooperation, emphasizing the need for timely rebalancing to maintain trust and momentum in the partnership.

What are the areas of conflict in India-Bangladesh relations?

§ River Water Sharing Dispute: Water sharing remains the most persistent structural problem in India-Bangladesh relations.

o Despite functional cooperation on more than 50 shared rivers under the Joint River Commission, the Teesta River water-sharing agreement has not been finalized so far.

• This delay deepens perceptions of unequal benefit-sharing in Bangladesh, especially during the dry season when northern districts face severe irrigation crises, crop losses, and livelihood insecurity.

o The expiry of the Ganga Water Sharing Treaty 1996 in December 2026 has made water resource management a sensitive and time-bound challenge in an environment of low diplomatic trust.

§ Political Sensitivities: Bilateral relations are occasionally influenced by domestic political narratives, especially during election cycles in both countries.

o In Bangladesh, opposition forces sometimes portray India as a dominant or unequal partner and use issues of sovereignty and water-sharing to shape public opinion.

o In India, especially in border states, electoral politics can harden stances on issues such as migration, border management, and river-water sharing, limiting diplomatic accommodation at the central level.

o In addition, the presence of former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina in India has become a serious diplomatic problem, as the interim government in Dhaka views her presence as an obstacle to domestic justice.

§ Border Management Challenges: Although cooperation has improved significantly, the more than 4,000 km long India-Bangladesh border remains complex and porous and continues to face challenges such as smuggling, human trafficking, cattle trade disputes, and occasional use of force.

o These issues arise from socio-economic inequalities, dense populations, riverine areas and insecure segments, and informal border economies.

o Domestic debates on the NRC and CAA in India have raised fears of displacement of immigrants or irregular migrants. Recent Bangladeshi riot events have prompted India to reassess border vigilance and refugee response.

§ "China-Pakistan" Tilt and Strategic Encirclement: India is facing a "strategic vacuum" as the interim government is reorganizing its foreign policy towards a "multi-aligned" approach that clearly favors Beijing and Islamabad.

§ New Delhi views the possibility of Chinese-led infrastructure projects near the Siliguri Corridor (Chicken's Neck) and the renewed defense talks with Pakistan as serious threats to its regional dominance.

o Recently, Bangladesh made a new investment of $2.1 billion from China, and there have been reports of negotiations to purchase JF-17 Thunder fighter jets from Pakistan, signaling a significant shift away from India's defense dependency.

§ Trade Imbalance and Non-Tariff Barriers: Despite the increase in bilateral trade, Bangladesh is facing a trade deficit with India, raising concerns about equitable market access.

o Bangladeshi exporters often point to non-tariff barriers such as strict BIS standards on food and plastic products, delays caused by limited testing and certification facilities at land ports.

o In April 2025, Bangladesh banned the export of Indian yarn and rice through land ports, in response to which India banned ready-made garments (RMG) from Bangladesh through its ports.

§ Delays in Project Implementation: Although the Line of Credit (LoC) and infrastructure assistance provided by India to Bangladesh are extensive, many projects have been delayed due to land acquisition challenges, inter-agency coordination gaps, as well as procedural delays on both sides. For example, the Khulna-Mongla Port Railway line has faced delays in the tendering process, land acquisition, and approval proceedings.

§ Decline in Soft Power: The recent humanitarian crisis in Bangladesh, including the increasing incidence of communal violence, with at least 51 cases registered in December 2025, has contributed to the decline in soft power.

o Growing political distrust, sporadic violence, and negative domestic perceptions in Bangladesh have weakened people-to-people goodwill, weakening the cultural, educational, and historical ties that once formed the basis of bilateral relations.

o This has also affected cricket diplomacy. Citing 'external developments,' the BCCI directed an IPL team to release a Bangladeshi fast bowler, after which the Bangladesh Cricket Board formally requested the ICC to relocate Bangladesh's T20 World Cup matches from India to Sri Lanka, citing security concerns.

What measures are needed to strengthen India-Bangladesh relations?

§ Balanced Diplomatic Rebalancing: India should pursue a balanced diplomatic rebalancing, giving strong but prudent signals to Bangladesh to address humanitarian and human rights concerns, especially communal violence, while leveraging dialogue, development cooperation, and people-to-people contact and exerting idealistic influence without appearing interventionist.

o India should explicitly and consistently counter misinformation and anti-India propaganda in Bangladesh through active diplomatic communication, strategic media engagement, while preventing tensions from escalating and reinforcing its image as a credible and respectful partner.

§ Resolving River Water Sharing Through Cooperative Federalism: Water cooperation should move beyond sporadic negotiations to institutional basin-level management. Early resolution of the Teesta water-sharing agreement by building common consensus with Indian states, especially West Bengal, will greatly strengthen trust.

o Joint hydrological data sharing, real-time flood forecasting, and river rejuvenation projects can transform water from a source of dispute to a source of cooperation.

§ Deepening Economic Integration Beyond Trade Volume: Bilateral economic engagement should evolve from trade expansion to value-chain integration.

o Reducing non-tariff barriers, harmonizing standards, and upgrading land ports to integrated check posts (ICP) will facilitate cross-border commerce.

o Promoting joint ventures in pharmaceuticals and agro-processing sectors can help Bangladesh move up the value chain, while also benefiting Indian manufacturers.

§ Accelerating Connectivity and Project Implementation: Timely implementation and outcome monitoring should be prioritized in connectivity projects. Strengthening coordination between implementation agencies in Bangladesh, resolving disputes quickly, and providing capacity support for project management will enhance credibility.

o Inland waterways, coastal shipping, and multimodal logistics should be expanded to integrate India's Northeast with Bangladesh and beyond.

§ Institutionalizing Security and Defense Cooperation: Security cooperation should be further strengthened through intelligence sharing protocols, coordinated border patrols, and capacity building of law enforcement agencies.

o Joint exercises should be extended to maritime security, cyber threats, and disaster response. A robust disaster risk management (HADR) framework will address common climate and disaster vulnerabilities.

§ Managing Migration and Border Issues Humanely: Balancing security and humanitarian concerns in border management is essential. Technology-based surveillance, non-lethal border management practices, and improved coordination between BSF and BGB can reduce civilian casualties.

o Regular dialogue with border communities can reduce tensions and increase trust.

§ Expanding Energy and Green Cooperation: The future of India-Bangladesh relations is committed to increasing mutual energy dependence. Expanding cross-border electricity trade, regional energy partnerships, and reliable gas supply connections can enhance the energy security of both sides.

o Joint cooperation in renewable energy, including emerging areas such as solar parks, grid-level storage, and green hydrogen and offshore wind energy in the Bay of Bengal, can establish bilateral development in line with climate commitments. Such partnerships will not only reduce carbon emissions but also establish both countries as major contributors in the regional clean energy transition in South Asia.

§ Strengthening People-to-People Contact and Cultural Exchange: A simpler and more predictable visa system is needed to deepen people-to-people relations, especially for students, patients, and business travelers.

o Medical tourism and cooperation in healthcare services provide direct benefits to citizens, as well as promoting goodwill and service sector development. In cooperation with skill development and youth functional exchanges, these initiatives create a sustainable social base that takes bilateral relations beyond politics.

Conclusion:

Strengthening India-Bangladesh relations requires transforming historical goodwill into institutionalized, people-centered cooperation. Resolving issues through dialogue, cooperative federalism, and timely project implementation will increase trust and credibility. Increasing cooperation in the areas of connectivity, energy, security, and climate change can lead to shared prosperity and regional stability. Ultimately, a mature partnership based on mutual sensitivity, strategic autonomy, and social relations will determine the future direction of bilateral relations.
